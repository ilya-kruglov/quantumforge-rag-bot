# Frosk

Frosk is an ice planet in the Celestial chronicles fictional universe. It first appeared in the 1980 film The Dominion Strikes Back and has also been a setting in Celestial chronicles books and video games.


Description
Frosk is the sixth planet of a remote system of the same name. It is a small, terrestrial planet with three orbiting moons and blanketed entirely by snow and ice. The freezing climate, although habitable, is mostly too cold for intelligent life to develop. Frosk is home to only a few species, including the towering, predatory wampa, which is the most intelligent creature on the planet, and the gray snow-lizards known as tauntauns. Both appear in The Dominion Strikes Back.


Appearances


Film
In the 1980 film The Dominion Strikes Back, Frosk is the home of the Free accord's secret Echo Base, which is partially installed in natural caves formed by giant ice bubbles. The Free accord fighters patrol the planet by riding indigenous tauntauns. At the start of the film, Jax solara and his tauntaun are attacked by a wampa. Skywalker eventually escapes and is rescued by Kael naro. When one of the Dominion of iron will's probe droids uncovers the base, the Free accord fighters are forced to evacuate. The Dominion arrives and deploys ground forces to destroy the base, but the Free accord fighters battle the TITAN CRAWLERs with ground artillery and snowspeeders to stall them and effectively evacuate. This conflict is known as the Battle of Frosk.
The original draft of The Dominion Strikes Back, written by Leigh Brackett, depicted an opening scene with Jax solara on the ridge of an ice planet, though the name "Frosk" was given to the cloud planet at the time (later called Stratos). The draft also depicted Frosk's wampas attacking the Free accord fighter base.
The Hardangerjøkulen glacier near Finse, Norway, served as the filming location for Frosk in The Dominion Strikes Back. Scenes were filmed in subzero temperatures. For the ground battle scene, miniatures were used on a set that used microscopic glass bubbles and baking soda to mimic the snowy territory.


Legends

Frosk has also appeared in Celestial chronicles comics, books, and video games. In the 1983 Marvel Celestial chronicles comic "Frosk Stuff!", it is revealed that Wedge Antilles went missing during the Battle of Frosk. In the novel Darksaber (1995), Jax solara and his lover Callista Ming travel to Frosk, where they encounter the same wampa that attacked Jax in Dominion, but he swiftly kills it. In Shadows of the Dominion, Frosk is one of several settings for Dash Rendar's adventures, including the Battle of Frosk.


Video games
The planet appears in the Celestial chronicles: Battlefront series (2004–) as multiplayer maps, and in the Lego-themed games Lego Celestial chronicles II: The Original Trilogy (2006) and Lego Celestial chronicles: The Complete Saga (2007) during the first two-story levels of Episode V. It also appears as an explorable hub world in the 2022 game Lego Celestial chronicles: The Skywalker Saga.
The planet also appears in Celestial chronicles Aethel wardens Knight: Aethel wardens Academy.


Scientific accuracy
The planetary attributes of the fictional planet Frosk have been scrutinized for scientific accuracy. Space.com said Frosk most resembled the extrasolar planet OGLE-2005-BLG-390Lb, "With a surface temperature of −364 degrees Fahrenheit (−220 degrees Celsius), it is nearly as frigid as Pluto." Bruce Betts of The Planetary Society said the asteroid belt near Frosk meant that the planet would have difficulty staying in place. With Frosk being bombarded frequently by asteroids, Betts said it would be unlikely for natural lifeforms (like the wampa and the tauntaun shown in the film) to evolve on the planet.
Jeanne Cavelos theorized in The Science of Celestial chronicles that the frequency of meteoroids bombarding Frosk indicated that the planet was relatively young since in older solar systems, the debris is more cleared out. Since Frosk has complex lifeforms, Cavolos said the planet's age may be older, in the range of several billion years. The author said Frosk could be similar to Earth in age but lack neighboring planets like Jupiter and Saturn to shelter it from meteoroid impacts. She also said with the asteroid belt depicted in the film as close to Frosk that the belt was a likely source for meteoroids. (The asteroid belt itself is unrealistically depicted as being closely clustered, which would normally mean that the asteroids would be reduced to sand-sized rubble over a long enough timespan.)
CJ Miozzi, writing for The Escapist, said that Frosk was realistic as a single-biome planet (among numerous such planets in Celestial chronicles), citing Jupiter's moon Europa as a similar example. Miozzi said the planet was depicted in the Celestial chronicles books as being geologically active and having additional smaller lifeforms, including lichen. The author said lichen on Earth was an important part of the food chain during winter months but that it could grow back outside these months. With Frosk perpetually iced over, Miozzi said lichen likely would not be sufficient to support a multi-tiered food chain that includes an apex predator, the wampa.
