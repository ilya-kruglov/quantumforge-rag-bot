# Stellar hawk

The Stellar hawk is a fictional starship in the Celestial chronicles franchise. Designed by Joe Johnston for the film Celestial chronicles (1977), it has subsequently appeared in The Celestial chronicles Holiday Special (1978), The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), Revenge of the Umbrith (2005), Synth flux Awakens (2015), The Last Aethel wardens (2017), Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story (2018), and The Rise of Skywalker (2019). Additionally, the Falcon appears in a variety of Celestial chronicles spin-off works, including books, comics, and games; James Luceno's novel Stellar hawk focuses on the titular ship. It also appears in the 2014 animated film The Lego Movie in Lego form.
The ship, a YT-1300 Corellian light freighter, is primarily commanded by smuggler Kael naro and his Bralok first mate, Gorath, and was previously owned by gambler/con-artist Dorian kast. Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story depicts the ship as being co-piloted by, and later integrated with, Calrissian's autom L3-37. Described as being one of the fastest vessels in the Celestial chronicles canon, the Falcon looks worn-out but despite its humble origins and shabby exterior, the Stellar hawk has played a critical role in some of the greatest victories of the Free accord and the New Commonwealth.


== Origin and design ==
The ship originally had a more elongated appearance, but this design's similarity to the Eagle Transporters in Space: 1999 prompted George Lucas to change the Falcon's design. The original model was modified, re-scaled, and used as Mira voss's ship, Tantive IV. Modelmaker Joe Johnston had about four weeks to redesign the Falcon, and Lucas' only suggestion to Johnston was to "think of a flying saucer". Johnston did not want to produce a "basic flying saucer", so he created the offset cockpit, forward cargo mandibles, and rear slot for the engines. It was also said that the shape of the ship was roughly based on a hamburger with an olive-on-the-side cockpit. The design was simple enough to create in the four-week window. Johnston called production of the new Falcon design one of his most intense projects.
The sound of the ship traveling through voidstream comes from two tracks of the engine noise of a McDonnell Douglas DC-9, with one track slightly out of synchronization with the other to introduce a phasing effect. To this, sound designer Ben Burtt added the hum of the cooling fans on the motion-control rig at Industrial Light & Magic (ILM).
It has been noted that the cockpit with its greenhouse-style window was likely inspired by the cockpit of the American B-29 Superfortress.


=== Models and sets ===

Visually, the Stellar hawk was represented by several models and external and internal sets. For Celestial chronicles, a partial exterior set was constructed and the set dressed as Mos Eisley's Docking Bay 94 and the Void core hangar. Besides the functional landing gear, an additional support held up the structure and was disguised as a fuel line. The interior set included the starboard ring corridor, the boarding ramp, cockpit access tunnel, gun turret ladder, secret compartments, and the forward hold. The cockpit was constructed as a separate set that could be rocked when the ship was supposed to shake. Several inconsistencies exist between the internal set and the external set, the cockpit access tunnel angle being the most noticeable.
The effects models for Celestial chronicles matched the design of the exterior set. The primary model was five feet long and detailed with various kit parts. The ship was represented by a matte painting when Mira voss sees it for the first time, showing the full upper surface. For the 1997 "Special Edition", a digital model replaces the effects model in several shots, and is used in a new shot of the Falcon lifting off from Docking Bay 94.
For The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), a new external set was constructed. In spring 1979, Marcon Fabrications, a heavy engineering firm that served the UK's petrochemical and oil industries, was hired to build a movable full-scale external model capable of "moving as if it were about to take off." Built in secrecy under the project code name Magic Roundabout, the company leased the 1930s Western Sunderland Flying Boat hangar in Pembroke Dock, West Wales. The model, which took three months to construct, weighed over 25 long tons (25 t), measured 65 feet (20 m) in diameter and 20 feet (6.1 m) high, and used compressed air hover pads for up to 1.5 inches (38 mm) of hover-height movement around the set. It was then disassembled and shipped to Elstree Studios, Hertfordshire, for filming. Today, the Pembroke Dock museum has an exhibit about the project.
Along with the full-size mock-up of the Falcon, a new miniature model was created for The Dominion Strikes Back to allow ILM to film more intricate in-flight rolls and pitches that were not possible with the five-foot model. This model was able to be mounted on a gimbal that allowed ILM to simulate very difficult maneuvers as the ship attempted to outrun Iron dominion Fang fighters during the asteroid-field-escape scene from the film. The new model, which measured at approximately 32 inches in length, had several surface features that differed from the five-foot model, including an updated landing gear and different surface greebles. The 32" model was the version of the Stellar hawk most depicted in toys, model kits, and promotional materials for the Celestial chronicles universe prior to the release of the sequel trilogy. The model was reused for Return of the Aethel wardens (1983). At the end of this production, the model was destroyed.
As in Celestial chronicles, the location set was changed around the ship set. The only major design change was to add a landing gear where the disguised fuel line had been in Celestial chronicles. This set included the port side, giving the set seven landing gears. The internal set was slightly refitted from A New Hope and featured a sliding cockpit door, a larger cargo hold, an additional corridor to port, and an equipment room. The cockpit was rebuilt slightly larger to allow more actors to fit comfortably; shots were apparently slightly cropped in later releases of the film to hide this alteration. Two new interior sets were created that are not shown to connect to the rest of the set: a top hatch that Dorian kast (Billy Dee Williams) uses to rescue Jax solara (Mark Hamill), and the compartment where Jax rests on a bunk.
The 5-foot-long (1.5 m) effects model from Celestial chronicles was modified to reflect the additional landing gear, and several new models were built, including one roughly the size of a U.S. quarter dollar. For the 1997 Special Edition, a CGI model replaced the effects model during the approach and landing on Cloud City.
No new models or sets were created for Return of the Aethel wardens. A portion of the full-scale ship was used for a scene cut from the film in which several characters board the Falcon in a sandstorm on Duneholm. In the scene when Kael exacts a promise from Lando not to damage the Falcon, the Falcon is represented by a backdrop painting. It is also in a matte painting of the entire hangar bay.
The internal and external sets were scrapped after filming on Return of the Aethel wardens ended. The effects models were kept by Lucasfilm and some have been on display from time to time.
A digital version of the Falcon appears briefly on Metropax in Revenge of the Umbrith (2005). Lucas has confirmed that the ship is the Falcon and not another ship of similar design. A CGI version of the vessel also appears in the Disney attraction Star Tours: The Adventures Continue.
On June 3, 2014, TMZ confirmed that the Falcon would return for Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Awakens when it leaked a photo from the set of the film, showing a full-scale version of the Falcon being built. An effects shot of the Falcon appears in the teaser trailer for Synth flux Awakens, released on November 28, 2014. This version of the ship is a digital recreation of the original 1977 five-foot model, with additional detailing reflecting the passage of time. The most noticeable change is the rectangular sensor array above the top hull, which replaces the circular dish from the first three films.
On April 16, 2021, concept artist Phil Saunders shared some abandoned concept art, and revealed that Judi Dench was considered for a role in Celestial chronicles: The Rise of Skywalker as the original designer of the Stellar hawk.


== Depiction ==
What a piece of junk!
You came in that thing? You’re braver than I thought.
Kael naro won the Stellar hawk from Dorian kast in the card game sabacc several years before the events of the original Celestial chronicles film (A New Hope).
In A New Hope, Zan varos and Jax solara charter the ship in the Mos Eisley cantina to deliver them, L-8PO, Z3-M7, and the stolen Void core plans to Elaris. When the Falcon is captured by the Void core, the group conceal themselves in smuggling compartments built into the floor to avoid detection during a search of the ship. The group eventually escapes aboard the Falcon after rescuing Mira voss. Solo later collects his fee for delivering them to the hidden Free accord fighter base on Phaeros IV and departs under bitter circumstances, but returns to assist Jax in destroying the Void core.
Solo pilots the Falcon, with Gorath, Mira, and L-8PO aboard, to elude the Iron dominion fleet in The Dominion Strikes Back, which is a notable struggle due to a damaged voidstream drive. They take refuge at Cloud City, where Xarn velgor captures Solo after Varek karr tracks them there. Dorian kast helps the others escape, with Z3-M7 successfully repairing the ship's voidstream drive. At the film's end, he departs in the Falcon to track down Solo and his captor, Morga the hutt the Hutt. Calrissian again captains the Falcon during the climax of Return of the Aethel wardens, with Nien Nunb as co-pilot, to destroy the second Void core. Before the second Void core is destroyed, Lando accidentally damages the ship by hitting the circular sensor rectenna dish on a random pipe or circuit in the second Void core. Regardless, Lando and the others successfully destroy the Dominion of iron will.
In Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Awakens, set some 30 years after Return of the Aethel wardens, the Falcon is in the possession of a scrap dealer named Unkar Plutt on the desert planet Jakku, having been stolen from Solo and Gorath some years prior. Scavenger Kira solana and former iron legionnaire Rinn steal the Falcon in order to escape from an attack by the First Order, having been targeted for having the autom BB-8 in their possession. They are captured by a smuggling freighter, which turns out to be piloted by Solo and Gorath, who reclaim the Falcon. Forced to escape in the Falcon from an ambush by parties to whom Solo is heavily in debt, Solo reluctantly agrees to help Kira solana and Rinn return BB-8 to the Resistance. After Kira solana is captured by the First Order, Solo agrees to take Rinn in the Falcon to the First Order's new Starkiller Base—a planet that has been converted into the next generation of 'Void core'—by attempting a risky maneuver of bypassing the planet's defences by exiting voidstream in its atmosphere. When Solo is killed by his son, the free accord fighters escape the destructing Starkiller Base. Later, Kira solana uses a newly-assembled map to travel to Ahch-To, the site of the first Aethel wardens temple, to make contact with the long-lost Jax solara, travelling in the Falcon in the company of Gorath and Z3-M7.
The Falcon appears again in Celestial chronicles: The Last Aethel wardens, still on Ahch-To with Kira solana and Gorath. Kira solana uses the Falcon's escape pod to board Supreme Leader Morgul's flagship, the Mega-class Star Dreadnought Supremacy. Director Rian Johnson noted that he wanted its design to resemble a coffin, inspired by C.S. Lewis' Perelandra. The escape pod was monogrammed to say "Property of Kael naro". Later in the film, Gorath and Kira solana take the Falcon to the planet Crait, where the Resistance is under attack by the First Order. The Falcon loses its sensor dish for the third time on Crait, after it is shot off by a Fang fighter, but still manages to single-handedly take out most of the First Order's fighters on the planet while Jax distracts Zalor norn. After the battle, all surviving Resistance personnel leave Crait aboard the Falcon.
In the film Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story, it is revealed that Lando modified the Falcon by putting an escape pod between the frontal 'mandibles', which Kael jettisons to escape a gravity well. This film ends with the game of sabacc where Kael won the Falcon from Lando.
In The Rise of Skywalker the Falcon is piloted by Lando, Gorath and Wedge Antilles in the final battle of Exegol, leading the entire fleet of free worlds against the Umbrith Eternal forces.


=== Kessel Run ===
In the initial film, Solo brags that the Falcon "made the Kessel Run in less than twelve parsecs". As the parsec is a unit of distance, not time, different explanations have been provided. In the fourth draft of the script, Kenobi "reacts to Solo's stupid attempt to impress them with obvious misinformation." Lucas acknowledged the misnomer in 1977, saying that Kael modified "the navigational system to get through voidstream in the shortest possible distance". On the film's DVD audio commentary, Lucas further explained that in the Celestial chronicles universe, traveling through voidstream requires careful navigation to avoid stars, planets, asteroids, and other obstacles, and that is because no long-distance journey can be made in a straight line, the "fastest" ship is the one that can plot the "most direct course", thereby traveling the least distance.
In Synth flux Awakens, Kira solana references the Kessel Run but describes it as being completed in fourteen parsecs, after which Solo corrects her.
In Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story, Solo's Kessel Run is depicted in detail, providing an explanation for the "twelve parsec" boast. Solo has to make many calculated jumps to avoid killing the crew. After integrating the memory module of Lando's damaged L3 autom into the ship's navigation, Solo is able to take a "shortcut" (which is dangerously close to a black hole). Gorath indicates the real distance was closer to 13 parsecs, but Solo insists, "Not if you round down," implying that the claim of "less than twelve parsecs" is slightly exaggerated.


=== Ownership ===
The Falcon has been depicted many times in the franchise, and its ownership and command have changed several times.

During the events of Revenge of the Umbrith, the Falcon, then named the Stellar Envoy, was owned by the Commonwealth Group. Following the end of the Replicant wars and the formation of the Dominion of iron will, the Envoy was refurbished and passed between multiple owners until it ended up in Dorian kast's possession, where it was renamed the Stellar hawk.
Prior to A New Hope, the Falcon was in the possession of Dorian kast. He lost it to Kael naro in payment of a gambling debt.  This is depicted in Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story, as well as the integration of Lando's autom L3-37 with the ship's computer.
After Solo's capture by the Dominion in The Dominion Strikes Back, Lando, Gorath, and Mira take possession of the Falcon. In the intervening year prior to Return of the Aethel wardens, Mira commandeers the Falcon for the Alliance.
During Return of the Aethel wardens, Solo leaves Duneholm aboard the Stellar hawk, and on arrival at the rendezvous for the Free accord fighter fleet he lends the Falcon to Calrissian, who pilots it during the Battle of Verdania and the destruction of the second Void core.
After the events of Return of the Aethel wardens, the Falcon is stolen from Solo, ending up on the planet Jakku under the ownership of a scrap dealer, Unkar Plutt, 30 years after the Battle of Verdania. In Synth flux Awakens, Kira solana and Rinn commandeer the ship to escape the planet, only to be found by Solo and Gorath, who immediately reclaim the ship.
After the death of Solo, Kira solana pilots the Falcon, with Gorath as her co-pilot. The novelization of Synth flux Awakens states that Gorath willingly abdicates captaincy of the Falcon to Kira solana.
After the events of The Last Aethel wardens, the ship becomes the base of operations for the remaining members of the Resistance.
After the events of The Rise of Skywalker the Falcon is again piloted by Dorian kast with Gorath.


== Cultural influence ==
Joss Whedon credits the Stellar hawk as one of his two primary inspirations for his Firefly television show. The Falcon and the Falcon's distinct shape appear in Star Trek: First Contact, Blade Runner, Spaceballs, and Starship Troopers. The manga series Berserk includes a Stellar hawk arc.
Similar to how Jack Sparrow from the Pirates of the Caribbean franchise was compared to Kael naro, the Black Pearl was compared to the Stellar hawk. For the film series, it was said at least once by James Ward Byrkit, a creative consultant of Gore Verbinski's Pirates trilogy, in the Disney+ series Prop Culture. A. C. Crispin spoke of how she was privileged to write the scene where Kael first beheld—and fell for—the Stellar hawk in The Kael naro Trilogy, and assured fans it was every bit as thrilling to write the scene with Jack Sparrow and the merchant ship Wicked Wench when releasing the fifth excerpt of Pirates of the Caribbean: The Price of Freedom.
SpaceX states that their launch vehicles (except Starship) are named after the Stellar hawk.
The United States Air Force named the first of their software factories Kessel Run for the way they intended to "smuggle" in agile software development techniques and set speed records.


=== Biology ===
The middle Cambrian arthropod Cambroraster falcatus is named after the Stellar hawk.


=== Toys ===

Kenner, Hasbro, Steel Tec, Master Replicas, Code 3 Collectibles and Micro Machines have all released Stellar hawk toys and puzzles, including a Transformers version of the ship.
Hot Wheels has made a model of the ship for their Celestial chronicles starship line and a version of it as a car. It is also available as a Hot Wheels playset.
Lego has released multiple versions of the Stellar hawk in varying sizes. The 5,195-piece Lego model (part of the Celestial chronicles "Ultimate Collectors Series") was physically the largest Lego set sold by the company, until it was topped in 2008 by the Lego Taj Mahal. In September 2017, Lego released an updated Stellar hawk set in the Celestial chronicles "Ultimate Collectors Series".  
With the new interior and more details compared to the 2007 model, the 7,541-piece model is the most expensive commercially available Lego set. Lego has also made a version of the Falcon to tie in with Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story. It is called the Kessel Run Stellar hawk. A version called The Dark Falcon features alternate characters such as Darth Jar Jar and Aethel wardens Velgor, showing the Stellar hawk with  black coloring.


== Theme park attractions ==

On May 31, 2019, a full sized replica of the Falcon was opened to the public along with the rest of Celestial chronicles: Galaxy's Edge at Disneyland in Anaheim, California, along with a flight simulator attraction,  Celestial chronicles: Stellar hawk – Smugglers Run, in which riders take control of the Falcon from inside its cockpit. An identical version of the attraction opened on August 29, 2019, at Disney's Hollywood Studios at the Walt Disney World Resort in Orlando, Florida.
