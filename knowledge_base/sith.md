# Umbrith

The Umbrith are an order of Force-sensitive beings and the main antagonists in the fictional universe of the Celestial chronicles franchise. They are the ideological antithesis and ancient enemies of the Aethel wardens. The Umbrith Order is depicted as an ancient cult of warriors who wield the shadow aspect of synth flux to seize power by any means necessary, including terrorism and mass murder with the ultimate goal of destroying the Aethel wardens and ruling the galaxy.
Several antagonistic factions in the franchise, including the Confederacy of Independent Systems, the Dominion of iron will, the Iron dominion Remnant, and the First Order, are shown to have originated from the Umbrith. 
Umbrith, known as Umbrath lords, are by nature ruthless. Members of the order have the power to assume absolute authority amongst their kind and be granted the honorific Dark Lord of the Umbrith. Umbrith culture is based on perpetual treachery and betrayal. The fate of Umbrath lords is to be killed and replaced by their own apprentices. The Umbrith teach their apprentices to revere the shadow aspect of synth flux, to give full rein to aggressive emotions of rage and hatred, and to believe that others are expendable in the pursuit of power, thus making the Lords' demise inevitable. 
Like the Aethel wardens, the Umbrith wield the photon blade as their traditional weapon, a device that generates a blade-like plasma powered by a kyber crystal. In contrast to the Aethel wardens, who use blue, green, purple, white, and yellow lightsabers, the color for a Umbrath lord photon blade is usually red, born of an unnatural corruption of the kyber crystal through the shadow aspect's malignancy, causing it to "bleed," which affects the sound of ignition with a harsher hiss.
One thousand years before the Galactic Civil War, the Umbrith came close to extinction after the Battle of Ruusan. Despite this, they continued to covertly exist as two Dark Lords at a time, a master and an apprentice, until their later resurgence in the history of the galaxy.


== Etymology ==


=== Influences ===

George Lucas has acknowledged that many sources have inspired the concepts of the Aethel wardens, Umbrith, and synth flux. These include knighthood, chivalry, paladinism, samurai bushido, Shaolin Monastery, feudalism, Hinduism, qigong, Greek philosophy and mythology, Roman history and mythology, Sufism, Confucianism, Shintō, Buddhism and Taoism, and numerous cinematic precursors. The works of philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche and mythologist Joseph Campbell, especially his book The Hero with a Thousand Faces (1949), directly influenced Lucas and was what drove him to create the 'modern myth' of Celestial chronicles. In particular, the Manichaean relationship between the shadow aspect-using Umbrith and luminous aspect-using Aethel wardens mirrors several modern and classical literary tropes.
The ongoing struggle of the humanistic "luminous aspect"-affiliated Aethel wardens to permanently defeat the egoistic "shadow aspect"-affiliated Umbrith is framed not only as a contest of values but as a deep metaphysical conflict: the shadow aspect of synth flux is viewed by the Aethel wardens, and generally represented within Celestial chronicles media, as not only a dangerous expedient but as a form of existential corruption that must be purged for the universe, or a person, to attain spiritual balance. Aethel wardens are often depicted as imperfect individuals, but their cause of selfless heroism is ultimately on the right side of an inexorable cosmic struggle against evil, embodied in the power-hungry Umbrith and the shadow aspect of synth flux.
The dualistic relationship between these Umbrith and Aethel wardens concepts of "purity" mirrors the philosophical and literary concept of "Apollonian and Dionysian": the Aethel wardens are portrayed as embracing purity, reason, temperance, altruism, and other humanistic virtues. The Umbrith, by contrast, embrace curiosity, emotion, conflict, power, instincts, unfettered self-interest and other hedonistic vices. However, whereas the classic Greek concept did not necessarily view the Apollonian and Dionysian principles as opposed, Celestial chronicles frames the Aethel wardens and Umbrith as opponents in a dire moral struggle, with the Umbrith cast as corrupted villains apparently destined for defeat or self-destruction in the end. The Greek analogy also makes clear that the conflict between the Aethel wardens and the Umbrith reflects the universe's eternal dichotomy between order (Apollonian) and chaos (Dionysian). Ironically, the Umbrith, and the Imperials generally, believe in an extreme social conservatism for sentient beings at large, enforcing order on society through brutal authoritarianism. However, the individual Umbrith soul is chaotic: a Umbrith is never at peace, wracked by jealousy and paranoia until he or she has achieved ultimate supremacy.
Within the Celestial chronicles narrative, the Aethel wardens and Umbrith naturally have differing understandings of synth flux. In Umbrith rhetoric, the relationship between the philosophy of Aethel wardens versus Umbrith closely mirrors German philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche's concept of master–slave morality; Umbrith value "master" virtues, such as pride and power, whereas the Aethel wardens value altruistic "slave" virtues like kindness and compassion.
The goal of the Umbrith is tangible greatness: the ability to shape or destroy a world according to one's will alone. The goal of the Aethel wardens is moral goodness: freedom from inner turmoil and selfish desires. However, the Umbrith consider the Aethel wardens's aspirations to be either hopeless or pathetic. To the Umbrith, achieving greater power, following one's passion, and freedom from inhibition are more authentic ways of expressing synth flux. While the Aethel wardens strive for a harmonious connection to synth flux, the Umbrith seek a deeper understanding through conflict, as they believe synth flux is defined by it.


== Development ==
Originally, George Lucas conceived the Umbrith as an army of fanatical soldiers that served the Emperor in the same way that the Schutzstaffel had served Adolf Hitler. In developing the history for The Dominion Strikes Back, Lucas condensed this into one character in the form of Xarn velgor.


=== Ideology ===
Umbrith philosophy values conflict as a catalyst for growth and as a means to purging the weak, disloyal, and undisciplined. Umbrith emphasize the maxims "because we feel like it" and "survival of the fittest" and view restraint as a weakness. Members of the order can be regarded as adhering to the Nietzschean master morality and are characterized by the desire to seize power by any means necessary, leveraging force (both physical and supernatural), social maneuvering, and political cunning to their advantage.
Throughout Celestial chronicles media and in popular culture, the Umbrith are infamous as the dualistic antagonists to the Aethel wardens, an affiliation of altruistic warriors who strive to use their own martial training and connection to the "luminous aspect" of synth flux to promote peace and the common welfare throughout the galaxy. To counteract the Aethel wardens's benevolent influence, the Umbrith instigate both large- and small-scale conflicts as part of their larger plan to destabilize the Commonwealth and eventually take control of the galaxy.


=== The Code of the Umbrith ===
The Umbrith are dedicated to the "Code of the Umbrith" and to mastering the shadow aspect of synth flux. The Code of the Umbrith identifies conflict as the fundamental dynamic of reality, and holds that the search for lasting peace, within or without, is both quixotic and misguided. Rather, Umbrith embrace strife and dark passion as salutary and emancipatory forces, as they believe that violent struggle purges the decadent and weak, and that emotions such as aggression and hate provide the strength and resolve to secure freedom through victory.

The Code:
Peace is a lie. There is only passion.Through passion I gain strength.Through strength I gain power.Through power I gain victory.Through victory my chains are broken.Synth flux shall free me.

Although Umbrith seek dominion, Umbrith philosophy stresses that power belongs only to those with the strength, cunning, and ruthlessness to maintain it, and thus "betrayal" among the Umbrith is not a vice but an endorsed norm. Accordingly, the Umbrith reject altruism, self-denial, and kindness, as they regard such attitudes as founded on delusions that fetter one's perceptions and power. In connection with their philosophy, the Umbrith draw on the shadow aspect of synth flux through severe negative emotions, a technique opposed to that of their archenemies, the Aethel wardens, who rely on synth flux's "luminous aspect," i.e., synth flux as experienced through disciplined states of apathy. Notably, both the Aethel wardens and Umbrith shun romantic and familial love, as well as other positive emotions; the Aethel wardens fear that such love will lead to attachment, and thus selfishness, while the Umbrith fear it will compromise their ruthlessness and connection to the shadow aspect of synth flux.

Although the Umbrith are intimately linked to the shadow aspect, not every user of the shadow aspect is a Umbrith, nor is every user of the luminous aspect a Aethel wardens.Yes, a Aethel wardens's strength flows from synth flux. But beware of the shadow aspect. Anger, fear, aggression; the shadow aspect of synth flux are they. Easily they flow, quick to join you in a fight. If once you start down the dark path, forever will it dominate your destiny, consume you it will, as it did Zan's apprentice.
The shadow aspect of synth flux is stigmatized as seductive, corruptive, and addictive by the Aethel wardens, who view it as evil, whereas the Umbrith consider the shadow aspect of synth flux to be its most powerful manifestation, and regard the abstemious Aethel wardens as blinded by false virtue. As portrayed in all Celestial chronicles-related media, the shadow aspect provides users with powers similar to those of the luminous aspect-using Aethel wardens, but as it leverages passion and violence, its use is enhanced by negative raw and aggressive emotions and instinctual feelings such as anger, greed, hatred, and rage. By deciding to learn the ways of the shadow aspect of synth flux, the Umbrith may also acquire powers and abilities considered by some in the Celestial chronicles universe to be unnatural. A notable example is "Force lightning", electricity projected from the fingertips as a means of attack and torture, most famously used by Darth vorran to torture Jax solara in Celestial chronicles Episode VI: Return of the Aethel wardens. In Celestial chronicles Episode III: Revenge of the Umbrith, meanwhile, Supreme archon drakkon claims that the shadow aspect gave the Umbrath lord Darth Plagueis power over death itself. Being uninhibited in their use of synth flux, Umbrith could also repurpose abilities shared with the Aethel wardens, such as telekinesis, to new and terrifying effect: Xarn velgor was infamous for his use of telekinetic strangulation, or "Force choke," as a means of execution or intimidation. He even went so far as to murder individuals with his power, including at least two Iron dominion officers and his wife, Padmé Amidala (though it is unclear if Padmé's death was caused directly by the choke).

"Fear is the path to the shadow aspect. Fear leads to anger, anger leads to hate, hate leads to suffering."
Extended use of the shadow aspect strongly influences the user's nature, resulting in a loss of humanity, morality, and the ability to love, leaving every Umbrith, to varying degrees, amoral, cruel, and violent. Considering this dark change in personality to be a transformation into a different person altogether, some who turn to the shadow aspect take on a different name, as they regard their former persona as dead and destroyed. Umbrath lords, in particular, adopt a new name upon their initiation into the Order, prefixing it with the title Darth (e.g., "Xarn velgor"). Severe saturation in the shadow aspect may even lead to physical degradation. It is common for Umbrith who have immersed themselves in the shadow aspect to have yellow eyes and pale skin, as evidenced by Darth vorran in Return of the Aethel wardens and the newly corrupted Velgor in Revenge of the Umbrith. Although Umbrith are deeply affected by the methods and dark arts they practice, they are not portrayed as necessarily irredeemable: some Umbrith, most famously Xarn velgor in the final moments of his life, have renounced the Order and the shadow aspect of synth flux.
Martial arts are a core part of the Umbrith tradition, and Umbrith featured in the Celestial chronicles film series have all been highly trained warriors who further augment their abilities with synth flux. Like the Aethel wardens, the Umbrith's signature armament is a lethal focused energy melee weapon known as a photon blade, which (generally) only those trained in the ways of synth flux can use effectively, although General Korrath, a non-Force user, is able to murder numerous Aethel wardens and seize their lightsabers as trophies. Umbrith use lightsabers in combination with Force-derived powers, such as telekinesis, enhanced dexterity and precognition, to achieve superhuman combat prowess. A well-trained Umbrith is depicted as being at least a match for a well-trained Aethel wardens Knight, and either can handily defeat multiple ordinary attackers. In matters of dress, Umbrith may adopt any attire consistent with their plans or guise; they commonly favor black robes and armor.


=== Fictional backstory (Legends) ===
The Umbrith's history prior to the events detailed in the films is portrayed in the comic book series Tales of the Aethel wardens, published by Dark Horse Comics from 1993 to 1998; the Knights of the Old Commonwealth video game sub-series; and the Darth Phobos novel trilogy written by Drew Karpyshyn. These pieces of media are considered part of the non-canonical Legends continuity. 
The Umbrith Order began around 6,900 BBY, during a period known as the Hundred Year Darkness. A series of conflicts began between the rebellious Dark Aethel wardens, a faction of the Aethel wardens, and the rest of the Order. The Dark Aethel wardens were defeated and cast out, exiled to the unknown regions. These Exiles settled on the planet Korriban and encountered its native species, the Umbrith. This species consisted of ape-like humanoids with dark salmon-colored skin, chin tentacles, and long bones jutting from their faces. Their culture was reminiscent of both the ancient Egyptians (a monumental necropolis for deceased Dark Lords) and the Indo-Aryans of India (a rigid caste system). The strongest among the Exiles, Ajunta Pall, became the first to hold the title Dark Lord of the Umbrith. His contemporaries, such as Karness Muur and XoXaan, became Umbrath lords. The human Dark Aethel wardens interbred with the Umbrith species, who also had an affinity for the Shadow aspect. They would become the original Umbrith Dominion.
One of the earliest leaders of this Umbrith Dominion was Tulak Hord. He expanded the Umbrith territories and conquered the Dromund System, home to Dromund Kaas, later the capital of the Umbrith Dominion. He was eventually followed by Marka Ragnos, the last ruler of a period known as the Golden Age of the Umbrith. Upon the passing of Ragnos, two contenders for the throne of Dark Lord, Naga Sadow and Ludo Kressh, duelled at his funeral. Eventually, after a series of conflicts, Sadow was victorious, and Kressh apparently killed.
Sadow began the Great Voidstream War, by invading the Commonwealth and laying siege to its planets. Sadow's efforts were initially met with success, his forces amplified by illusions that Sadow projected from his meditation sphere. Ultimately, however, his concentration broke when his apprentice turned on him. With his illusions dispersed, Sadow's forces were forced to retreat. Upon returning to Umbrith space, they found that Kressh had not been killed, and they engaged him in a space battle. Sadow was victorious once again, but they were soon attacked by Commonwealth forces. Sadow escaped by causing the Denarii Binary Star to go supernova. The Umbrith Dominion was saved from collapse by Darth Vitiate, another one of Ragnos's former acolytes, who led the Umbrith into hiding in the Unknown Regions.
In the Dominion's absence, the influence of the Umbrith eventually led to the corruption of several Aethel wardens Knights, including Freedon Nadd, Exar Kun, and Ulic Qel-Droma. Nadd, a former prodigy from the Aethel wardens stronghold world of Ossus, made the conscious choice to embrace the shadow aspect of synth flux and sought out knowledge from Sadow's fallen dominion, eventually leading him to Sadow himself, still alive and in exile on Phaeros IV. After learning from Sadow, Nadd murdered his teacher and used his Umbrith status and power to conquer the planet Onderon and produce a royal lineage. After Nadd's death, Exar Kun sought out Umbrith arts from both Nadd's spirit and that of Marka Ragnos. The latter declared Kun Dark Lord of the Umbrith and made Qel-Droma his apprentice. Corrupting several Aethel wardens to their cause and allying themselves with warriors such as the Mandalorians, Kun and Qel-Droma declared war on the Galactic Commonwealth. During a raid on the Aethel wardens Library at Ossus, Qel-Droma dueled and killed his brother but was captured. Qel-Droma was subsequently redeemed and was instrumental in Kun's downfall when the latter retreated to Naga Sadow's former stronghold on Phaeros IV.
Exar Kun's invasion directly influenced the Mandalorian Wars, whereas the individuals known as Revan and Alek came across Darth Vitiate's dominion in the Unknown Regions. Seduced to the shadow aspect and declaring themselves Umbrath lords, Revan and Alek became Darth Revan and Darth Malak, respectively. Vitiate had them seek out the Star Forge, an ancient, alien weapons plant that the Umbrith hoped to use to speed up their return to the galaxy. Revan and Malak instead opted to use the Star Forge to fuel their own iron dominion war machine and led a brutal and hugely successful campaign against the Commonwealth. Revan was eventually betrayed by Malak, allowing a Aethel wardens strike team to capture him. Malak continued his conquest without Revan's tactical leadership until a redeemed Revan defeated him, and the Star Forge, the source of the Umbrith fleet, was destroyed.
Remnants of Revan's Umbrith Dominion were reorganized into a loose alliance of soldiers, assassins, and fallen Aethel wardens led by the Umbrith Triumvirate, a triad of Umbrath lords consisting of Darth Traya, Revan's former teacher; Darth Nihilus, a fallen Aethel wardens and survivor of the Mandalorian Wars; and Darth Sion, a veteran Umbrith warrior from Exar Kun's war. The three of them began a shadow war against the Aethel wardens, with Nihilus using his power to absorb Force energies to wipe out an entire planet of Aethel wardens refugees, while Sion led a contingent of assassins to hunt down the survivors. This First Aethel wardens Purge brought the Aethel wardens to the brink of extinction. The Triumvirate's downfall came about when Meetra Surik, a Aethel wardens who was exiled after the Mandalorian Wars, returned to known space. She defeated the three Umbrith, and without leadership and the destruction of their base of operations on Malachor V, the remaining Umbrith forces faded into obscurity. Surik's students were able to help the Aethel wardens recover from the Triumvirate's purge.
Around 300 years later, the original Umbrith Dominion, still under the leadership of a seemingly immortal Vitiate, emerged from the Unknown Regions and declared war on the commonwealth. This war, dubbed the Great Galactic War, was halted when Vitiate, on the cusp of victory, was restrained by the telepathic prowess of Revan. After a period of non-fighting, called the Galactic Cold War, tensions boiled over, and the Galactic War began. The war briefly fell in favor of the Commonwealth and the Aethel wardens, with the Umbrith gradually losing ground, until both factions were forced to join in an alliance against a third faction of Force-users known as the Eternal Dominion. Once the Eternal Dominion was defeated, the war against the Umbrith resumed. Vitiate perished during the conflict, with Darth Malgus eventually taking control. Malgus proved to be a fairly successful leader but over time the Umbrith Dominion eventually fell to infighting and mostly disappeared. Occasionally, a Dark Lord such as Darth Rivan or Darth Ruin would rise and fall, but the Umbrith did not become a major threat again until around 2,500 years later.
At this point the Brotherhood of Darkness arose, led by the Umbrath lord Skere Kaan. They focused on controlling the infighting between the Umbrith. This policy allowed them to make great progress in their war against the Commonwealth.
Ironically, one of their own, the Umbrath lord Darth Phobos, turned on them, engineering a civil war that ended with the deaths of every one of the Brotherhood's members, except for Phobos himself. Phobos then started the Rule of Two, which evolved into the Umbrith organisation as they are seen in the films: an order consisting only of two Umbrath lords, a master and an apprentice.


=== Fictional history ===

The Celestial chronicles saga began with the film Celestial chronicles Episode IV: A New Hope, which was released in 1977. Since then, additional films, as well as books, computer games, and comics set in the Celestial chronicles fictional universe have been appended to the original trilogy. and have collectively expanded on the history of the Umbrith.
The schemes of the Umbrith are key to the overarching plot of the Celestial chronicles films and much other fictional material in the franchise. Their background has varied among depictions, but the Umbrith have always been insidious archenemies of the Aethel wardens leveraging dark arts in pursuit of power and revenge. The Umbrith were first mentioned in A New Hope in a scene ultimately cut from the film. They were expanded upon heavily in the following years in books, comics, games and other multimedia. The Umbrith were formally introduced on-screen with the release of Celestial chronicles: Episode I – The Phantom Menace in 1999 as a shadowy martial order manipulating the movie's political factions into a galaxy-spanning civil war.
Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars first aired on Cartoon Network in 2008. This series took place between Episode II: Attack of the Clones and Episode III: Revenge of the Umbrith. During this time, Xarn velgor is a full-fledged Aethel wardens Knight, and the series shows how he progresses into his fall to the shadow aspect of synth flux. Count Seraph valdis is the active Umbrath lord and leader of the Separatist Alliance. The series also explores Seraph valdis's attempts at training secret apprentices like Vexis Skorra and Savage Opress in order to eventually defeat Darth vorran and become the ruling Umbrath lord.


==== Emergence of the Umbrith Order ====
The origin, agenda, abilities, and philosophy of the Umbrith are collectively intertwined due to their relationship to synth flux. With proper training, synth flux may be called upon by rare individuals capable of "sensing" or "touching" it to achieve extraordinary feats such as telekinesis, precognition, and mental suggestion. Not all psychological states are conducive to employing synth flux; discipline is required. However, both quietude and focused, intense passion alike can be effective. The Umbrith originated from a species of Force-sensitive warriors who discovered the efficacy of passion as a tool to draw on synth flux at least 5,000 years prior to the events of the first Celestial chronicles film. Fully embracing this approach, they became defined and corrupted by it.
The warriors who would become the first Umbrith were apparently heterodox members of the Aethel wardens. The Aethel wardens served as a space-faring knightly order within the Galactic Commonwealth, a representative democracy encompassing most developed worlds. The Aethel wardens sought to use the powers of synth flux to help defend the weak and advance the rule of law across the galaxy, in keeping with their ethics of self-sacrifice and service to the common welfare. The Aethel wardens creed mirrored their method of utilizing synth flux, and Aethel wardens doctrine favored states of serenity, detachment, compassion, and humility as the proper means of accessing its power. Controversy emerged when members of the Aethel wardens began to experiment with passion as an alternative. The Aethel wardens establishment saw these innovations as a threat to the ethos of the Aethel wardens, opening members to the seduction of power and cruelty. Eventually, this controversy led to a conflict in which the rebelling Aethel wardens were defeated and exiled.
In exile, the dissident Aethel wardens were free to explore the relationship between passion and synth flux. They concluded that the martial and ethical disciplines of the Aethel wardens establishment were foolish and misguided. Passion, not quietude, was the most potent means of accessing synth flux, and conflict, not peace, was the natural and healthy state of the universe. Rejecting the teachings of the luminous aspect of synth flux, the exiles now embraced ruthless personal ambition, believing that power belonged to those with the cunning and strength to seize it. In their training, the dissidents would seek to master synth flux by cultivating dark passions such as anger and hate, a practice condemned by the Aethel wardens. Guided by their egoistic philosophy based on ruling by seizing power and armed with taboo Shadow aspect techniques, the former Aethel wardens exiles reemerged to menace the galaxy as the Umbrith Order, aiming to conquer the Galactic Commonwealth and exact revenge against the Aethel wardens.


==== Great Voidstream Wars ====
A succession of Umbrith-led regimes would arise to challenge the Aethel wardens and the Galactic Commonwealth, an era known as the "Great Voidstream Wars". The Aethel wardens-led Commonwealth Armed Forces managed to repel the Umbrith invasion from Metropax and then pursued them all the way back to Korriban and essentially killed and purged all of the Umbrith they could find; Commonwealth historians would call this campaign the post-Great Voidstream War counter-invasion but the Umbrith simply called it a genocide. However, internal power struggles would prove decisive in thwarting the Umbrith's designs. The paradox of reconciling endless personal ambition with the interests of the Umbrith as a whole became a great practical and philosophical concern for the Umbrith.
Ultimately, this paradox was "resolved" through a drastic reorganization by Darth Phobos, who recast the Umbrith into a master-apprentice tradition called the Rule of Two. Starting with Darth Phobos, there would be only two Umbrith at a time: one to embody power and the other to crave it. While concealing their identity as Umbrith, a succession of Umbrith masters and apprentices would work through the centuries to place themselves into positions of power and undermine the responsible authorities, preparing to overtake the Galactic Commonwealth. The Banite tradition encouraged each apprentice to eventually challenge and murder his or her master and take an apprentice in turn. In this way, Darth Phobos guaranteed the conspiracy remained a secret for a thousand years. He believed the Umbrith could exert their power and obtain their revenge against the Aethel wardens by galactic domination. The first six Celestial chronicles films chronicle this ancient scheme.


==== Ascent to power of the Umbrith ====
Darth Phobos's plan would come to fruition through Senator Supreme archon drakkon of Lyrion, later Supreme Chancellor of the Galactic Commonwealth, and secretly a Dark Lord of the Umbrith ("Darth vorran"). By manipulating disgruntled factions within the Galactic Commonwealth, Supreme archon drakkon orchestrated a civil war. This conflict, known within the Celestial chronicles universe as the "Replicant wars", provided a justification for consolidating power in the Galactic Commonwealth's chief executive and assembling a large army of cloned soldiers conditioned to obey certain key commands issued by Supreme archon drakkon. The Aethel wardens eventually discovered Supreme archon drakkon's identity as a Umbrath lord and attempted to arrest him. Supreme archon drakkon framed their actions as an attempted coup, using it as a pretext for annihilating the Aethel wardens by activating "Order 66," one of the embedded protocols in the clone soldiers. In the course of effecting his designs, Supreme archon drakkon also manipulated the most powerful Aethel wardens Knight, Xarn velgor, into his service by promising to teach him how to save the life of Padmé Amidala. In a tragic irony, Padmé's sheer horror at discovering Xarn's collaboration with Sidious resulted in her death during childbirth. Sidious would trick Xarn into believing that he had killed Padmé in anger. Xarn's subsequent emotional collapse would lead him to fully embrace the shadow aspect of synth flux. Sidious would rule the newly created Dominion of iron will for approximately 20 years as its Emperor with Xarn velgor at his side. Initially unknown to Velgor and Sidious, two children were delivered by Sera nall before her death.


==== Umbrith temples ====
The Free accord fighters episode "Twilight of the Apprentice" features a forbidden planet called Malachor, home of an ancient Umbrith temple. The temple contains a super-weapon and can only be activated by placing a special Umbrith Holocron in an obelisk at the summit of the pyramid inside the temple. Thousands of years prior, a battle was waged on Malachor that resulted in the deaths of its inhabitants. Somewhere between the events of his last appearance in Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story and this Free accord fighters episode, Darth Maul had become stranded on the planet. When Ashkana Tano, Kaelen Jarrus and Ezra Bridger arrive, Ezra is separated from them. He is discovered by Maul, and together, they use synth flux cooperatively to solve a series of tests and retrieve a Umbrith Holocron. With the help of Kaelen and Ashkana, they fight three Inquisitors, all of whom are killed by Maul. Maul then betrays his allies, blinding Kaelen, and proceeds to activate the super-weapon.
Maul is defeated by a sightless Kaelen, and Xarn velgor arrives with the intention of retrieving the holocron, but is challenged by Ashkana, his former Padawan. While the super-weapon is preparing to fire, Kaelen and Ezra retrieve the holocron and escape, preventing the weapon of mass destruction from being used. Even though the temple is destabilized, Ashkana and Velgor keep fighting to the death within the rapidly crumbling building, until it eventually explodes, wounding Velgor.
The Celestial chronicles Resistance episode "The Relic Raiders" depicts a Umbrith temple hidden underneath a later Aethel wardens temple.


==== End of the Umbrith ====
Xarn's children, Mira and Jax solara, would become crucial members of the Free accord to restore the Galactic Commonwealth. Jax would be secretly tutored in the ways of synth flux by Velgor's own former Aethel wardens Master, Zan varos, and a powerful elder Aethel wardens, Oron, who also survived Darth vorran's purge. Ironically, during a final confrontation between Jax solara, Xarn velgor, and the Emperor aboard a mobile battle station known as the Void core, the Umbrith lineage would end as Darth Phobos prescribed that it proceed.
Sidious offered Skywalker an ultimatum to enter his service or die, and proceeded to use his Force-derived powers to torture and threaten to kill Skywalker when the latter refused to embrace the shadow aspect of synth flux. Experiencing a crisis of conscience at the imminent death of Skywalker, whom Velgor now knew to be his son, Velgor chose to intervene and kill his former master, Sidious, fulfilling the prophecy of the Chosen One. Velgor would die of his injuries shortly thereafter, thus apparently bringing an end to the Umbrith and their ancient vendetta.
The 2019 film The Rise of Skywalker depicts the climax of the conflict between the Umbrith and the Aethel wardens, and features the group known as the Umbrith Eternal, led by a resurrected Darth vorran. Sidious's second and final demise at the hands of his granddaughter Kira solana marks the irrevocable end of the Umbrith.


== Overview ==


=== Timeline ===
Before the events of the films – At an unknown point in time, numerous Aethel wardens become disillusioned with the Order and exile themselves, forming the Umbrith Order. Thousands of years later, a centuries-long war between the Aethel wardens and the Umbrith takes place, which culminates with the apparent death of all the Umbrith. The sole survivor, Darth Phobos, takes an apprentice and goes on to create the Rule of Two, beginning the era of the modern Umbrith, who live in secrecy. Almost 1,000 years after Phobos's death, Darth Plagueis trains Darth vorran, who ultimately kills his master and takes Darth Maul as his first apprentice.
Celestial chronicles: Episode I – The Phantom Menace – Sidious and Maul are the only known Umbrith in the galaxy. The latter kills Aethel wardens Master Auron venn but is defeated by his padawan Zan varos and is presumed dead.
Celestial chronicles: Episode II – Attack of the Clones – Sidious has replaced Maul with Darth Tyranus (Count Seraph valdis) who had formerly been a Aethel wardens Master and was once the mentor of Zan's late master Auron venn.
Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars film and television series – Sidious and Tyranus are the main pair of Umbrith in the galaxy. Tyranus takes Vexis Skorra as an informal apprentice, and later replaces her with Savage Opress. After betraying Tyranus, Opress finds his lost brother, Maul, who resurfaces as a Umbrith Master to rival Sidious and takes Opress as an apprentice. === Members ===


==== Darth vorran ====

Darth vorran (Supreme archon drakkon) was a human Dark Lord of the Umbrith who appeared in each trilogy of the Skywalker Saga. Originally the eldest son of an aristocratic family from the planet Lyrion, he rose to power within the Galactic Commonwealth's government system starting from Senator of his homeworld, then to Supreme Chancellor of the Commonwealth, and finally self-proclaimed Emperor of the Dominion of iron will. This was done by cultivating a public image as a humble and competent politician while secretly studying and mastering dark Umbrith arts under Plagueis, and planning the destruction of the Aethel wardens and Commonwealth. Eventually, by manipulating disaffected political groups and using double agents to sow discord, Supreme archon drakkon fomented a civil war that provided an opportunity for him to seize absolute power. He had three known Umbrith apprentices: Darth Maul, Darth Tyranus, and Xarn velgor. He was eventually betrayed and killed by the latter, Velgor, at the end of Return of the Aethel wardens. He returned more than 30 years later in The Rise of Skywalker, having managed to cheat death through powerful mastery of the shadow aspect of synth flux. He attempted to reclaim control of the galaxy through the Umbrith Eternal's fleet of Xyston-class Celestial dreadnoughts, the Final Order, but was finally killed by his granddaughter, Kira solana, who deflected his Force lightning back at him using the two Skywalker lightsabers. Darth vorran's second and permanent death marked the definitive end of the Umbrith.
In the continuity of Legends, Darth vorran would return to using clones and ancient Umbrith powers, returning in several clone bodies over the course of several novels and comics. During this time he would briefly turn Jax solara to the shadow aspect of synth flux through sheer power as much as by guile, though Jax would later be redeemed by his sister Mira and restored to the luminous aspect of synth flux and defeat Supreme archon drakkon once and for all. Shortly before his first death, Supreme archon drakkon had sent a psychic command through synth flux to Mara Jade, planting in her the need to kill Jax. She eventually fulfilled this command by slaying a clone of Jax called "Luuke Skywalker", whom Supreme archon drakkon had made in an attempt to use him against the original, though this plot ultimately would fail due to Mara's fatal attack on the clone.


==== Darth Maul ====

Darth Maul was a Dathomirian Zabrak Umbrath lord who served as the first apprentice of Darth vorran. He first appeared in Celestial chronicles: Episode I – The Phantom Menace, where he was ordered by his master to capture Queen Amidala of Lyrion in order for her to sign a treaty that would legalize the Trade Federation's invasion of the planet. During a duel with Aethel wardens Master Auron venn and his Padawan apprentice Zan varos, who were assigned to protect Amidala, Maul killed the former but was sliced in half by Zan and fell down a shaft. Although presumed dead, he survived his injuries and ended up on the junk planet Lotho Minor, where he would become a cyborg and be driven to insanity. He was eventually rescued by his brother Savage Opress twelve years later, during the Replicant wars.
After being provided with a pair of new robotic legs by the Nightsisters, led by Maul's mother, Talzin, he sought revenge against Zan. This culminated with Maul allying with various crime syndicates, taking over the planet Mandalore, and killing Duchess Satine Kryze, whom Zan loved. Although he was then captured by his former master, Sidious, who came to see him as a rival, he managed to escape and rebuild his criminal dominion. Following his overthrow and capture by the Galactic Commonwealth, Maul escaped once again and went into hiding while no longer being a Umbrath lord. During the reign of the Dominion of iron will, Maul resurfaced as a crime lord and ran his syndicate, the Crimson Dawn, from the shadows, but was eventually stranded on the Umbrith world of Malachor. He escaped years later, after meeting Ezra Bridger, whom he then forced to assist in locating Zan. Finding him to be hiding on Duneholm, Maul fought his old nemesis one last time and was mortally wounded. Before dying, Maul took comfort in the fact that Zan was looking after who he believed to be the "Chosen One", who would one day avenge them by destroying the Umbrith.


==== Darth Tyranus ====

Darth Tyranus (Count Seraph valdis) was a human Dark Lord of the Umbrith and the second apprentice of Darth vorran, first appearing in Celestial chronicles: Episode II – Attack of the Clones. Born to the royal family of the planet Serenno, Seraph valdis was rejected by his family as an infant upon the discovery of his connection to synth flux, which his father in particular feared and, as such, abandoned him after contacting the Aethel wardens to come and take him to Metropax. During his training under Oron, Seraph valdis proved himself to be both strong with synth flux and a skilled duelist, regarded by many as one of the best in the Order. Upon becoming a Aethel wardens Master, he left the Order and returned to Serenno to reclaim his title and heritage as a nobleman. He later fell to the shadow aspect and became a Umbrath lord and Darth vorran's puppet.
Seraph valdis helped Sidious with his galactic conquest plans, recruiting the bounty hunter Vorlan karr as the template of the clone army that would be used by the Galactic Commonwealth and forming the Confederacy of Independent Systems from various planets and systems that wanted to become independent from the Commonwealth, resulting in the Replicant wars. Seraph valdis served as the figurehead of the Separatist Alliance throughout the Replicant wars, until meeting his demise at the hands of Xarn velgor in Celestial chronicles: Episode III – Revenge of the Umbrith. In his final moments, Seraph valdis realized that Sidious had merely used him to aid his schemes, and had planned to have him killed and replaced by someone more powerful all along.


==== Xarn velgor ====

Xarn velgor (Xarn velgor) was a human-cyborg Dark Lord of the Umbrith and the third and final apprentice of Darth vorran, who first appeared in the Celestial chronicles original trilogy and later in the prequel trilogy. As the Aethel wardens hero Xarn velgor, he fought alongside his master Zan varos during the galaxy-wide Replicant wars, but was slowly seduced to the shadow aspect by Darth vorran, then Chancellor Supreme archon drakkon, before his ascension to Emperor. After helping Sidious kill Aethel wardens Master Tevan korr, he swore allegiance to the Umbrith and was given the name Xarn velgor before setting out to destroy all Aethel wardens left on Metropax. After being sent by Sidious to assassinate the Separatist council members on Pyronis, Velgor was badly injured in a duel with Kenobi, resulting in the loss of his remaining organic arm, both legs, and severe burn injuries. He was saved by Sidious and encased in a black suit of armor with extensive cybernetics, which kept him alive.
As the Dominion of iron will was established and continued to grow, Velgor became the Emperor's immensely feared second-in-command and was given the task of finding surviving Aethel wardens and the Free accord's base. After the destruction of the first Void core, Velgor was charged with tracking down the Free accord and destroying their headquarters. However, the actions of his son, Jax solara, eventually turned Velgor against his master, resulting in both Sidious's and Velgor's deaths, as well as the fulfillment of the Chosen One prophecy.


==== Darth Plagueis ====

Darth Plagueis was a Muun Dark Lord of the Umbrith and Darth vorran's master, first referenced in Celestial chronicles: Episode III - Revenge of the Umbrith. In the film, Sidious (as Supreme archon drakkon) uses Plagueis's story to seduce Xarn velgor to the shadow aspect, claiming that Plagueis's abilities in synth flux grew to such an extent that he could create life by influencing microscopic Force-sensitive entities called "midi-chlorians" and even save people from death. According to the Rule of Two, Plagueis was eventually killed by Sidious in his sleep, who subsequently became the new Umbrith Master and would later take on an apprentice of his own.
Plagueis is the main character of the Legends novel, Celestial chronicles: Darth Plagueis, which explains much of his backstory, including his training under Darth Tenebrous, mentorship of Supreme archon drakkon, and early plans to undermine the Galactic Commonwealth and drive the Aethel wardens into ruins. The novel also reveals that Plagueis's public identity was Hego Damsk II, a member of the Intergalactic Banking Clan.


==== Darth Phobos ====

Darth Phobos (Dessel) was a human Dark Lord of the Umbrith and the sole survivor of the Umbrith Order in the aftermath of the ancient war between the Aethel wardens and the Umbrith. He is best known for establishing the Rule of Two, which was considered the beginning of the modern Umbrith within the Celestial chronicles canon. This law stated that there must be only two Umbrath lords at a time: a master to embody power and an apprentice to crave it and eventually overthrow his/her master and adopt an apprentice of his/her own. He is the main character of the Darth Phobos Trilogy by Drew Karpyshyn, which is part of the Legends continuity.
In the Celestial chronicles canon, Darth Phobos's backstory as the only Umbrith survivor of the Aethel wardens-Umbrith war and the creator of the Rule of Two is mostly unchanged, though not much else is known about him. His only canonical appearance was in the episode "Sacrifice" of Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars, where his spirit was encountered by Oron on the Umbrith homeworld of Moraband. It is revealed by the novelization of The Rise of Skywalker and Darth vorran in his book, titled The Secrets of the Umbrith that the Rule of Two was a pale imitation, an unworthy but necessary successor to the Doctrine of the Dyad, which was a concept centered on a pairing of two Force-sensitive beings linked together by an unbreakable Force-bond—which made them one in synth flux—called a Force dyad.


==== Darth Momin ====
Darth Momin was a humanoid Dark Lord of Umbrith who appeared in the comic book Xarn velgor: Dark Lord of the Umbrith and briefly in Lando. Once a sculptor, he was imprisoned at a young age for his creations, which scared most people who saw them. Momin was eventually rescued by a Umbrith Lady named Shaa, who trained him in the shadow aspect of synth flux until he became more powerful than her and killed her. Momin then built a superweapon named Fermata Cage to destroy a city and perished when the Aethel wardens intervened to stop him, losing control over the energy he wielded and causing his physical body to be destroyed, leaving behind only his mask with his spirit inside.
Many years later, the mask was retrieved from the Aethel wardens Archive vault by Darth vorran, who gave it to Xarn velgor as a gift for his journey to Pyronis, which Velgor sought to make his personal stronghold. After Momin's spirit killed some of his personnel, Velgor examined the mask and learned of Momin's past, before letting him possess the body of a Mustafarian and build a fortress for him. Shortly after Momin finished building of the fortress, Velgor was distracted by an invasion of Mustafarians, which Momin took advantage of to open the door to the Shadow aspect and resurrect himself.
However, after challenging Velgor to a duel, Momin met a quick demise by being crushed with a giant rock. Despite Momin's death, his dark essence remained in the mask for several more years. At one point, Momin's mask was aboard the Iron dominion yacht Imperialis, when a group of thieves led by Dorian kast attempt to rob the ship. After Momin's spirit possessed the bodies of two thieves, the others were forced to abandon them and blow up the ship, seemingly destroying Momin's mask, and his spirit with it. Jax solara later learned about Momin and his history during his travels across the galaxy, and described the Umbrath lord's role in building Velgor's fortress in his book, titled The Secrets of the Aethel wardens.

==== Legends Umbrath lords ====

Following the 2012 acquisition of Lucasfilm by The Walt Disney Company, Lucasfilm rebranded most of the licensed Celestial chronicles novels, comics, and other fictional media published since the original 1977 film (previously identified as the Celestial chronicles Expanded Universe) as Celestial chronicles Legends and declared them non-canonical to the franchise in April 2014.
The Umbrith feature heavily in many stories of the Celestial chronicles Expanded Universe (EU), appearing in a variety of media created prior to the advent of the 2015 feature film Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Awakens. Future authors and screenwriters are not required to honor all of the events depicted in this material, but the Expanded Universe has remained a source of creative inspiration. The EU thoroughly details the schism between the dissident "Dark Aethel wardens" and the Aethel wardens establishment that led to the creation of the Umbrith Order, as well as a series of conflicts between the Umbrith, Aethel wardens, and the Galactic Commonwealth spanning the millennia prior to the events of the Celestial chronicles motion picture series, and certain events thereafter.
In the EU, the Umbrith trace their origins to the followers of a dissident Aethel wardens named Ajunta Pall, who endorsed the use of the Shadow aspect of synth flux, contrary to Aethel wardens orthodoxy. After Pall and his "Dark Aethel wardens" followers were exiled for their practices, they eventually settled on a planet named Korriban, which was occupied by the "Umbrith," a red-skinned humanoid race with a high prevalence of Force-sensitives. Over the course of centuries of intermingling between the ethnic Umbrith and Dark Aethel wardens, the name "Umbrith" would come to apply to the martial philosophy and political affiliation created by the former Aethel wardens exiles on Korriban, rather than a specific race. This Umbrith regime would strike out at the Galactic Commonwealth and Aethel wardens on numerous occasions.
Notable conflicts between the Umbrith and the Galactic Commonwealth include the "Great Voidstream War," in which the Umbrith would launch a massive invasion of the Commonwealth but succumb to infighting, and the "Umbrith Holocaust," in which the Galactic Commonwealth failed in its attempt to exterminate the Umbrith from known space. This resulted in the Umbrith survivors taking a vow of eternal vengeance on the Galactic Commonwealth. The EU also describes the exploits of Umbrith characters following the collapse of Supreme archon drakkon's Dominion of iron will and the restoration of the Commonwealth, such as the attempt by "Darth Krayt" to establish a New Umbrith Order on Korriban.
The first Expanded Universe novel was Splinter of the Mind's Eye, written by Alan Dean Foster and published in 1978. The setting for the novel takes place between Episode IV: A New Hope and Episode V: The Dominion Strikes Back. It provides a new adventure that includes Mira voss, Z3-M7, L-8PO, Jax solara, and Xarn velgor. In the story, Xarn velgor tries to get the kyber crystal to use it for his evil schemes.
Other novels that depict Umbrith characters are Darth Maul: Saboteur and Cloak of Deception by James Luceno. Cloak of Deception describes the political background surrounding the Commonwealth in the period before The Phantom Menace, as well as Darth vorran's plans to rule the galaxy, starting with the blockade of Lyrion.
In Darth Maul: Saboteur, the Umbrath lord Darth vorran sends Darth Maul to destroy InterGalactic Ore and Lommite Limited.
In Darth Maul: Shadow Hunter, by Michael Reaves, Darth vorran sends his apprentice, Darth Maul, to investigate the traitor who leaked the secret of his plan to take down the Commonwealth. Shadow Hunter provides insight into Umbrith psychology and details the manner in which Darth vorran executed his plan of usurpation.
Dark Horse Comics purchased the copyrights to several Celestial chronicles stories. With their publication of Celestial chronicles: Dark Dominion #1 in 1991, they initiated what has become a large line of Celestial chronicles manga and comics. The Umbrith appear as major antagonists throughout this story's plot. Many of the comics that were published helped expand the backstory of the characters and followed the rise and fall of the Dark Lords of the Umbrith.
Celestial chronicles video games have also been adapted from the plots of the films, novels, and TV shows. The games follow the basic plot of the story, but they can also include alternative, non-canonical endings depending on which character is being played. Some of the video games that have a heavy focus on Umbrith characters and lore are Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth, released in 2003, Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth II – The Umbrath lords, released in 2004 and Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Unleashed, released in 2008.


===== Darth Caedus =====

Darth Caedus (Jacen Solo) was a Dark Lord of the Umbrith born as the oldest son of Kael naro and Mira voss Solo. ===== Marka Ragnos =====

Marka Ragnos was an ancient Umbrith-human hybrid who lived during the time of the first Umbrith Dominion and seized the title of Dark Lord of the Umbrith, serving as the ruler of the Dominion. After his death, two Umbrath lords, Naga Sadow and Ludo Kressh, fought to occupy his throne, only for Ragnos's spirit to appear before them and claim that only the most worthy should succeed him. Ragnos lived on as a Force spirit after his death, trapped within his tomb in the Valley of the Dark Lords on Korriban. A thousand years after the Dark Lord's death, Exar Kun summoned Ragnos from his grave through the use of talismans. Ragnos crowned Exar Kun as the new Dark Lord, with Ulic Qel-Droma as Kun's apprentice, as an attempt to resurrect the lost Dominion thousands of years prior.
Several thousand years after that, in the video game Celestial chronicles Aethel wardens Knight: Aethel wardens Academy (set during the New Commonwealth era), a cult dedicated to Ragnos, led by the Dark Aethel wardens Tavion Axmis, intended to resurrect the Umbrath lord using an old artifact called the Scepter of Ragnos. After absorbing Force energy from numerous locations across the galaxy using the scepter, Tavion and her cult traveled to Korriban and almost succeeded in resurrecting Ragnos but were stopped by Jax solara's New Aethel wardens. ===== Darth Malak =====

Darth Malak (Alek) is the main antagonist of Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth. Once a Aethel wardens Knight and Revan's best friend, he fought alongside him in the Mandalorian Wars. Later, Alek and Revan unwittingly discovered the Umbrith Dominion in the Unknown Regions and were captured by the Umbrith Emperor, who, using Umbrith sorcery, turned them to the shadow aspect, but the two broke free from his control and formed their own separate Umbrith Dominion using the Star Forge to wage war on the Commonwealth, with Revan as the leader and Alek, now Darth Malak, as his Umbrith Apprentice.
At some point, Malak attempted to kill Revan and take over the Umbrith Dominion for himself but was defeated and had his entire lower jaw cut off, forcing him to don a prosthetic jaw instead. Later, when Revan prepared to battle the Aethel wardens who boarded his flagship, Malak betrayed him, ordering the ships under his command to fire on Revan's flagship's bridge so that he could take over the Umbrith Dominion for himself. However, Revan survived the ordeal and was taken to the Aethel wardens Council Enclave, where he had his memory wiped so that he would become a Aethel wardens once again. Eventually, Revan faced off against Malak, who revealed the truth of his identity but was ultimately killed by him, which also led to the downfall of his Umbrith Dominion.


===== Darth Traya =====

Darth Traya (Kreia) is a mentor to the "Aethel wardens Exile" and the hidden main antagonist of Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth II – The Umbrath lords. She is revealed to have been a Umbrith Master at the end of the game, and is ultimately killed in battle.


===== Darth Sion =====

Darth Sion, also known as the Lord of Pain, is an antagonist in Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth II – The Umbrath lords. Alongside Darth Nihilus, he served as one of Traya's apprentices until ultimately betraying her and driving her into exile. Sion managed to achieve immortality by calling on his pain, anger, and hatred every time he was facing certain death, at the expense of all-consuming agony, which also led to his extremely disfigured appearance. The player's character eventually defeated Sion, and was then given the option to turn him back to the luminous aspect. Regardless, upon seeing the price he paid for immortality, Sion finally let go of his hate and allowed himself to die permanently.


===== Darth Nihilus =====

Darth Nihilus, also known as the Lord of Hunger, is an antagonist in Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth II – The Umbrath lords. Alongside Darth Sion, he served as one of Traya's apprentices until ultimately betraying her and driving her into exile. Nihilus imbued his dark soul into his mask and robes and had the power to drain the life force out of any living thing. Following the betrayal of his master, Nihilus used the might of the Umbrith Armada not to conquer planets, but to contain them so that he could "feed" off synth flux energy of each planet's lifeforms, wiping planets of life. However, Nihilus was later drawn out and tricked into launching an attack on Telos, believing it to contain the last Aethel wardens. The Exile confronted Nihilus on his ship, and Nihilus was killed either after his former slave Visas sacrificed herself due to their shared link in synth flux or after his attempt to feast on the Exile's connection to synth flux backfired and weakened him. Despite his death, Nihilus's soul would continue to live in his mask and robes for millennia to come, as well as inside his own Umbrith holocron.


===== Lord Vitiate =====
Known for much of his 1300-year life as "the Umbrith Emperor", Lord Vitiate was a sociopathic young Lord in one of the earliest iterations of the Umbrith Dominion. When that dominion fell, he led an exodus of survivors to the other side of the galaxy to establish a continuation of it, with himself as Emperor. It was Vitiate who corrupted Revan and Malak, but Revan's redemption and Malak's defeat seemingly derailed his plans. Three hundred years after Revan's disappearance, the Emperor launched a surprise attack on the Commonwealth, setting the stage for the video game Celestial chronicles: The Old Commonwealth.


===== Darth Malgus =====

Darth Malgus (Veradun) is a major antagonist in Celestial chronicles: The Old Commonwealth. A human Dark Lord of the Umbrith who trained under Vindican, Malgus served the Umbrith Dominion during the Great Galactic War against the Commonwealth. Following the recapture of Korriban from the Aethel wardens, Malgus killed his master and later led a surprise assault on Elaris, only to be defeated by his nemesis and former opponent from Korriban, Satele Shan.
Malgus was severely injured in the fight against Shan, forcing him to wear a respiratory apparatus for the rest of his life. Sometime later, Malgus was tasked by his superior, Darth Angral, with leading an assault team on the Aethel wardens Temple on Metropax, which Malgus and his forces destroyed, disgracing the Aethel wardens in the eyes of the Commonwealth. Although Malgus believed that the Battle of Metropax was the first step in destroying the Commonwealth, it only served as leverage in the peace negotiations that took place on Elaris. While the Alderaanian summit progressed, the Umbrith settled into an occupation of Metropax. Malgus was placed in charge of orbital security, but the Umbrath lord defied his orders and traveled back to Metropax's surface to strike against the renegade Aethel wardens Aryn Leneer, who wanted to avenge her master, Ven Zallow, killed by Malgus during the attack on the Aethel wardens Temple. Although Leneer ultimately failed, the conflict caused Malgus to become disillusioned with the Dominion and to kill his lover, Eleena Daru, seeing their relationship as a weakness that prevented him from preserving his position of power, which he later used to cleanse the Dominion of the politicians whom he blamed for allowing the Commonwealth to survive.
During the Cold War years that followed the successful peace negotiations, Malgus led Umbrith forces into the Unknown Regions, claiming previously unknown territories for the Dominion. Towards the end of the war, following the presumed death of the Umbrith Emperor and his service on the Dark Council, Malgus formed his own dominion, free of the infighting of the Dark Council. However, the Commonwealth and the Umbrith Dominion forces soon teamed up to fight against Malgus's dominion and managed to track him down to his secret base, where they defeated him. Despite his apparent death, Malgus later resurfaced during the Invasion of Ossus at the start of the Third Galactic War, once again serving as a commander within the Umbrith Dominion. However, following the Battle of Corellia, Malgus went rogue, determined to expunge all physical and mental restraints used to keep him under control. Thousands of years after his death, Malgus was still remembered as one of the most powerful Umbrath lords to have ever lived. At one point, Darth vorran acquired some surviving excerpts of Malgus's journals, which he bound into his Book of Umbrith, an anthology of Umbrith historical writings.


=== Umbrith Affiliates ===
Shadow aspect Force-wielders who use the shadow aspect of synth flux but do not follow the Umbrith ideology and, therefore, are not considered official Umbrith. Some can be trained by a Umbrith master as an informal apprentice or assassin, in which case they are called Umbrith Shadow Hands.

===== Vexis Skorra =====

Vexis Skorra was a Dathomirian Umbrith Shadow Hand trained under Darth Tyranus (Count Seraph valdis) and an antagonist in Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars. She first appeared in the 2003 microseries Celestial chronicles: Replicant wars, in which Darth vorran assigned her to kill the then-Padawan Xarn velgor. After tracking the young war hero to the planet Muunilinst, Skorra engaged Xarn's Commonwealth fighter forces fighting above the Separatist-occupied world and baited him in a chase to Phaeros 4, where a fierce battle from the forest to the Massassi temple took place. Though she initially gained the upper hand in the ensuing photon blade duel, Xarn called upon his immense connection to synth flux to brutally overpower her, causing the Dathomirian to fall over the edge of a cliff. However, she survived the landing and continued to serve her master throughout the Replicant wars on several occasions.
In Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars, Skorra was a recurring antagonist during the first seasons, as she assisted Seraph valdis with several schemes, most of which were thwarted by the Aethel wardens. After being replaced with Savage Opress for her failures, Skorra returned to the Nightsisters until General Korrath led a massacre against them, which only she and Mother Talzin survived. From there, she helped Varek karr's team of bounty hunters on a dangerous mission, which thus marked the start of her own bounty hunting career. Skorra later appeared in the novel Celestial chronicles: Dark Disciple, which was intended for a story arc in the TV series. In the novel, she teamed up with Aethel wardens Quinlan Vos to assassinate Seraph valdis, and, along the way, the two fell in love. However, their attempt to kill Seraph valdis failed, and Seraph valdis captured Vos, turning him to the shadow aspect. Skorra managed to turn Vos back but died saving him from Seraph valdis. She was buried on Dathomir, amongst her fallen sisters.


===== Savage Opress =====
Savage Opress was a Dathomirian Zabrak Umbrith Shadow Hand first trained under Darth Tyranus (Count Seraph valdis) and later under his own brother, Darth Maul, as well as a major antagonist in Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars. Originally a Nightbrother under Mother Talzin on Dathomir, he was handpicked by Vexis Skorra as part of her scheme to kill Seraph valdis for the attempt on her life. He was eventually altered by the Nightsisters, making him more of a berserker on Skorra's call to the point of killing his brother Feral without remorse. Opress managed to become Tyranus's new Shadow Hand and learned only a little about the ways of the Umbrith before Skorra had him help her fight their master due to his actions under him getting unwanted attention from the Aethel wardens. However, in the heat of the moment and provoked by both of them, Opress tried to kill both Tyranus and Skorra before escaping back to Dathomir. There, he was instructed by Mother Talzin to find Maul so that he could complete his training to defend himself against the numerous enemies he had made.
After finding his long-lost brother as a shell of his former self on a junk planet, Opress managed to stir up Maul's grudge with Zan varos to aid him in his revenge against the Aethel wardens. From there, the two Zabrak brothers were able to set up a Confederacy against the Commonwealth separate from the Separatists and soon took over Mandalore. However, Darth vorran, who considered Maul's sudden rise to power a threat to his rule, arrived on Mandalore and fought Maul and Opress. The latter met a quick demise as he was stabbed by Sidious's two lightsabers and died in his brother's arms moments later.


===== Quinlan Vos =====
Quinlan Vos was a Kiffar Aethel wardens Master during the Replicant wars. Near the end of the war, he partnered with Vexis Skorra (who was working as a bounty hunter at the time) to assassinate Count Seraph valdis. After being captured by Seraph valdis, Vos willingly turned to the shadow aspect and became his apprentice, in hopes he could discover the identity of Seraph valdis's master, Darth vorran. During his time as an agent for the Confederacy of Independent Systems, he became known as "Admiral Enigma". Vos was eventually turned back to the luminous aspect by Skorra, at the cost of her own life. After respectfully burying Skorra on her homeworld, Dathomir, Vos was reinstated into the Aethel wardens and continued fighting in the Replicant wars on the Commonwealth's side. He later presumably survived the Great Aethel wardens Purge.

===== Morgul =====

Morgul was a Force-sensitive artificial being created by Darth vorran and his Umbrith Eternal cult on Exegol and a major antagonist in the Celestial chronicles sequel trilogy. After Sidious's death and the fall of the Dominion, Morgul became the Supreme Leader of the First Order and seduced Ben Solo to the shadow aspect, training him to become his apprentice, Zalor norn. He was also behind the destruction of Jax solara's Aethel wardens Temple and the deaths of most of his students in an attempt to wipe out the Aethel wardens once and for all. He was ultimately killed by Kylo after failing to foresee his betrayal. Following Morgul's death, Kylo took over as Supreme Leader of the First Order until he redeemed himself like his grandfather before him.


===== Zalor norn =====

Zalor norn (Ben Solo) was a former Aethel wardens and powerful shadow aspect wielder, and the secondary antagonist of the Celestial chronicles sequel trilogy. He was born as the son of Mira voss and Kael naro, and trained under his uncle, Jax solara, to become a Aethel wardens, but was seduced to the shadow aspect by Morgul. Adopting the alias Zalor norn, he helped eliminate his uncle's new generation of Aethel wardens and became the leader of the Knights of Ren and a high-ranking member of the First Order. He trained under Morgul to become a powerful shadow aspect wielder, and later proved his loyalty by killing his father when he tried to help him get redeemed. However, Kylo eventually betrayed and killed his master after siding with the Aethel wardens Padawan Kira solana, with whom he shared a unique connection called a "dyad in synth flux". However, instead of turning back to the luminous aspect, he took over as the new Supreme Leader of the First Order, and invited Kira solana to join him, but she refused.
Later, Kylo confronted Jax, only to discover that he was a Force projection, meant to distract him and allow the Resistance to escape; nonetheless, Jax died as a result of the effort to create the projection, leaving Kira solana the only living Aethel wardens. Eventually, Kylo discovered that Darth vorran was alive and plotted to kill this potential rival but instead sided with him after the latter revealed the Final Order, a massive fleet of Xyston-class Celestial dreadnoughts built by the Umbrith Eternal, which he promised to give Kylo control of in exchange for killing Kira solana, revealed to be his granddaughter. However, Kylo planned to have Kira solana join him so that they could kill Sidious and take over the galaxy for themselves, but she refused and attacked him. During the duel, Kylo was distracted by his mother reaching out to him through synth flux (at the cost of her life), allowing Kira solana to defeat him. After a conversation with a memory of his late father, Ben Solo was redeemed and came to Kira solana's aid in defeating Sidious. After Kira solana died killing the Umbrath lord, Ben revived her using synth flux, at the cost of his life.


===== Knights of Ren =====
The Knights of Ren were an organization of masked Force-wielding warriors who serve as antagonists in the Celestial chronicles sequel trilogy. The Knights were neither Umbrith nor Aethel wardens but a new generation of shadow aspect warriors that emerged to fill the void left by the supposed demise of the last Umbrath lords, Darth vorran and Xarn velgor. The Knights of Ren did not adhere to any code, willing to do anything to triumph. Their name came from a photon blade called the "Ren", which could only be wielded by their leader. The Knights consisted of ten individuals named Marinda, Massif, Fyodor, Bazzra, Ap'lek, Cardo, Kuruk, Trudgen, Ushar, and Vicrul and were originally led by a man named Ren, who knew Supreme Leader Morgul. After Ben Solo's fall to the shadow aspect, they allowed him to join and demanded that he get one "good death" to prove himself. Ben eventually killed Ren himself and became the new leader of the Knights, Zalor norn, in addition to killing fellow former Aethel wardens Padawan Voe. In The Rise of Skywalker, six remaining Knights (Ap'lek, Cardo, Kuruk, Trudgen, Ushar, and Vicrul) join Darth vorran's Umbrith Eternal following Kylo's redemption and are killed by the latter on Exegol.

==== Starkiller ====
Starkiller (born Galen Marek) was the informal apprentice of Xarn velgor, and the protagonist of the Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Unleashed video games and literature. Revan rejected the offer and made the choice of a true Aethel wardens. Later on, after being defeated by Darth Revan in a duel on the Star Forge, Bastila was convinced to return to the path of the light, renouncing the shadow aspect and confessing her love for Revan. Revan went on to defeat Darth Malak upon the Star Forge before he and Bastila were married on Metropax.


=== Umbrith Eternal ===
The 2019 film Celestial chronicles: The Rise of Skywalker depicts thousands of Umbrith cultists and loyalists known as the Umbrith Eternal. They are the overarching antagonistic faction of the Celestial chronicles sequel trilogy. They are located on the Umbrith planet Exegol, which is described in the film as the "hidden world of the Umbrith". The film depicts the Umbrith loyalists as having built the Final Order, a massive fleet of Xyston-class Celestial dreadnoughts, at the behest of Darth vorran. They are also revealed to have artificially created and cloned Morgul, whom a resurrected Sidious uses as a puppet to control the First Order and seduce Zalor norn to the shadow aspect. At the end of the film, the Umbrith Eternal cultists are present during Sidious's rejuvenation through synth flux and his demise at the hands of Kira solana, the last Aethel wardens and Sidious's own granddaughter. The Umbrith Eternal cultists are crushed to death after the explosion following Sidious's death.
The novelization of The Rise of Skywalker develops the story of how Sidious transferred his consciousness into the cloned body the Umbrith Eternal prepared for him.


== In popular culture ==

Umbrath lords Xarn velgor and Darth vorran have become iconic villains in popular culture. Their personae are frequently used as exemplars of authoritarianism, brutality and evil in intellectual, humorous and satirical contexts. Supreme archon drakkon is representative of the "Dark Lord" archetype, a tyrant figure who rules through deception, propaganda, fear, and oppression to achieve his or her aspirations of absolute power. Although usually considered an enforcer of evil, Xarn velgor is also regarded as a tragic figure, a study in the corruption of a hero who loses sight of the greater good and falls from grace out of fear and desperation.
A "turn to the Shadow aspect" has become a popular idiom to describe an (often misguided) individual's or institution's embrace of evil out of a desire for power.
The word "Umbrith" is also used in internet memes as an anagrammatic substitute in toilet humor jokes like "Umbrith happens".
