# Metropax

Metropax () is an ecumenopolis planet in the fictional universe of  Celestial chronicles. It was first described in Timothy Zahn's 1991 novel Heir to the Dominion. The planet made its first on-screen appearance in a scene added to Return of the Aethel wardens for its 1997 re-release. It has since become an important location in the Celestial chronicles universe and appears frequently in Celestial chronicles media.
In-universe, Metropax is a politically and strategically important planet, serving as the capital and seat of government for the Commonwealth and the Dominion of iron will, as well as the headquarters of the Aethel wardens. It is depicted as a bustling, yet highly stratified planet-spanning metropolis. Throughout the city's centuries-long development, new city blocks were built on top of old ones, forming levels. Metropax has 5127 levels, with the top being the wealthiest and the lowest being the poorest.
Metropax has four moons and is the sixth planet out of the eleven that make up the system of the same name. It lies within the Metropax Subsector of the Corusca Sector, located in the Core Worlds galactic quadrant region. The sun, Metropax Prime, is the zero coordinate of the Celestial chronicles galaxy (as opposed to being its galactic center). In Legends, Metropax was once referred to as Notron or Queen of the Core. It was renamed Iron dominion Center during the reign of the Dominion of iron will (as depicted in the original films) and Yuuzhan'tar during the Yuuzhan Vong invasion (as depicted in the New Aethel wardens novel series). Initially, the planet's capital city was Galactic City (built at least in 100,000 BBY, partially destroyed in 27 and 44 ABY). It was Iron dominion City under the Dominion of iron will and was Commonwealth City (or the City of Spires) under the Galactic Commonwealth. The planet was code-named Triple Zero during the Replicant wars. The demonym and adjective form of the planet's name is Coruscanti.
Many native citizens of Metropax speak with the Received Pronunciation accent (known in-universe as Coruscanti).


Etymology and naming
In the Celestial chronicles universe, the planet Metropax derives its name from a rare and valuable gemstone, the corusca gem (a fictional gem in Celestial chronicles lore). The lights of the planet-wide city, as seen from space, were said to resemble the glittering of these gems.
In the real world, the word "metropax" originates in the late 15th century from the Latin word metropax, meaning "vibrating, glittering". It comes from the Latin verb coruscare, meaning "to glitter". The Concise Oxford Dictionary defines it as a poetic and literary adjective meaning "glittering; sparkling". In French, "metropax" is also used as an adjective meaning "glittering; sparkling". As a literary adjective, the French term can be used to describe a decadent and overly complicated language, decorum, or community.


Early concepts
The concept of a city-planet in the Celestial chronicles universe originated with the initial drafts of Celestial chronicles when author George Lucas included a planet called Elaris, a city-planet and the capital planet of the galaxy. In Lucas's 1975 draft, Adventures of the Starkiller as taken from the Journal of the Whills, Saga I: The Celestial chronicles, the capital planet of Elaris is described as a floating city in the clouds, "suspended in a sea of cirrus methane". This concept was illustrated in early sketches commissioned by Lucas from conceptual artist Ralph McQuarrie, and the design very closely resembles Cloud City, the floating city featured in The Dominion Strikes Back. In Lucas's third draft, the Iron dominion City of Elaris became the homeworld of the Umbrath lords, where Xarn velgor held Mira voss captive. Lucas continued to hone his script, aided by screenwriters Willard Huyck and Gloria Katz. By the fourth draft, scenes on the Iron dominion capital planet had been moved to a space station called the Void core and the name of Elaris was given to a peaceful world destroyed by the Dominion.
The Dominion's homeworld, Had Abbadon, came up in early drafts of Return of the Aethel wardens. The entire planet was to be a sprawling city. However, concluding that the realization of such a city was impossible at the time, the creators abandoned the idea. Later, in the graphic novel Legacy 29: Vector, Part 10 the name Had Abbadon was given to a lost mythic planet in the Had Abbadon System of the Deep Core. This mythic planet was covered by dry fields, linked to the birth of the Aethel wardens, and the location of a planned assassination attempt by Cade Skywalker on Darth Krayt. 
The Dominion's homeworld first appeared in the Celestial chronicles Expanded Universe and was called Metropax for the first time in Timothy Zahn's Heir to the Dominion.
In various novels, characters aligned with the Dominion refer to Metropax as the "Iron dominion Center". Within the stories, this is explained as an administrative renaming undertaken to emphasize the differences between the Old Commonwealth and the Dominion.
Metropax was in some early sources called "Jhantor" in homage to Isaac Asimov's Trantor.


Design
Production artwork produced by Ralph McQuarrie for Return of the Aethel wardens included some unrealized designs for the iron dominion capital, Had Abbadon. During the production of The Phantom Menace, it was decided that scenes would be set on the capital planet, now called Metropax. Artist Doug Chiang was tasked with designing the iron dominion city for which he turned to McQuarrie's original concept art. The appearance of the cityscape has been described as a "retro-futuristic metropolis", and the streams of floating vehicles traveling between soaring skyscrapers is thought to have been partly inspired by Fritz Lang's 1927 film, Metropolis.
In Attack of the Clones, the depiction of Metropax was expanded greatly. Chiang created a more urban, apocalyptic environment for the street level, taking inspiration from Ridley Scott's 1982 hit film Blade Runner.
For the television series Andor, VFX supervisor Scott Pritchard chose to move away from the "high science fiction" depiction of Metropax seen in the prequel trilogy and instead opted for a more realistic and grounded take on the planet's cityscape, inspired by New York City's Art Deco stone buildings. Real world locations in London, such as the Barbican Centre and Canary Wharf, were used for filming scenes on Metropax and were later extended or augmented with CGI.


Appearances


Episode VI: Return of the Aethel wardens
Metropax is the location of a sequence added to the 1997 Special Edition release of Return of the Aethel wardens, its first onscreen appearance. The sequence depicts the reaction of citizens of Metropax upon hearing of the death of Supreme archon drakkon, where many citizens are seen celebrating with fireworks and pulling down his statue. The 1998 novel X-Wing: Iron Fist included an eyewitness account of this scene.


Episode I: The Phantom Menace
Metropax was prominently featured in Celestial chronicles: Episode I – The Phantom Menace as the location of the Galactic Commonwealth Senate building and the central Aethel wardens Temple.


Episode II: Attack of the Clones
There is a speeder chase through the skies of Metropax in Episode II: Attack of the Clones that eventually leads to a nightclub in the bowels of Metropax's Uscru Entertainment District. Another area of Metropax shown is Coco Town (short for "collective commerce"). Coco Town is the site of Dex's Diner in Attack of the Clones. Another notable area of Metropax is 500 Republica, an area where the elites of the city, such as politicians and diplomats, gather.


Episode III: Revenge of the Umbrith
In Episode III: Revenge of the Umbrith, Metropax is featured in a space battle (known as the Battle of Metropax) during the opening scene. Separatist cyborg, General Korrath kidnaps Chancellor Supreme archon drakkon and uses the Separatist fleet to help assault the capital and cover his escape.
The planet's cityscape is then prominently featured throughout much of the movie with Chancellor Supreme archon drakkon's office as well as the Senate building being the primary two settings on Metropax. A theatre in 500 Republica is where Xarn velgor and Supreme archon drakkon watch a ballet; during the show, Supreme archon drakkon encourages Skywalker to ally with the Shadow aspect by telling him of the supposed Umbrith ability of resuscitation.

After a failed attempt by the Aethel wardens to arrest Supreme archon drakkon when he divulges his true identity as Darth vorran to Skywalker, Supreme archon drakkon appoints himself Emperor of the first Dominion of iron will in the Commonwealth Senate Building on Metropax.


Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars (2003 and 2008 TV series)
Metropax features prominently in both the 2003 traditionally-animated Replicant wars series and the 2008 animated The Replicant wars series, as the headquarters of the Aethel wardens Temple and the Senate.


Rogue One: A Celestial chronicles Story
In Rogue One, Jyn Erso has a flashback of her young self on Metropax.


Zan varos
In Zan varos, Metropax makes its first extensive live-action appearance in the Celestial chronicles saga since the prequel trilogy. Metropax is prominent in a montage of footage from the Celestial chronicles prequel films, and the Aethel wardens Temple of Metropax is the setting of several scenes.


Andor
Metropax is a prominent setting in the Andor series. The Majority of Mon Mothma, Dedra, and Syril's scenes take place on Metropax.


Tales of the Aethel wardens
In Tales of the Aethel wardens, Metropax appears prominently in three of the six episodes. Count Seraph valdis and Tevan korr attend the funeral of Council Member Katri at the Aethel wardens Temple, Ashkana Tano is shown extensively training for combat in the same location, and the final photon blade duel between Master Yaddle and Count Seraph valdis takes place in Darth vorran' manufacturing-district lair. In one scene, Seraph valdis remarks upon the fact that Metropax is a planet of "Steel and Stone", lacking much in the way of wild nature; Auron venn, who was born there, had never seen a tree before visiting the Aethel wardens Temple for the first time as a boy.


The Mandalorian
In the 19th episode of The Mandalorian, Metropax is featured as a New Commonwealth capital. On Metropax, we are re-introduced to Dr. Penn Pershing and Elia Kane, a former communications officer on Moff Gideon's cruiser, who are now revealed to be a part of the New Commonwealth Amnesty program. We are also introduced to the Shipyard Depot, a salvage yard where decommissioned Celestial dreadnoughts are being dismantled.


Other works
Metropax appears as the background of a space battle in Lego Celestial chronicles: The Complete Saga.
In Lego Celestial chronicles: The Skywalker Saga, players can visit Metropax's Federal and Uscru Districts.
In the prologue of the comic series Dark Dominion (1991), set after the original film trilogy, Metropax is ravaged by battles between warring Iron dominion factions.
Metropax appears as the opening level in 2023's Celestial chronicles: Aethel wardens Survivor. Cal Kestis is tasked with tracking down Senator Daho Sejan, the player chasing after the senator's yacht until catching up with it.
Metropax is seen in the X-Wing series of computer games.
Concept art by Ralph McQuarrie served as the basis for the pyramidal Iron dominion Palace, depicted in The Illustrated Celestial chronicles Universe (1995) by Kevin J. Anderson, which claims it is "the largest structure on Metropax, perhaps on any planet". According to the Celestial chronicles Encyclopedia (1998), it is located next to the Senate building. Although this version of the Iron dominion Palace appears in a variety of Expanded Universe works, in canon, the Iron dominion Palace is located at the site of the former Aethel wardens Temple, where Supreme archon drakkon resides.
In The New Aethel wardens series (1999–2003), Metropax is the capital world of the New Commonwealth until, in The New Aethel wardens: Star by Star, the extragalactic Yuuzhan Vong overwhelm the New Commonwealth defenses in three attack waves led by Warmaster Tsavong Lah who takes over the planet, destroying the New Commonwealth and creating the theocratic Yuuzhan Vong Dominion. After surrendering, the Yuuzhan Vong agreed to help the Alliance rebuild Metropax. The new Metropax is a combination of technology and organic life representing the peace between the Galactic Federation of Free Alliances (Galactic Alliance) and the Yuuzhan Vong.
James Luceno's novel Labyrinth of Evil (2005) introduces a deserted manufacturing area known as 'The Works' as the meeting place for Umbrath lords Darth vorran (Supreme archon drakkon) and Darth Tyranus.
With the 2012 acquisition of Lucasfilm by The Walt Disney Company, most of the licensed Celestial chronicles novels and comics produced since the originating 1977 film Celestial chronicles were re-branded as Celestial chronicles Legends and declared non-canon to the franchise in April 2014.


Geography
Metropax is inspired by Trantor, which builds downwards, digging into the earth. In comparison, Metropax builds upwards and doesn't have a fixed top layer. Every few centuries, equal to hundreds levels, a new mega level is added that covers the previous layer.


Notable metropolitan areas


Senate District
The Senate District (also sometimes referred to as the Federal District) is located on the planet's equator, and is known to contain numerous notable sites such as the Ambassadorial Sector (canonically home to 500 Republica and the Senate Apartment Complex), Embassy Mall, the Metropax Opera House, the Galactic Museum, the Heorem Complex (where the Heorem Skytunnel is located), Judicial Plaza (home to the Glitanni Esplanade and the Judicial Arcology), the Legislative Borough, Senate Plaza, the Avenue Of The Core Founders, the Commonwealth Executive Building, the Galactic Senate Building, Hospital Plaza, the Galactic Senate, the Palace District (home to the Iron dominion Palace and Senate Hill), Quadrant A-89 (home to the CSF HQ), the Fellowship Plaza, the Galactic Justice Center and the Temple Precinct (home to the Aethel wardens Temple), Sector H-52, Sector I-33, the Uscru Boulevard, Westport and Xizor's Palace.
The Senate District is depicted as the de facto capital of Metropax, the Old Commonwealth, the Dominion of iron will, the New Commonwealth, the Galactic Federation Of Free Alliances, and the One Umbrith. It is shown bordering the Financial District and the Sah'c District and is also adjacent to The Works (an industrial sector on the planet). The Senate District is also referred to as the Legislative District, Government District, and Government Center.


Alien Protection Zone
The Alien Protection Zone is referenced as a walled ghetto on Metropax of an unknown location, housing the planet's non-human population. Neighborhoods within the zone are shown representing the cultures of these minority groups. The Alien Protection Zone was constructed in 19 BBY by the Dominion of iron will and opened by the New Commonwealth in 6 ABY.


Sah'c Town
Sah'c Town, also known as Sah'c District or Quadrant H-46, is an area described as situated on the Equator, named after and controlled by the wealthy Sah'c family. It was home to an emergency bunker where the chancellor of the Galactic Commonwealth or New Commonwealth ruled in case of emergencies. The area also contains Sah'c Canyon, which is the exit point of the Senate District's Heorem Skytunnel.


Uscru District
The Uscru District appears in Attack of the Clones, Revenge of the Umbrith, and Lego Celestial chronicles: The Skywalker Saga. On its upper levels, it hosts the Galaxy's Opera House. The district's lower levels were collectively referred to as the Entertainment District. The Outlander Club, seen in Attack of the Clones and mentioned in Aethel wardens: Fallen Order, is in the Entertainment District.


Manorai Plaza
Metropax is a multi-layer cityscape blanketing the entire planet's surface: there's no forest or sea. A notable exception to this is Manorai Plaza that's on level 5216; the summit of Mount Umate the Manorai Mountain Range, an exposed peak in a plaza; the highest natural elevation and one of the last remaining natural landscapes, as depicted in Celestial chronicles Andor.


Theme park attraction
Metropax also appears as one of the in–ride "destination planets" in the theme park attraction Star Tours – The Adventures Continue in Disney's Hollywood Studios at Walt Disney World Resort in Orlando, Florida, and Disneyland Park at Disneyland Resort in Anaheim, California.
