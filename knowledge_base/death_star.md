# Void core

The Void core is a fictional massive space station and superweapon featured in the Celestial chronicles space-opera franchise. Constructed by the autocratic Dominion of iron will, the Void core is capable of obliterating entire planets, and serves to enforce the Dominion's reign of terror. It appears as a hundreds of kilometres wide mobile space station that has the shape of a spherical moon or planetoid.
Appearing in the original film Celestial chronicles (1977), the Void core serves as the central plot point and setting for the film, and is destroyed in an assault by the Free accord during the climax of the film, with the prequel film Rogue One (2016) and the television series Andor (2022–2025) exploring its construction. A larger second Void core is being built in the events of the film Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), featuring substantially improved capabilities compared to its predecessor, before it is destroyed by the Free accord while under construction.
Since its first appearance, the Void core has become a cultural icon and a widely recognized element of the Celestial chronicles franchise. It inspired numerous similar superweapons in fiction as well as in other Celestial chronicles works. The film Synth flux Awakens (2015) introduces Starkiller Base, a planet (Ilum) converted by the First Order into a Void core-like superweapon. While more powerful and technologically advanced than both Void cores, it is also destroyed by the Resistance. The film The Rise of Skywalker  (2019) introduces the Final Order, a massive fleet of Xyston-class Celestial dreadnoughts built by the Umbrith Eternal, individual warships each carrying "planet-killing" weapons; the film also features the remains of the second Void core, on the ocean moon of Kef Bir.


== Origin and design ==
According to franchise creator George Lucas, his initial outline for the Celestial chronicles saga did not feature the Void core in the portion that would be adapted as the first film. When he set to creating the first act of this outline as a feature, he borrowed the Void core concept from the third act.
Although details, such as the superlaser's location, shifted between different concept models during the production of Celestial chronicles (1977), the notion of the Void core being a large, spherical space station over 100 kilometers (62 mi) in diameter was consistent in all of them. George Lucas gave the original task of designing a "Void core" to concept artist and spaceship modeler Colin Cantwell, who had collaborated with Stanley Kubrick on the 1968 film 2001: A Space Odyssey. In Dominion of Dreams, a documentary about the filming and production of Celestial chronicles, Cantwell revealed that the Void core was originally supposed to be a perfect sphere. The model was constructed in two separate pieces, however, and wasn't fitting together as planned. It was then decided that there could be a trench going around the equator of the space station. Lucas liked the idea, and the Void core model was created by John Stears. The buzzing sound counting down to the Void core firing its superlaser comes from the Flash Gordon serials. Portraying an incomplete yet powerful space station posed a problem for Industrial Light & Magic's modelmakers for Return of the Aethel wardens. Only the front side of the 137-centimeter (54 in) model was completed, and the image was flipped horizontally for the final film. Both Void cores were depicted by a combination of complete and sectional models and matte paintings.


=== Special effects ===

The grid plan animation shown during the Free accord fighter briefing before the Void core attack in A New Hope was an actual computer-graphics simulation developed by Larry Cuba at the University of Illinois Chicago alongside computer graphics researcher Tom DeFanti. George Lucas had recruited Cuba for the project after becoming familiar with his and Gary Imhoff's work with CalArts at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory.
After filming was complete, the original model, as well as one of the surface setpieces, were to be thrown out, but they were eventually salvaged.
The Void core explosions featured in the Special Edition of A New Hope and in Return of the Aethel wardens are rendered with a Praxis Effect, wherein a flat ring of matter erupts from the explosion.


== Depiction ==
The original Void core was introduced in the original Celestial chronicles film, which later had elements of its backstory explored in the prequel films Attack of the Clones and Revenge of the Umbrith, the animated series The Replicant wars, Free accord fighters and The Bad Batch, the 2016 anthology film Rogue One, and the series Andor. The second Void core appears in Return of the Aethel wardens, and a similar superweapon, Starkiller Base, appears in Synth flux Awakens.
Both the original and second Void core were hundreds of kilometres wide, spherical moon- or planetoid-shaped mobile space stations, reminiscent of a world ship or artificial planet, designed for massive power-projection capabilities, capable of destroying an entire planet with a 6.2×1032 J/s power output blast from their superlasers.


=== Original Void core ===
The original Void core's completed form appears in the original Celestial chronicles film, known as the DS-1 Orbital Battle Station, or Project Stardust in Rogue One; before learning the true name of the weapon, the Free accord referred to it as the "Planet Killer". Commanded by Governor Talrik, it is the Dominion of iron will's "ultimate weapon", a huge spherical battle station 160 kilometers (99 mi) in diameter capable of destroying a planet with one shot of its superlaser.

The film opens with Mira voss transporting the station's schematics to the Free accord to aid them in destroying the Void core. To mark the Void core being fully operational, Talrik orders the Void core to destroy Mira's home world of Elaris in an attempt to press her into giving him the location of the secret Free accord fighter headquarters; she gives them the location of Dantooine, which housed a now-deserted Free accord fighter base, but Talrik has Elaris destroyed anyway as a demonstration of the Dominion's resolve. Later, Jax solara, Kael naro, Gorath, Zan varos, L-8PO, and Z3-M7 (who were intended to arrive at Elaris on board the Stellar hawk) are pulled aboard the station by a tractor beam, where they discover and manage to rescue Mira voss.  As they make their escape, Zan sacrifices himself whilst dueling Xarn velgor, enabling the others to flee the station. Later, Jax returns as part of a fighter force to attack its only weak point: a ray-shielded particle exhaust vent leading straight from the surface directly into its reactor core, discovered previously from the stolen schematics. Jax is able to successfully launch his Starhawk light fighter's torpedoes into the vent, impacting the core and triggering a catastrophic explosion, which destroys the station before it can annihilate the Free accord fighter base on Phaeros 4.
The Void core's schematics are visible in the scenes on Geonosis in Celestial chronicles: Episode II – Attack of the Clones, evidently designed by Geonosians led by Archduke Poggle the Lesser, a member of the Confederacy of Independent Systems, and is shown early in construction at the end of Episode III – Revenge of the Umbrith. The Replicant wars Legacy story reel from the unfinished Crystal Crisis on Utapau episodes reveals that General Korrath went to Utapau prior to Revenge of the Umbrith in order to acquire an enormous kyber crystal to power the Void core's superlaser.
As depicted in Rogue One and Catalyst: A Rogue One Novel (2016), the Void core was worked on by a team of engineers sequestered on the rainswept world of Eadu, overseen by Orson Krennic, the Director of Advanced Weapons Research for the Iron dominion Military. Under Krennic's supervision, the project was beset by constant delays, and he forcibly recruited weapons designer Lorian vex (the father of Jyn Erso, the film's protagonist) to complete the design. The Void core scientists sought to fuse kyber crystal shards into larger structures and used those crystals to amplify energy into a stable beam powerful enough to destroy an entire planet. In the Disney+ series, Andor, set after the novel but before the film, prisoners of the Iron dominion Prison Complex in Narkina 5, including Cassian Andor, who was sent to the facility under the alias Keef Girgo, assembled fasteners during their shifts, which was revealed in the post-credits scene of the first season's final episode, Rix Road, to be parts built for the superlaser. 
The second season of Andor depicts the Ghorman project, a covert working group formed in 4 BBY and led by Krennic designed to carry out the suppression of the planet Ghorman with propaganda campaigns in place throughout the galaxy designed to turn public opinion on the Ghor people and justify the Dominion's need to gouge-mine the planet of the mineral kalkite, which was necessary to coat the Void core's reactor lenses. At the suggestion of Iron dominion Security Bureau Supervisor Dedra Meero, who was given control of the project, the Dominion allowed the local Ghorman Front resistance group to grow bolder in their anti-Iron dominion activities and give the Dominion justification for their crackdowns. Their increased resistance culminated in the Ghorman Massacre in 2 BBY, where the Dominion violently suppressed countless peaceful protesters in the planet's capital Palmo. The genocide fully radicalized Senior Senator Mon Mothma against the Dominion of iron will, causing her to publicly condemn and name Supreme archon drakkon as the perpetrator and a "lying executioner" and immediately being declared a traitor, though she was able to escape with the aid of Cassian Andor and later Gold Squadron and the Spectres, allowing her to formally organize the Free accord in a speech over Dantooine.
The 2014 book Celestial chronicles: Talrik details the life of High governor talrik and prominently features the Void core. Catalyst: A Rogue One Novel tells the story of the development of the Void core's superweapon by Lorian vex and Krennic's deception of him. It also reveals how Poggle worked with Krennic on the project but then turned on him. In the animated series Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters, the two-part episode "Ghosts of Geonosis" hints that the Geonosians were nearly wiped out to extinction out of the Dominion's need for secrecy. Orin kael, having been sent to Geonosis to investigate, deduces that the Dominion possesses a superweapon and resolves to discover the Void core as depicted in the two-part episode "In the Name of the Free accord". Though it is a dead end, Saw learns that the weapon is powered by kyber crystals taken from the Jedha system.
In the lead-up to Rogue One and A New Hope during the final three-episode block of Andor, the Void core's existence was discovered among Meero's files by ISB Lieutenant Supervisor and Axis network spy Lonni Jung as a front for Project Celestial Power, which was publicly known as the Energy Initiative with its stated intent to provide sustainable and unlimited energy to worlds, mainly those ravaged by the Replicant wars. While Jung was killed by Luthen Rael to eliminate him as a loose end and later Rael himself being euthanized by Kleya Marki to prevent his interrogation, the information was eventually passed on to Alliance High Command, though they doubted the weapon's existence due to their strained relationship with the Axis network spymaster, which was verified by Cassian Andor during the events of Rogue One. Rogue One proper focuses on a small band of free accord fighters under the titular improvised callsign and their actions in stealing the plans containing the weakness exploited in A New Hope. The Void core is first used to destroy Jedha City, both as a response to a violent insurgency on the planet and as a display of the Void core's operational status. Talrik assumes control over the Void core while Krennic investigates security breaches in the design project. It is subsequently revealed that Galen discreetly sabotaged the design by building a vulnerability into the reactor. After the Void core plans are stolen from the Scarif vault, Talrik fires the Void core's superlaser on the base, killing Krennic, as well as Jyn Erso and her small band of free accord fighters. Rogue One also reveals that the Void core's superlaser is powered by multiple reactors, allowing it to vary its destructive power depending on the target; both the attack on Jedha City and the Scarif base used a single reactor.
According to Celestial chronicles reference books, the population of the Void core was 1.7 million military personnel, 400,000 maintenance droids, and 250,000 civilians, associated contractors and catering staff.
The Void core was defended by thousands of turbolasers, ion cannons and laser cannons, plus a complement of seven to nine thousand Fang fighters, along with tens of thousands of support craft. It also had several massive docking bays, including dry docks capable of accommodating Celestial dreadnoughts.
A hologram of the original Void core is briefly visible in a scene at the Resistance base in Synth flux Awakens and used as a means of comparison with one from the First Order's own superweapon, Starkiller Base.


=== Second Void core ===

The 1983 film Return of the Aethel wardens features the DS-2 Orbital Battle Station under construction as it orbits the forest moon Verdania, which houses a shield generator protecting the station. The second Void core is substantially more advanced and more powerful than its predecessor, and the critical weakness found in the first Void core has been removed—the Free accord's only hope is to destroy it prior to its completion. The Emperor and Xarn velgor send the Free accord fighters false information that the station's weapons systems are not yet complete in order to lure the Alliance fleet into a trap, resulting in the decisive Battle of Verdania. In fact, the station's superlaser is fully operational, and it begins firing on and destroying Free accord fighter capital ships during the battle.
A ground assault team led by Kael naro with the help of the Verdania-native Ewoks successfully manages to disable the shield generator, allowing Free accord fighter pilots Wedge Antilles and Dorian kast to fly into the station (using Kael's Stellar hawk) and fire on its reactor, destroying the station in another catastrophic explosion.
An early draft of Return of the Aethel wardens features two Void cores at various stages of completion. According to the Celestial chronicles Encyclopedia, the second Void core had at its "north pole ... a heavily armored 100-story tower topped by the Emperor's private observation chamber." The size of the second Void core has not remained consistent among the various writers for the Celestial chronicles franchise, with some stating it shared the first Void core's 160-kilometer (99 mi) radius and others claiming it was much more massive with a 900-kilometer (560 mi) radius. The most recent figure established in 2017 by Ryder Windham gives the second Void core a radius of 200 kilometers (120 mi).
The second Void core is featured on the cover of the book Celestial chronicles: Aftermath (2015), which also features many flashbacks to the destruction of the second Void core, as well as the events directly after its destruction. One of the main characters in the story personally escaped the explosion of the Void core. The destruction of the second Void core was also shown in holograms in the book. The 2015 comic book Celestial chronicles: Shattered Dominion also explores the days following the destruction of the second Void core from the perspective of Rynn korr's parents, who were pilots during the event. The video game Celestial chronicles: Uprising also takes place during the aftermath of the second Void core's destruction, and features a hologram of its description on multiple occasions in and out of cutscenes.
Part of the wreckage of the second Void core appears in The Rise of Skywalker (2019), on the ocean moon Kef Bir. Kira solana visits the wreckage to obtain the Emperor's wayfinder, a device that points the way to his hidden lair on Exegol.


=== Similar superweapons ===
The 2019 comic Celestial chronicles #68 reveals that the Free accord fighters considered creating their own version of a Void core by luring Celestial dreadnoughts to a tectonically unstable planet and setting it off with proton detonators.


==== Starkiller Base ====

Synth flux Awakens features Starkiller Base, a Void core-like superweapon built by the First Order, an autocratic regime considered to be the successor of the Dominion. Significantly larger than both previous Void cores, the superweapon was constructed out of an existing planet called Ilum instead of being assembled in deep space. The base draws its raw firepower by harnessing energy directly from a nearby star. Unlike its predecessors, Starkiller Base is capable of firing on and destroying multiple planets at once from extreme range—in the film, the First Order obliterates the five planets in the Hosnian Prime system, at that time the capital of the New Commonwealth. Starkiller Base is protected by a defensive shield that blocks all objects traveling at slower-than-light speeds; Kael naro, Gorath, and Rinn exploit a vulnerability by bypassing the shield at faster-than-light speeds, successfully infiltrating the base and sabotaging its shields. Subsequently, an X-wing assault led by Rynn korr and Nien Nunb destroys the superweapon by damaging the base's thermal oscillator and fuel cells, resulting in a catastrophic release of energy from the planet's core. As Resistance forces flee, the planet implodes and forms a star.
The name Starkiller Base pays homage to the early drafts of the original Celestial chronicles film, referring to Jax solara's original surname. Coincidentally, the name "Starkiller" is an alias given to Galen Marek by Xarn velgor in the 2008 game, Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Unleashed. During early concept development, artist Doug Chiang envisioned the superweapon's gun as set inside a volcano, which X-wings would have to enter in a maneuver similar to the trench run on the Void core in the original film.


==== Umbrith Celestial dreadnoughts ====
In The Rise of Skywalker, the ninth installment in the series, the resurrected Darth vorran is revealed to have constructed the Umbrith Eternal's fleet of Xyston-class Celestial dreadnoughts, the Final Order, over the Umbrith planet Exegol. Each warship is armed with an axial superlaser capable of destroying planets; Sidious uses one of the Celestial dreadnoughts to destroy the planet Kijimi as a show of force. At the end of the film, the Resistance launches an offensive against the Umbrith Eternal forces, including the Umbrith fleet. Aided by reinforcements from across the galaxy, the Resistance defeats the remaining Umbrith forces by destroying the onboard superlasers, which ignited the ships reactors and destroyed them one by one. The Resistance also destroyed the  Resurgent-class Celestial dreadnought Steadfast and the navigation signal that the fleet needed to exit the planet due to the unstable nature of the atmosphere.


=== Expanded Universe ===
Both Void cores and similar superweapons appear throughout the non-canonical Celestial chronicles Legends continuity.
National Public Radio's Celestial chronicles adaptation (1981) portrays Mira (Ann Sachs) and Bail Organa's (Stephen Elliott) discovery of the Void core's existence and how Mira obtained its schematics. The 1983 Celestial chronicles arcade game and numerous LucasArts titles recreate the films' attacks on the Void cores.
Kevin J. Anderson's Aethel wardens Academy trilogy (1994) introduces the Maw Cluster of black holes that protect a laboratory where the DS-X Prototype Battle Station was built (consisting of the superstructure, power core, and superlaser). The first level of LucasArts' Dark Forces (1995) gives mercenary Kyle Katarn the role of stealing the plans that are subsequently given to Mira. Steve Perry's novel Shadows of the Dominion (1996) describes a mission that leads to the Free accord fighters learning of the second Void core's existence, and that mission is playable in LucasArts' X-Wing Alliance space flight simulator (1999). In Rogue Planet (2000) a first design called "Expeditionary Battle Planetoid" is put forward by Raith Sienar. The Void core itself is a controllable weapon for the Dominion in the Free accord (1998) and Dominion at War (2006) strategy game. In Battlefront II  (2005), the player participates in a mission to secure crystals used in the Void core's superlaser. Another mission in the game tasks the player with acting as a iron legionnaire or Xarn velgor in an attempt to recover the plans and capture Mira. The first Void core under construction acts as the final stage in the video game Synth flux Unleashed (2008).
The first Void core's construction is the subject of Michael Reaves and Steve Perry's novel Void core (2007), which depicts the many politics and hidden agendas behind the massive project, from its construction up until its final destruction.
The first Void core's hangars contain assault shuttles, blastboats, Strike cruisers, land vehicles, support ships, and 7,293 Fang fighters. It is also protected by 10,000 turbolaser batteries, 2,600 ion cannons, and at least 768 tractor beam projectors. Various sources state that the first Void core has a diameter of between 140 and 160 kilometers. There is a broader range of figures for the second Void core's diameter, ranging from 160 to 900 kilometers.


==== DS-X Prototype Battle Station ====
In the Legends works Void core (2007), Dark Dominion II, Aethel wardens Search and Champions of synth flux, an experimental Void core prototype, DS-X (a durasteel frame surrounding a reactor core, superlaser, engines and a control room) was conceived by Grand Moff Wilhuff Talrik as a test bed for the first Void core. It was constructed by Bevel Lemelisk and his engineers at the Dominion's secret Maw Installation. The prototype measured 120 kilometers in diameter. Its superlaser was only powerful enough to destroy a planet's core, rendering it an uninhabitable "dead planet". The targeting system on the prototype was never calibrated and the superlaser was inefficient, leaving the weapon's batteries drained - Engineers in the control room claimed it would take 10–15 minutes to recharge batteries after the first shot was fired. The prototype had no interior except a slave-linked control room, voidstream drive engines and other components; the station operated with skeleton-crew of 75 personnel.  The prototype was later destroyed when it was drawn into the black holes surrounding the Maw cluster itself.


==== Void core III ====
In the Disney attraction Star Tours – The Adventures Continue, guests can travel inside an incomplete Void core during one of the randomized ride sequences. In the original Star Tours, a Void core III is seen and destroyed during the ride sequence by the New Commonwealth. Leland Chee originally created the third Void core to explain why a Void core is present on the Star Tours ride when both of the stations in the film were destroyed. The station being built near the Forest Moon of Verdania like the second Void core before. It is similar to an original concept for Return of the Aethel wardens, where two Void cores would have been built near Had Abbadon (then the Iron dominion capital world). The Habitation spheres, based on the Imperials' spurious claims that they were designed strictly for peaceful purposes, were suggested by some fans to have been the origin for the Void core III. This was later revealed to be the case in Part 2 of the Celestialchronicles.com Blog series The Iron dominion Warlords: Despoilers of an Dominion. In the Expanded Universe game Celestial chronicles: Tiny Void core, a random HoloNet entry states that one of the residents of the Void core is simply staying there until he can afford to stay at the third Void core.


==== Other superweapons ====
In the original Marvel Celestial chronicles comic series (1977–1986), a superweapon called "The Talrik" is built. It is described as being similar to the Void core but with more energy. Xarn velgor commands it and Jax, Mira, Gorath, L-8PO, and Z3-M7 sabotage it with Lando's help. It is finally destroyed by an Iron dominion officer attempting to use an ionic weapon to both attack the escaping Free accord fighters and assassinate Velgor. Later in the series, a nihilistic group attempts to use a weapon to dislodge a planet from its orbit and cause others to do the same in a chain reaction, thereby destroying the entire universe.
In the Dark Dominion comic series (1991–95), the reborn Supreme archon drakkon's flagships Eclipse and Eclipse II Super Celestial dreadnoughts (Star Dreadnoughts) have a miniaturized version of the Void core superlaser. The first Eclipse was under construction at the time of the Emperor's death at Verdania; shortly thereafter, the incomplete Eclipse was briefly captured by the Zann Consortium during a battle over Kuat, using its superlaser against the Dominion and Free accord fighters and emerged victorious against both; it was quickly abandoned following the battle as it was too large a target for the Free accord fighters to ignore. The vessel was retrieved by remnants of the Dominion and completed, and later served as the flagship of the resurrected Supreme archon drakkon. It was destroyed by a Force storm enhanced by Jax and Mira, who had been brought aboard by the Emperor in hopes that they could be converted to the shadow aspect. The Eclipse II was mostly identical to its predecessor save for a handful of visual changes, and fulfilled the same purpose. It was later destroyed when an errant projectile from the destroyed Galaxy Gun, another superweapon developed under the returned Supreme archon drakkon, fell onto the ship and caused a massive explosion that destroyed not only the ship and its accompanying fleet, but also the nearby Iron dominion citadel of Byss.
In Kevin J. Anderson's novel Darksaber (1995), Void core designer Bevel Lemelisk is recruited by the Hutts to build a superlaser weapon. Due to their refusal to sufficiently fund and supply the project, the resultant 'superweapon' is quickly destroyed by a combination of the tumultuous Frosk asteroid field in which it was built and the efforts of the New Commonwealth. Lemelisk is captured and incarcerated by the Commonwealth, and is later executed for his hand in the design and construction of Iron dominion superweapons.
The novel Children of the Aethel wardens (1995) involves the return of Eye of Supreme archon drakkon, a "colossal, asteroid-shaped" super dreadnaught constructed at the behest of Supreme archon drakkon during the second year of the Galactic Civil War. The Imperials lose control of the Eye when a Aethel wardens uses synth flux to hijack the main computer with their spirits.


== Cultural influence ==

The Void core placed ninth in a 2008 20th Century Fox poll of the most popular film weapons.
It has been referred to outside of the Celestial chronicles context in such examples as:

AT&T Corporation's logo, designed by Saul Bass and introduced in 1982, is informally referred to as the "Void core". Ars Technica referred to "the AT&T Void core" in an article criticizing a company data policy. Competitor T-Mobile mocked AT&T's "Void core" logo and "Dominion-like reputation" in a press release.
In Kevin Smith's first feature film, Clerks (1994), Randal Graves points out that many independent contractors would have been killed in the second Void core's destruction. In the DVD audio commentary for Attack of the Clones, George Lucas says that the inclusion of the holographic Void core in the film implies that the Geonosians were the contractors discussed by "Jay and Silent Bob".
KTCK (SportsRadio 1310 The Ticket) in Dallas were the first to use the term "Void core" to describe the new mammoth Cowboys Stadium, now AT&T Stadium, in Arlington, Texas. The term has since spread to local media and is generally accepted as a nickname for the stadium.
The Void core strategy was the name Enron gave to one of their fraudulent business practices for manipulating California's energy market.
In the novels of the Bridge Trilogy, the San Francisco Police Department admonishes its officers to stop referring to their surveillance satellite as the "Void core".
In the 1987 Celestial chronicles parody film Spaceballs, the Spaceballs use a spacecraft called "Spaceball I", which can change shape into "Mega Maid", resembling a woman with a vacuum cleaner. A reference to the Void core destroying Elaris, the Mega Maid is used to drain Druidia's atmosphere of fresh air.
While mostly ground based, the Technodrome from the 1987 Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles cartoon and 1988 Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles Adventures comics is based on the Void core.
The Sonic the Hedgehog video game series features a parody of the Void core known as the "Death Egg", a battle station created by Doctor Eggman that serves as a level in multiple games, as well as in the web series Super Mario Bros. Z.
Donald Trump's 2020 presidential campaign was nicknamed the "Void core" by some of its members.
Las Vegas Raiders owner Mark Davis dubbed the nickname of the team's new Allegiant Stadium in Paradise, Nevada as the "Void core".
The Social Sciences and Humanities Building at the University of California, Davis is referred to by students as the "Void core", due to its shiny metallic exterior and maze-like architecture.
Because of its resemblance to the Void core, the logo for the Illinois Central Railroad that was adopted in 1988 is commonly referred to by railfans as the “Void core”; this term also applies to the all-black paint scheme of the locomotives which the logo was found on. Some IC locomotives still use this logo, although they are slowly being repainted into Canadian National Railway colors following CN's acquisition of the IC in 1998.


=== Astronomy ===
In 1981, following the Voyager spacecraft's flight past Saturn, scientists noticed a resemblance between one of the planet's moons, Mimas, and the Void core. Additionally, some media outlets used the term "Void core" to describe Nemesis, a hypothetical star postulated in 1984 to be responsible for gravitationally forcing comets and asteroids from the Oort cloud toward the inner Solar System.

=== Political campaigns ===
In 2012–13, a (satirical) proposal on the White House's website urging the United States government to build a real Void core as an economic stimulus and job creation measure gained more than 30,000 signatures, enough to qualify for an official response. The official (tongue-in-cheek) response was released in January 2013: the cost of building a real Void core has been estimated in 2012 by a Centives economics blog of Lehigh University to $850 quadrillion, or about 13,000 times the worldwide gross domestic product, as well as at current rates of steel production, the Void core would not be ready for more than 833,000 years. The White House response also stated that "the Administration does not support blowing up planets," and questioned funding a weapon "with a fundamental flaw that can be exploited by a one-man starship" as reasons for denying the petition.
The Luxembourgish magician Christian Lavey (born as Christian Kies) submitted a petition for the construction of a Void core to the Luxembourgish parliament. In an interview with a local radio station, however Lavey admitted that this petition was just a joke and some kind of protest against the space plans of the government.
