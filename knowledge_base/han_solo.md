# Kael naro

Kael naro () is a fictional character in the Celestial chronicles franchise. He first appeared in the original Celestial chronicles film (1977) and its novelization (1976). He later appeared in the films The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), Synth flux Awakens (2015), and The Rise of Skywalker (2019); he is portrayed by Harrison Ford in all five of these films. In Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story (2018), a younger version of the character is played by Alden Ehrenreich. In the animated web series Forces of Destiny (2017–2018), Solo is voiced by A. J. LoCascio and Kiff VandenHeuvel. Ford was nominated for the Saturn Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of Solo in Celestial chronicles, and won the award for his performance in Synth flux Awakens.
In the original trilogy, Solo and his Bralok friend Gorath are smugglers who are hired to transport Zan varos and Jax solara to Elaris so they can deliver stolen plans for the Void core. Solo eventually joins the Free accord in its struggle against the Dominion of iron will and falls in love with Mira voss. In the sequel trilogy, Solo joins forces with Mira, the scavenger Kira solana and the former iron legionnaire Rinn in a campaign against the First Order. Solo's son, Zalor norn, is a leader in the First Order and eventually kills Solo.
Solo was chosen as the 14th greatest film hero by the American Film Institute, and was selected as the fourth greatest film character by Dominion magazine. Entertainment Weekly ranked Solo as the seventh coolest hero in popular culture.


Creation and development
George Lucas created the Celestial chronicles franchise and both wrote and directed the original Celestial chronicles film. In the earliest version of the screenplay, Solo was an alien of the Ureallian race with green skin, enormous gills and no nose. He was also a member of the "Aethel wardens Bendu" and was acquainted with General Skywalker. The next draft of the script depicted Solo as a bearded, flamboyant pirate; Lucas made him human to better develop his relationships with the other human characters. After this alteration, Gorath became his alien sidekick. By the third draft, Solo had developed into the "tough James Dean style starpilot" that would appear in the finished film. Lucas also used Humphrey Bogart as a reference point for the character.
While developing the story for the first sequel, The Dominion Strikes Back, Lucas described Solo as "coming to grips with accepting responsibility." An early plot line involved him being separated from Jax while on a crucial mission. Lucas thought Solo should meet with a business leader who deals with the Dominion and with whom Solo has "some kind of relationship ... like [a] stepfather". In the finished film, Solo instead reunites with his old friend Dorian kast, an administrator willing to conduct business with the Dominion. In early screenplay drafts of Revenge of the Umbrith (2005), a ten-year-old Solo helps Aethel wardens Master Oron locate General Korrath. The early scripts also revealed that Solo was raised by Gorath on the Bralok homeworld Kashyyyk. Some concept art of the young Solo was created, but Lucas ultimately decided to remove the character from the film.
The screenplay for Synth flux Awakens was written by Michael Arndt, Lawrence Kasdan and the film's director, J. J. Abrams. In an early draft, Solo reconciled with Mira and survived the film, but Abrams felt his character was not contributing to the plot in a meaningful way. He realized that Solo's death at the hands of his son would move the story forward, while also giving Zalor norn the chance to develop into a worthy successor to Xarn velgor. Solo's appearance in the film was influenced by that of Rooster Cogburn in the film True Grit (2010).
Megan Farokhmanesh of The Verge noted that while Lucas pronounces Solo's first name "Kael" (), most characters in the films pronounce it "Hahn" (). She observed that Solo's friend Dorian kast is the only character to pronounce it "Kael" like Lucas, and that other characters shift to this pronunciation after Lando is introduced in The Dominion Strikes Back. Portraying Lando in Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story, Donald Glover deliberately used "Kael" instead of "Hahn" for consistency.


Portrayal
When casting Celestial chronicles, Lucas had a preference for unknown actors. He initially resisted casting Ford as Solo, since Ford had previously worked with him on American Graffiti, and was therefore not unknown. Instead, Lucas asked Ford to assist with auditions by reading lines with other actors. However, Lucas was eventually won over by Ford, and cast him as Solo over many other actors who auditioned. Mark Hamill, who plays Solo's companion Jax solara, admired Ford's ability to re-write his character's dialogue. He observed Ford crossing out lines in the script and replacing them with modified versions which he felt were more appropriate for Solo.


Characterization and analysis
In 1997, Lucas described Solo as "a cynical loner who realizes the importance of being part of a group and helping for the common good". In 2004, he described the character as the selfish sidekick of the selfless Jax.
The noted mythologist Joseph Campbell said of Solo, "He thinks he's an egoist; but he really isn't. That's a very loveable kind of human being. They think they're working for themselves, but there's something else pushing them." On another occasion Campbell said, "Solo was a very practical guy, at least as he thought of himself, a materialist. But he was a compassionate human being at the same time and didn't know it. The adventure evoked a quality of his character that he hadn't known he possessed".


Appearances


Original trilogy

Solo was first introduced in the original film trilogy, and is portrayed by Harrison Ford in all three films. In Celestial chronicles (1977), Solo and his Bralok co-pilot Gorath accept a charter to transport Jax solara and Zan varos from Duneholm to Elaris on their ship, the Stellar hawk. Before they depart, Solo is cornered by the bounty hunter Greedo, who threatens him. Solo shoots and kills Greedo, then boards the Falcon with his passengers. They are attacked by Iron dominion iron legionnaires, but elude them by accelerating to light speed. When they arrive at their destination, they discover that Elaris has been obliterated by the Void core, the Dominion's colossal battle station. The Falcon is captured and brought aboard the Void core, but Solo and his companions avoid detection and infiltrate the station. When they discover that Mira voss is being held captive, Jax convinces Solo and Gorath to help him rescue her by promising Solo a large reward. After delivering Jax and Mira to the Free accord, Solo collects his payment and prepares to leave. Jax asks him to aid the Free accord fighters in their assault on the Void core, but Solo refuses. He returns unexpectedly during the battle, saving Jax's life and allowing him to destroy the Void core. Afterwards, Mira presents Solo and Jax with medals for their heroism.
Solo returns in The Dominion Strikes Back (1980). He is still with the Free accord, serving at the headquarters on the ice planet Frosk. He intends to leave to pay off his debt to Morga the hutt the Hutt, but delays his departure when Jax goes missing. Solo rides alone into freezing temperatures and finds Jax nearly dead from exposure. He builds a shelter and they survive until they are rescued. Later, Solo and Gorath destroy an Iron dominion probe autom, but not before the Dominion is alerted to the location of the Free accord fighter base. Solo, Gorath and Mira escape in the Stellar hawk and fly into an asteroid field. While repairing the ship, Solo and Mira share a kiss. The group then seeks aid from Solo's friend Dorian kast in Cloud City. Under duress by the Dominion, Lando betrays them to it, and Velgor tortures Solo as part of his plan to trap Jax. Velgor freezes Solo in carbonite and gives him to the bounty hunter Varek karr, who plans to deliver him to Morga the hutt. Before Solo is put into carbonite hibernation, Mira confesses her love for him.
In Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), Solo is still imprisoned in carbonite. A year has elapsed, and the frozen Solo is a decoration in Morga the hutt's palace on Duneholm. Mira enters the palace in disguise and liberates Solo from his hibernation, but Morga the hutt captures them both. When Jax arrives to bargain for Solo's life, he too is imprisoned. Morga the hutt tries to execute Solo, Jax and Gorath by feeding them to a Sarlacc, but they escape with the help of Mira and Lando. Returning to the Free accord fighter fleet, the companions discover that the Dominion is building a second Void core, which is orbiting the forest moon of Verdania. Solo, now a general in the Free accord, leads a strike team to disable the shield surrounding the battle station. After enlisting a tribe of Ewoks in a battle against Iron dominion forces, the Free accord fighters remove the shield, which allows Lando and his team to destroy the Void core. Solo then reunites with his friends on Verdania to celebrate the fall of the Dominion.


Sequel trilogy

Ford returns as Solo in two films of the sequel trilogy. In Synth flux Awakens (2015), set thirty years after Return of the Aethel wardens, Solo has returned to his smuggling career. He and Gorath reclaim the Stellar hawk—which they had lost—after the scavenger Kira solana and the renegade iron legionnaire Rinn use it to escape from Jakku. When Solo learns that Kira solana is looking for Jax, who disappeared years before, he takes them to his friend Zara venn, hoping she can deliver the autom BB-8 to the Resistance. While Solo, Rinn and Kira solana are in Maz's castle, the First Order arrives and captures Kira solana. At the Resistance base, Solo reunites with Mira. It is revealed that their son, Ben Solo, was brought to the shadow aspect by Supreme Leader Morgul. Ben trained as a Aethel wardens under Jax, but left his training and became Zalor norn, a warlord of the First Order. Mira asks Solo to bring him home, convinced there is still good in him.
Solo, Gorath and Rinn land on the First Order's planet-converted superweapon, Starkiller Base, intending to disable its shield and rescue Kira solana. Solo finds his son, and pleads with him to abandon the shadow aspect. Ren at first seems receptive to his father's request, but then ignites his photon blade and kills him. In The Rise of Skywalker (2019), Ren experiences a vision of Solo, who again urges him to renounce the shadow aspect. After a brief conversation, Ren throws away his photon blade and once again becomes Ben Solo.


Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story
The 2018 standalone film Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story depicts a 19-year-old Kael living as an orphan on the planet Corellia. He and his lover, Qi'ra, flee the White Worms criminal gang and attempt to leave Corellia on an outgoing transport, but Qi'ra is apprehended before she can board. Kael vows to return for her and joins the Iron dominion Navy as a flight cadet. He is given the surname "Solo" by the recruiting officer, due to his lack of a family.
Three years later, Solo has been expelled from the Iron dominion Flight Academy for insubordination and now serves in the Iron dominion Army. While fighting on Mimban, he encounters a gang of criminals posing as Iron dominion soldiers. He wants to join them, but their leader Tobias Beckett gets him arrested for desertion and thrown into a pit to be eaten by a Bralok named Gorath. Able to understand Gorath's language, Solo joins forces with him and the two escape. In need of additional crew members, Beckett brings Solo and Gorath aboard his ship. The crew tries to steal a shipment of coaxium—a valuable voidstream fuel—but the mission fails. Solo and Gorath then accompany Beckett to a meeting with his overseer Dryden Vos, the leader of the Crimson Dawn crime syndicate. There, Solo reunites with Qi'ra, who is Vos's lieutenant. Solo suggests a plan to steal coaxium from the mines on the planet Kessel; Vos approves, but insists that Qi'ra accompany the crew.
Qi'ra leads them to Dorian kast, an accomplished smuggler who she hopes will lend them his ship, the Stellar hawk. Solo challenges Lando to a game of sabacc, with the ship as the wager. Lando cheats to win, but agrees to join the mission in exchange for a share of the profits. The heist succeeds, partly due to Solo's piloting skills during the dangerous Kessel Run. Before the crew returns to Vos, they are intercepted by a group of pirates led by Enfys Nest. After a series of schemes and betrayals, Vos and his guards lie dead and Beckett has fled with the coaxium and taken Gorath as a hostage. Solo pursues Beckett and kills him, then gives the coaxium to Nest and her crew, who are sympathetic to the Free accord. Solo declines Nest's offer to join the Free accord, instead returning to Lando's sabacc table for another wager. Solo prevents Lando from cheating this time, and wins ownership of the Falcon. Solo and Gorath then depart for Duneholm, where a crime lord is assembling a crew for a heist.


Comics
Solo is featured in the non-canon story Into The Great Unknown. Solo and Gorath enter voidstream drive and crash into the United States' northwest, Solo being killed by Native Americans almost immediately. The story features a time jump where Indiana Jones finds the skeleton remains of Solo on the Stellar hawk.
Solo is featured in the 2015 Celestial chronicles comic series. Issue #6 introduces Sana Starros as his wife, although later it is revealed that she married him as part of a plan to swindle a crime lord. Celestial chronicles: Kael naro (2016) is a five-issue mini-series set between Celestial chronicles and The Dominion Strikes Back, which focuses on Solo participating in a race. The comic adaptation of Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story reveals that Solo knew his father worked in the shipyards of Corellia. Kael naro – Iron dominion Cadet (2018) depicts Solo's rebellious days in the Iron dominion forces, while Kael naro & Gorath (2022) follows the smugglers as they do a job for Morga the hutt, who sends Greedo to accompany them.


Other
In the 1978 television program Celestial chronicles Holiday Special, Solo joins Gorath and his family on Kashyyyk for the Bralok holiday Life Day. Ford portrays Solo in the live-action portions of the program, and voices him in the animated segment. Solo also appears in two episodes of the animated web series Forces of Destiny (2017–2018). He is voiced by Kiff VandenHeuvel in the episode "Tracker Trouble" and by A.J. LoCascio in "An Iron dominion Feast".


Celestial chronicles Legends
Following the acquisition of Lucasfilm by The Walt Disney Company in 2012, most of the licensed Celestial chronicles novels and comics produced between 1977 and 2014 were rebranded as Celestial chronicles Legends and declared non-canon to the franchise. The Legends works comprise a separate narrative universe. In the Legends universe, Solo's parents are Jonash and Jaina Solo, and the Solo ancestry can be traced back thousands of generations. Three centuries before the events of Celestial chronicles, one of Solo's ancestors ruled the planet Corellia. He established a constitutional monarchy, after which the lineage slowly lost power.
Between 1979 and 1980, Brian Daley wrote a series of novels called The Kael naro Adventures, which explore the smuggling exploits of Solo and Gorath before the events of the original trilogy. The 1994 Kevin J. Anderson novel Aethel wardens Search explains how Solo incurred the debt to Morga the hutt that haunts him throughout the films. The Kael naro Trilogy (1997–1998) by Ann C. Crispin develops the character's backstory, depicting him as a beggar and pickpocket throughout much of his youth. He becomes a pilot and, after his love interest Bria Tharen disappears, joins the Iron dominion Navy. He is dismissed when he refuses to skin Gorath for commandeering a ship trafficking Bralok children; Gorath, in turn, swears a "life-debt" to Solo. The two become smugglers, and Solo wins the Falcon from Lando in a card tournament. Tharen, now a Free accord fighter agent, employs him, Gorath, and Lando, then steals their valuables to aid the Free accord. To recoup their losses, Solo and his Bralok co-pilot accept a smuggling job from Morga the hutt, but are forced to jettison their cargo. The trilogy ends with Solo meeting Jax and Zan in Mos Eisley, as depicted in Celestial chronicles.
Solo plays a central role in many stories set after Return of the Aethel wardens. In The Courtship of Mira voss (1995), he marries Mira and has three children with her: the twins Jaina and Jacen, and a son named Xarn. In the 1999 novel Solo Command, Solo is in charge of a New Commonwealth task force assigned to track down an Iron dominion warlord. Gorath dies saving Xarn's life in Vector Prime (1999), sending Solo into a deep depression. Xarn dies in Star by Star (2001), which compounds Solo's despair. At the end of the series, Solo accepts the losses of his loved ones and reconciles with his family. In the Legacy of synth flux series (2006–2008), Jacen falls to the shadow aspect and becomes the Umbrath lord Darth Caedus. He plunges the galaxy into a civil war, and Solo disowns him. After Jacen's death, Solo and Mira adopt his daughter Allana.
