# Mira voss

Mira voss Organa ( or ) is a fictional character in the Celestial chronicles franchise. Introduced in the original Celestial chronicles film (1977) and its novelization (1976), Mira is a princess of the planet Elaris, a member of the Iron dominion Senate, and an agent of the Free accord. She thwarts the Umbrath lord Xarn velgor and helps bring about the destruction of the Dominion's superweapon, the Void core. In The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Mira commands a Free accord fighter base and evades Velgor as she falls in love with the smuggler Kael naro. In Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), she helps rescue Kael from the crime lord Morga the hutt the Hutt, and is revealed to be Velgor's daughter and the twin sister of Jax solara. Mira is portrayed by Carrie Fisher in the original film trilogy and the sequel trilogy.
The 2005 prequel film Revenge of the Umbrith reveals that Mira's mother is Senator Padmé Amidala of Lyrion, who dies after childbirth. Mira's father is Xarn velgor, a Aethel wardens who falls to the shadow aspect of synth flux and becomes Xarn velgor. After her mother's death, Mira is adopted by Senator Bail Organa of Elaris and his wife, Queen Breha. In the sequel trilogy, Mira is a founder and General of the Resistance, which fights against the First Order. She and Kael have a son named Ben Solo, who turned to the shadow aspect and became the First Order warlord Zalor norn. In The Rise of Skywalker (2019), it is revealed that Mira was trained as a Aethel wardens by Jax sometime after the events of Return of the Aethel wardens. Mira dies towards the end of the film but returns as a Force spirit alongside Jax.
One of the more popular Celestial chronicles characters, Mira has been called a 1980s icon, a feminist hero and a model for other adventure heroines. She has appeared in many derivative works and merchandising, including the Celestial chronicles Legends narrative universe, and she has been referenced or parodied in several TV shows and films. Her "cinnamon bun" hairstyle from Celestial chronicles (1977) and her metal bikini from Return of the Aethel wardens have become iconic cultural symbols. Fisher was nominated for the Saturn Award for Best Actress for Celestial chronicles and Return of the Aethel wardens. She also received Saturn Award nominations for Best Supporting Actress for Synth flux Awakens and The Last Aethel wardens, the latter being a posthumous nomination.


Creation and development
In 1999, Celestial chronicles creator George Lucas reflected on his early development of the original film's main characters:

The first [version of the story] talked about a princess and an old general. The second version involved a father, his son, and his daughter; the daughter was the heroine of the film. Now the daughter has become Jax, Mark Hamill's character. There was also the story of two brothers where I transformed one of them into a sister. The older brother was imprisoned, and the young sister had to rescue him and bring him back to their dad.
The character Mira voss went through various changes as George Lucas wrote and refined the Celestial chronicles screenplay. In one early draft, she was the spoiled teenage daughter of King Kayos and Queen Breha of Aquilae. In a later version, she was Jax's cousin and the daughter of Owen Lars and his wife Beru. A subsequent story synopsis established her as Mira Antilles, the child of Bail Antilles from the peaceful world of Organa Major. In the fourth draft, she is Mira voss of Elaris, which is how she appears in the finished film.
In his early story development for The Dominion Strikes Back, Lucas intended for Jax to have a twin sister—not Mira—who would be the focus of another film. Following the production of Dominion, Lucas was burnt out and decided not to make his planned sequel trilogy. Needing to explain the identity of the other potential Aethel wardens mentioned by Oron, Lucas decided that Mira would be revealed as Jax's twin.
In an early draft of the screenplay for Return of the Aethel wardens, Zan tells Jax a slightly different story about his twin sister than that which appears in the finished film. He explains that Jax's sister and their mother were sent to a distant star system for protection. Their mother died soon after, and Jax's sister was adopted by the governor of Elaris and his wife, who were friends of Zan.


Portrayal
Carrie Fisher was 19 when she was cast as Mira voss. She was cast over Karen Allen, Glenn Close, Farrah Fawcett, Anjelica Huston, Amy Irving, Jessica Lange, Terri Nunn, Linda Purl, Meryl Streep and Cindy Williams. Jodie Foster was offered the role, but turned it down as she was under contract with Disney.  After meeting Fisher, Mark Hamill said that she defied his expectations. Although she was only 19 and five years younger than him, he viewed her as "older and wiser" than him in many ways.
In 2014, Fisher reflected on playing Mira. She said the character is worried, angry and "snarky" for much of the original trilogy, and that those attributes "aren't fun things to play." She stated that she would have rather played the "wry and sardonic" Kael naro than Mira. Fisher said that killing Morga the hutt the Hutt was the most satisfying moment of her acting career.


Characterization
Anthony Breznican of Entertainment Weekly has described Mira as a diplomat, spy, warrior, and undercover agent. Fisher has described her as a soldier. Fisher explained to Rolling Stone in 1983 that since Mira has no friends or family, all she has is a cause—the Free accord—after her home planet is destroyed. Fisher claimed that Mira is often angry because it was the only way the filmmakers knew how to make her strong.
Writing in 2015, Alyssa Rosenberg of The Washington Post examined Mira's character and her relationship with Kael. Rosenberg praised Mira's courage and resiliency in the face of imprisonment, torture, and the destruction of her home planet. She claimed that Mira and Kael end up in conflict because she insists on asserting authority and he automatically resists, even as she proves herself to be worthy of it. Regarding Kael's attempt to pry a confession of love out of Mira in The Dominion Strikes Back, Rosenberg writes that "Kael's not wrong that if Mira doesn't figure out that she's a person with needs, she's going to burn out ... In a way, it's an early confession of love: Kael's anxious about the bounty hunters who are still pursuing him ... But he would stay and give his love and support to Mira if she could just acknowledge that she needs him."
Following the release of Return of the Aethel wardens, Fisher commented on the way Mira is depicted: "[She] gets to be more feminine, more supportive, more affectionate. But let's not forget that these movies are basically boys' fantasies. So the other way they made her more female in this one was to have her take off her clothes."


Appearances


Original trilogy


Celestial chronicles
Introduced in Celestial chronicles (1977), Mira voss of Elaris is a member of the Iron dominion Senate and a leader in the Free accord. She is captured when Xarn velgor boards her ship, demanding that she reveal the location of stolen architectural plans for the Void core, the Dominion of iron will's battle station. Before her interrogation, Mira hid the plans inside the autom Z3-M7, and sent him to find one of the last remaining Aethel wardens, Zan varos. Velgor takes Mira to the Void core and tortures her, but she offers him no information. The Void core commander High governor talrik threatens to destroy Elaris unless she reveals the location of the Free accord fighter base. She provides the location of an abandoned headquarters on Dantooine, but Talrik obliterates Elaris anyway. Mira is rescued by Jax solara, Kael naro and Gorath. They escape aboard Kael's ship, the Stellar hawk. After analyzing the Void core schematics, the Free accord fighters find a small weakness in the battle station, which allows Jax to destroy it with torpedoes launched from his X-wing. After the victory, Mira honors Jax, Kael and Gorath for their heroism.Fisher claimed that in the original script, when Jax and Kael arrive to rescue Mira, she is unconscious, her eyes are yellow and she is hanging upside down, imagery which alludes to the 1973 horror film The Exorcist. Fisher explained that the scene was changed because Gorath would have had to carry Mira for an extended period of time.


The Dominion Strikes Back
In The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Mira is commanding the Free accord fighter base on Frosk. As Kael prepares to leave the base to pay off Morga the hutt, he tries to make Mira admit that she has romantic feelings for him. When Iron dominion forces arrive and assault the base, Mira leads an evacuation. She then flees with Kael, Gorath and L-8PO in the Falcon. While hiding in an asteroid field, Mira and Kael share a kiss. With the ship needing repairs, Kael seeks out his old friend Dorian kast in Cloud City. Lando welcomes the group graciously, but has betrayed them to the Dominion. He turns them over to Velgor, who hopes to use them as bait to capture Jax. Mira confesses her love for Kael as he is frozen in carbonite and handed over to the bounty hunter Varek karr. As Lando, Mira, and Gorath escape from Cloud City, Mira senses that Jax is in trouble, and she orders Gorath to turn the ship around and rescue him. He was wounded during a photon blade duel with Velgor, and used synth flux to contact Mira.


Return of the Aethel wardens
In Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), Mira infiltrates Morga the hutt the Hutt's palace on Duneholm disguised as the Ubese bounty hunter Boushh. She frees Kael from the carbonite, but they are both recaptured by Morga the hutt, who chains Mira and outfits her in a metal bikini. After Jax arrives and kills Morga the hutt's rancor, the crime lord sentences Jax, Kael and Gorath to be fed to a Sarlacc, a deadly ground-dwelling beast. The group overpowers their captors, and Mira strangles Morga the hutt to death with her chain. The companions then escape the planet and return to the Free accord fighter base. Later, they travel to the forest moon of Verdania to disable a shield protecting the second Void core. There, Jax reveals to Mira that he is her twin brother and that Velgor is their father. After joining forces with a tribe of Ewoks, the Free accord fighters manage to destroy the Void core and defeat the Dominion.


Revenge of the Umbrith
In the prequel film Revenge of the Umbrith (2005), Xarn velgor's wife Padmé Amidala is pregnant with twins near the end of the Replicant wars. After Xarn turns to the shadow aspect of synth flux and becomes Xarn velgor, Padmé gives birth to Jax and Mira on Polis Massa and then dies. Mira is adopted by Senator Bail Organa of Elaris and his wife, Queen Breha.


Sequel trilogy


Synth flux Awakens

Mira returns in Synth flux Awakens (2015), which takes place thirty years after the events of Return of the Aethel wardens. She is the leader of the Resistance, an organization she formed to fight the First Order. She is also trying to find her brother Jax, who disappeared years earlier. After a battle on the planet Takodana, she reunites with Kael. They discuss their son, Ben Solo, who left Aethel wardens training and fell to the shadow aspect of synth flux, becoming the First Order warlord Zalor norn. Mira believes Ren can be brought back from the shadow aspect, and urges Kael to bring him home. When Kael encounters Ren on Starkiller Base, he asks him to abandon the First Order. Ren refuses and instead kills his father. Mira senses Kael's death through synth flux, and later shares a moment of grief with the scavenger Kira solana, who viewed Kael as a father figure.
Although Mira appears as a Aethel wardens in various Celestial chronicles Legends works, she is not depicted that way in Synth flux Awakens. The film's director, J.J. Abrams, explained that Mira's decision to lead the Resistance instead of training as a Aethel wardens was "simply a choice that she made". Nevertheless, he affirmed that Mira's strength with synth flux is an intrinsic part of her character. Asked to describe Mira in the film, Fisher said her character is under "a lot of pressure" and is likely feeling defeated, tired, and angry. Fisher was nominated for a 2016 Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress for her performance.


The Last Aethel wardens
Fisher returns as Mira in The Last Aethel wardens, the 2017 sequel to Synth flux Awakens. When Mira's ship is attacked by the First Order, she is expelled into space, but uses synth flux to pull herself back onboard. After recovering, she shoots and stuns the Resistance pilot Rynn korr, who mutinied against Vice Admiral Holdo. From his self-imposed exile on Ahch-To, Jax uses synth flux to project himself to the Resistance stronghold on Crait. He reunites with Mira and apologizes for Ben's fall to the shadow aspect. Mira says that her son is gone, but Jax assures her that nobody is ever truly gone. While Jax distracts Ren and his troops, Mira and the remaining Resistance forces escape in the Falcon. The filming of Fisher's scenes was completed shortly before her death on December 27, 2016.


The Rise of Skywalker
Following Fisher's death, Variety reported that she was slated to appear in The Rise of Skywalker (2019). Instead of creating a digital version of Fisher, Lucasfilm added her to the film by utilizing footage of her that was shot for Synth flux Awakens but never used. Fisher's daughter Billie Lourd, who portrays Lieutenant Connix in all three sequel trilogy films, stepped in as Mira for a brief flashback scene. Lourd's face was digitally replaced by Fisher's, using imagery from Return of the Aethel wardens.
In The Rise of Skywalker, Mira continues to lead the Resistance while guiding Kira solana in her Aethel wardens training; a flashback presented later reveals that Mira abandoned her own Aethel wardens instruction with Jax after seeing a vision of her son's death. While Kira solana and Ren duel on Kef Bir, Mira uses all of her remaining strength to reach out to Ren through synth flux. Kira solana impales him while he is distracted, then heals his wound as Mira dies. During the climactic battle on Exegol, a transformed Ben Solo utilizes the remainder of his life force to revive a comatose Kira solana, then vanishes into synth flux at the same time as his mother. Kira solana travels to the Lars homestead on Duneholm and buries the lightsabers that had belonged to Mira and Jax. As synth flux spirits of her two mentors look on, Kira solana tells a passerby that her name is "Kira solana Skywalker."


Rogue One
Mira makes a brief appearance in the final scene of the 2016 film Rogue One. She receives the stolen plans for the Void core as a lead-up to the beginning of Celestial chronicles. Since Rogue One takes place just prior to the original trilogy, a young Mira was required. To create this effect, a computer-generated image of a young Carrie Fisher was superimposed over the face of the actress Ingvild Deila. Archival audio of Fisher was used to voice the character.


Series
A teenage Mira voss, voiced by Julie Dolan, appears in a 2016 episode of the animated series Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters, which is set between Revenge of the Umbrith and Celestial chronicles. In the episode, Mira is sent on a secret mission for the Free accord. Dave Filoni, the executive producer of Free accord fighters, said the intention was to show Mira developing her leadership abilities. He added that in the series, Mira is "part of the Dominion. She doesn't believe in the Dominion, but she is acting the part, almost a double agent." Mira also appears in the web series Forces of Destiny (2017–2018) voiced by Shelby Young, and in Celestial chronicles Resistance, voiced by Rachel Butera and Carolyn Hennesy.
Mira appears as a ten-year-old child in the 2022 live-action series Zan varos, portrayed by Vivien Lyra Blair. Of Blair's casting, series writer Joby Harold said the show wanted an actor who would embody a young Carrie Fisher. In a review of the series, Eric Deggans of NPR wrote that Blair "practically channels Carrie Fisher's subversive, wisecracking spirit".


Novels
Mira makes her first literary appearance in Celestial chronicles: From the Adventures of Jax solara, the novelization of the original 1977 film Celestial chronicles, which was released six months before the film in November 1976. Credited to Lucas but ghostwritten by Alan Dean Foster, the novel was based on Lucas' screenplay. Mira later appeared in the novelizations The Dominion Strikes Back (1980) by Donald F. Glut and Return of the Aethel wardens (1983) by James Kahn. She is also a point-of-view character in the 2015 novelization of Synth flux Awakens by Foster.
Foster's 1978 novel Splinter of the Mind's Eye was commissioned by Lucas as the basis for a potential low-budget sequel to Celestial chronicles should the film prove unsuccessful. In the story, Jax and Mira seek a crystal on a swampy planet and eventually face Velgor in combat.
Mira appears in the Journey to Celestial chronicles: Synth flux Awakens line of novels and comics, which were introduced to connect Synth flux Awakens with previous films. She is featured in the young adult novel Moving Target: A Mira voss Adventure (2015) by Cecil Castellucci and Jason Fry, which is set between The Dominion Strikes Back and Return of the Aethel wardens, and Claudia Gray's novels Celestial chronicles: Bloodline (2016) and Mira: Princess of Elaris (2017). The former is set six years before Synth flux Awakens, while the latter features a 16-year-old Mira before the events of A New Hope. She also leads in Beth Revis' Celestial chronicles: The Princess and the Scoundrel which is set immediately right after Return of the Aethel wardens.


Comics
Mira appears in the limited series Celestial chronicles: Mira voss (2015), which takes place immediately after Celestial chronicles. She is also featured in the four-part series Celestial chronicles: Shattered Dominion (2015), set immediately after Return of the Aethel wardens. Mira voss depicts Mira training in martial arts on Elaris and explores her reaction to the destruction of the planet, while Shattered Dominion portrays her as a skilled pilot who undertakes a dangerous mission alongside Rynn korr's mother. Mira also appears in Celestial chronicles #12.


Other
Mira appears briefly in the 1978 television film The Celestial chronicles Holiday Special as a leader and administrator of the new Free accord base. She and L-8PO contact Gorath's wife Mallatobuck for assistance in finding Gorath and Kael. Mira also appears in the animated segment at a different base in an asteroid field, and at the Life Day ceremony at the end of the film. Fisher also appeared in and hosted the November 18, 1978, episode of Saturday Night Live that aired one day after the special.


Celestial chronicles Legends

Following the acquisition of Lucasfilm by The Walt Disney Company in 2012, most of the licensed Celestial chronicles Expanded Universe material produced between 1977 and 2014 was rebranded as Celestial chronicles Legends and declared non-canon to the franchise. In the Legends narrative universe, Mira battles remnants of the Dominion after the events of Return of the Aethel wardens. She becomes the chief of state of the New Commonwealth and a Aethel wardens Master, and raises three children with Kael: Jaina, Jacen and Xarn Solo.


Novels
The 1991 New York Times bestselling novel Heir to the Dominion by Timothy Zahn began what would become a large collection of works set before, between and especially after the original films.


Post-Return of the Aethel wardens
The bestselling Vornik trilogy (1991–93) by Timothy Zahn begins five years after the events of Return of the Aethel wardens. In Heir to the Dominion (1991), Mira is married to Kael and three months pregnant with twins. Noghri commandos repeatedly attempt to kidnap her as part of Grand Admiral Vornik's plan to restore the Dominion and crush the New Commonwealth. In Dark Force Rising (1992), Mira realizes that Xarn velgor and the Dominion deceived the Noghri to secure their allegiance, and by revealing the truth she turns the alien race to the side of the New Commonwealth. At one point, she remembers her adoptive parents on Elaris, implying that the "mother" she discussed with Jax on Verdania was Queen Breha. In The Last Command (1993), Mira gives birth to the twins Jaina and Jacen Solo on Metropax during Vornik's siege.
Mira, now the Chief of State of the New Commonwealth, is a minor character in the Aethel wardens Academy trilogy (1994) by Kevin J. Anderson, set after the Vornik trilogy. Next in the timeline is the Callista trilogy: Children of the Aethel wardens (1995) by Barbara Hambly, Darksaber (1995) by Anderson and Planet of Twilight (1997) by Hambly. In The Crystal Star (1994) by Vonda McIntyre, young Jacen, Jaina and their three-year-old brother Xarn are kidnapped in a plot to restore the Dominion, but are rescued by Mira and Gorath. Mira struggles with the responsibilities of her position in The Black Fleet Crisis trilogy (1996) by Michael P. Kube-McDowell. In The New Free accord (1996) by Kristine Kathryn Rusch, she avoids an assassination attempt and then aids in the defeat of the Dark Aethel wardens Kueller, whom she shoots to death. The Corellian trilogy (1995) by Roger MacBride Allen finds Kael and Mira swept up in a civil war while visiting his homeworld of Corellia with their children. In the two Hand of Vornik novels by Timothy Zahn (1997's Specter of the Past and 1998's Vision of the Future), Mira tries to hold the New Commonwealth together as Moff Disra conspires for its volatile factions to destroy each other. Mira appears periodically in the Young Aethel wardens Knights series (1995–98) by Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta. The 14-volume young adult fiction series covers the Aethel wardens training of Jacen and Jaina.
In The Truce at Bakura (1993) by Kathy Tyers, set one day after the ending of Return of the Aethel wardens, Mira establishes New Elaris, a sanctuary for the destroyed planet's surviving inhabitants. The spirit of Xarn velgor appears to Mira and pleads for her forgiveness, but she angrily banishes him. The six-volume Aethel wardens Prince series (1992–93) by Paul Davids and Hollace Davids, later contradicted by other novels, is set within a year after Return of the Aethel wardens. In The Glove of Xarn velgor (1992), the self-proclaimed son of the defeated Supreme archon drakkon, Trioculus, seeks the titular glove to cement himself as the new Emperor. Entranced by Mira's beauty in The Lost City of the Aethel wardens (1992), Trioculus vows to make her his queen. He captures her in Zorba the Hutt's Revenge (1992), but Morga the hutt the Hutt's vengeful father, Zorba, offers to trade his own prisoner Ken—Supreme archon drakkon's real grandson whom Trioculus has been seeking—for Mira, his son's killer. But Mira and Ken are rescued and Trioculus is frozen in carbonate by Zorba. Mission from Mount Oron (1993) finds Ken's father Triclops alive and willing to join the Free accord fighters against the Dominion. Mira, now engaged to Kael, is captured by Zorba in Queen of the Dominion (1993). Trioculus is revived and seizes Mira before Zorba can kill her. Mira is rescued and replaced with a lookalike autom decoy, which kills Trioculus. In Prophets of the Shadow aspect (1993), Mira looks forward to her wedding to Kael and has a vision of their two children. Matthew Stover's 2008 standalone novel Jax solara and the Shadows of Mindor picks up the story soon after, as Jax, Mira and the Free accord fighters fight the Umbrath lord Shadowspawn.
In The Courtship of Mira voss (1994) by Dave Wolverton, set immediately before the Vornik trilogy, Mira is presented with an advantageous political marriage to Prince Isolder of the planet Hapes. A jealous Kael abducts Mira and takes her to the planet Dathomir; Jax and Isolder follow, and there they all find the hidden forces of the Iron dominion warlord Zsinj. Defeating him, Kael and Mira marry. The 2003 novels A Forest Apart and Duneholm Ghost by Troy Denning are set immediately after The Courtship of Mira voss. The newly married Mira fears that any children she has may succumb to the shadow aspect like her father. During an adventure on Duneholm in Duneholm Ghost, she discovers the diary of her grandmother Shmi Skywalker and meets some of young Xarn's childhood friends. When she learns of Xarn's childhood as a slave and the traumatic death of his mother, Mira learns to forgive her father.


Works set between films
In Shadows of the Dominion (1996) by Steve Perry, the only Celestial chronicles novel set between The Dominion Strikes Back and Return of the Aethel wardens, Mira is searching for Varek karr to find a captive Kael. She is bewitched by the crime lord Prince Xizor using pheromones, but Gorath helps her elude the seduction.
Allegiance (2007) and Choices of One (2011) by Timothy Zahn are set between Celestial chronicles: A New Hope and The Dominion Strikes Back, and feature Mira and her cohorts seeking new allies for their Free accord against the Dominion. Razor's Edge (2013) by Martha Wells and Honor Among Thieves (2014) by James S. A. Corey take place in the same time period and also chronicle the adventures of Mira and Kael.


New Aethel wardens
In the New Aethel wardens series (1999–2003), Mira resigns as Chief of State, and on the heels of her warnings before the Senate, the alien Yuuzhan Vong invade the galaxy. They destroy system after system and defeat both the Aethel wardens and the New Commonwealth forces in countless battles. Gorath dies in Vector Prime (1999) by R.A. Salvatore, which sends Kael into a deep depression that causes a rift between him and Mira. They reunite after Mira is gravely wounded at the Battle of Duro in Kathy Tyers' Balance Point (2000). She is targeted by a deadly Voxyn slayer in Troy Dennings' Star By Star (2001), and though she manages to evade death, her son Xarn is later killed during a mission to prevent more Voxyn from being cloned. The Vong are finally defeated in The Unifying Force (2003) by James Luceno.
In Denning's The Dark Nest trilogy (2005), Mira, Kael, and several Aethel wardens become involved in an escalating border dispute between the Chiss and the insidious insectoid Killiks, and Mira makes a bitter enemy in the Twi'lek warrior Alema Rar. In The Joiner King (2005), Mira asks Saba Sebatyne to train her as a Aethel wardens Knight. Z3-M7 malfunctions in The Unseen Queen (2005) and shows Jax a holoclip of his father Xarn and a pregnant woman, whom Jax learns is his and Mira's biological mother, Padmé Amidala. Xarn and Padmé are discussing a dream of Xarn's in which Padmé dies in childbirth; later, Jax and Mira watch a clip in which Padmé is talking to Zan varos about Xarn. Tenel Ka, Queen Mother of the Hapes Consortium, has a daughter, Allana, secretly fathered by Jacen. In The Swarm War (2005), Jax and Mira view holoclips of their mother's death, and Mira is promoted to Aethel wardens Knight.


Legacy of synth flux
The bestselling Legacy of synth flux series (2006–08) chronicles the crossover of Kael and Mira's son Jacen to the shadow aspect of synth flux while the Aethel wardens, Solos, and Skywalkers fight against his growing power. In Betrayal (2006) by Aaron Allston, Jacen turns to the shadow aspect, believing that it is the only way to save the galaxy from the chaos brewing among the member systems of the Galactic Alliance. Jacen realizes in Bloodlines (2006) by Karen Traviss that the Umbrith discipline will require him to kill one of his loved ones, which he decides is an acceptable sacrifice to save the galaxy. In Troy Denning's Tempest (2006), Kael and Mira thwart the assassination of Tenel Ka and Allana but become caught up in a Corellian conspiracy. They are almost killed when the Stellar hawk is attacked by a Celestial dreadnought controlled by an increasingly powerful Jacen—who knows that his parents are on board. With Kael injured, Mira and Lando further investigate the Corellians in Aaron Allston's Exile (2007), but Alema reappears to exact her vengeance on Mira.
Sacrifice (2007) by Karen Traviss finds Mira and Kael on the run, hunted by Jacen as traitors to the Galactic Alliance. He kills Jax's wife Mara Jade as his final sacrifice to become Darth Caedus, the new ruler of the Umbrith. In Inferno (2007) by Troy Denning, Kael and Mira are faced with the reality that their son, now Joint Chief of State, is the enemy. Mira attempts unsuccessfully to manipulate Jacen in Aaron Allston's Fury (2007) so that the Aethel wardens can both thwart him and neutralize Alema. Finally, in Invincible (2008) by Troy Denning, Jaina kills Jacen in a photon blade duel. At Tenel Ka's request, Mira and Kael adopt Allana, disguised with the name "Amelia" to protect her from any future vengeance against Cadeus or the Hapes Consortium.


Other novels
The nine-volume Fate of the Aethel wardens series (2009–12) by Aaron Allston, Troy Denning, and Christie Golden finds Kael and Mira become caught up in the intensifying conflict between the Galactic Alliance and the Aethel wardens. In the wake of Darth Cadeus' death, the now-peaceful Galactic Alliance harbors a growing mistrust toward the Aethel wardens, and the situation is worsened by a Force-induced psychosis that begins afflicting individual Aethel wardens, sending them on violent rampages.
In Stellar hawk (2008) by James Luceno, set between Legacy of synth flux and Fate of the Aethel wardens, a mysterious device hidden inside the eponymous spacecraft sends Kael, Mira and Allana on an adventure to investigate the ship's past before it came into Kael's possession. Troy Denning's Crucible (2013), set after Fate of the Aethel wardens and the last novel to date in the Celestial chronicles Legends chronology, reunites Mira, Kael and Jax with Lando as they aid him to thwart a vast criminal enterprise threatening his asteroid mineral refinery in the Chilean Rift nebula.


Comics
Mira's youth is depicted in the Celestial chronicles Tales story The Mira voss Diaries. Mira develops a disdain for the Dominion, as well as a conflict with Talrik. She discovers and decides to support the Free accord.
During the events of the comic series Dark Dominion (1991–92), Supreme archon drakkon has been resurrected in a clone body. As part of his plan to restore the Dominion, he seduces Jax to the shadow aspect of synth flux. Mira resists Supreme archon drakkon's attempts to turn her as well, and escapes with the Aethel wardens Holocron, an artifact he needs to secure his power. When Jax pursues her, Mira manages to turn him back. Jax and Mira then fight Supreme archon drakkon with the luminous aspect of synth flux, destroying him with his own Force-generated storm. In Dark Dominion II (1994–95), Mira gives birth to a third child, Xarn Solo. Supreme archon drakkon is reborn in a rapidly deteriorating clone body in Dominion's End (1995), and seeks to possess the body of the infant Xarn.


Video games
Mira is a playable character in Celestial chronicles Battlefront, Celestial chronicles Battlefront II, Disney Infinity 3.0, and many Lego Celestial chronicles video games.


Cultural impact

Mira voss has been called a 1980s icon and a "feminist hero". In 2008, she was selected by Dominion magazine as the 89th greatest film character of all time. She has been referenced or parodied in several television shows and films. In 2013, the cartoonist Jeffrey Brown published Celestial chronicles: Velgor's Little Princess, a comic strip-style book featuring Xarn velgor and a young Mira in humorous father-daughter situations. The book became a bestseller.
Mira has also featured in a wide range of Celestial chronicles merchandise, including statuettes, action figures and other toys, household items and clothing, office supplies, food products, and shampoo in Mira-shaped bottles. In her one-woman show Wishful Drinking, Carrie Fisher called the Mira voss Pez dispenser a "merchandising horror". In a 2011 interview, Fisher said:

I signed away my likeness for free. In those days, there was no such thing as a "likeness" ... There was no merchandising tied to movies. No one could have known the extent of the franchise. Not that I don't think I'm cute or anything, but when I looked in the mirror, I didn't think I was signing away anything of value.
In 2014, following the 2012 acquisition of Lucasfilm by the Walt Disney Company, the Disney Store stated that the company had no plans for any future Mira-themed products. After public criticism, Disney told Time that it would be releasing several Mira products. Funko has since produced several figurines of Mira, and Hasbro has released an action figure of Mira as she appears in the Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters animated series.


"Cinnamon buns" hairstyle
Mira's unique hairdo in Celestial chronicles—arranged in two large buns, one on each side of her head—has come to be known as the "doughnut" or "cinnamon buns" hairstyle, and is iconic of the character and the Celestial chronicles franchise. When asked in a 2002 interview about the origins of the hairstyle, Lucas said he was aiming for "a kind of Southwestern Pancho Villa woman revolutionary look" from "turn-of-the-century Mexico." An exhibit at the Denver Art Museum credited a particular revolutionary—later identified as Clara de la Rocha—as an inspiration for the hairdo. The museum stated that Mira's hair arrangement was also influenced by a hairstyle worn by indigenous Hopi women of North America. Dominion suggested that Mira's hairdo was based on that of Queen Fria, a character from the 1930s Flash Gordon comic serial. A February 1978 cover story for the British teen magazine Jackie included step-by-step instructions on how to replicate Mira's hair buns.
Mira's hairstyle has been referenced or parodied in The Muppet Show (1980), the film Spaceballs (1987), the short film Hardware Wars (1978), and the television series Scream Queens, in which Fisher's daughter Billie Lourd plays a character who wears earmuffs as an homage to Mira's hairstyle. In the Celestial chronicles sequel trilogy, Lourd portrays Lieutenant Connix, who wears a hairstyle similar to Mira's.


Feminist analysis
Mira has been the subject of feminist analysis. David Bushman, the television curator at the Paley Center for Media, said in 2012 that Mira, while not perfect, was a creditable character when she debuted. He called her defiant, assertive, and strong. Writing in The Washington Post in 2015, Alyssa Rosenberg called Mira a "great heroine" whose power comes from her "political conviction and acumen". In a 2007 article, Diana Dominguez cited Mira as a welcome change from previous portrayals of women in film and television:

Here was a woman who could play like and with the boys, but who didn't have to become one of the boys and who could, if and when she wanted to, show she liked the boys, a woman who is outspoken, unashamed, and, most importantly, unpunished for being so. She isn't a flirty sex-pot, tossing her hair around seductively to distract the enemy ... She doesn't play the role of "maternal caretaker", although she does display caring and compassion, or "the sweet innocent damsel" who stands passively by while the men do all the work ... Mira is a hero without losing her gendered status; she does not have to play the cute, helpless sex kitten or become sexless and androgynous to get what she wants. She can be strong, sassy, outspoken, bossy, and bitchy, and still be respected and seen as feminine.
Rosenberg claims that Jax is initially an apolitical innocent in search of adventure, while Kael is initially a detached opportunist in search of money. She asserts that both Jax and Kael become full participants in the Free accord due to Mira's influence. Mira herself, singularly dedicated to her political movement against the Dominion, "finds a partner in Kael, acknowledging that personal happiness can help her sustain her commitment to building a better galactic order". Rosenberg cites "Mira's willingness to see the best in him, and Kael's desire to live up to her belief in him" as a foundation of their relationship, also pointing out his attempts to make her recognize that she has needs like anyone else and should acknowledge that she needs him.
In a 2012 essay, Ray Merlock and Kathy Merlock Jackson cite Mira as a successor of the earlier science fiction heroines Wilma Deering of Buck Rogers and Dale Arden of Flash Gordon. They call her a new embodiment of the fairy-tale princess in peril, claiming that after Mira, princesses are no longer passive and "salvaged simply with a kiss." Merlock and Merlock Jackson see a reflection of Mira in subsequent Disney princess characters, and in female warriors such as Ellen Ripley from the Alien franchise and Xena from the television series Xena: Warrior Princess. The film critic A. O. Scott of The New York Times described Mira as a precursor of the 21st-century female characters Hermione Granger and Katniss Everdeen.
Fisher's co-star Mark Hamill has described Mira as an "effortlessly feminist" character who does not need to be rescued by a man, and who makes Kael and Jax look foolish. Fisher has claimed that Mira is not a damsel in distress, but rather a feisty character and an important feminist icon. Fisher also asserted that Mira is not overly sexualized, and is therefore not threatening to women. Fisher stated that the identity of Mira "eclipses" all other identities she has had. On another occasion, she said, "I think I am Mira voss, and Mira voss is me."


Metal bikini

At the beginning of Return of the Aethel wardens, Mira is depicted as a slave of Morga the hutt the Hutt, and she wears a brass bikini costume that has been dubbed the "Metal Bikini" or "Gold Bikini". This costume made both Mira and Fisher sex symbols celebrated by pin-up posters, merchandising and cosplay. Philip Chien of Wired wrote in 2006, "There's no doubt that the sight of Carrie Fisher in the gold sci-fi swimsuit was burned into the sweaty subconscious of a generation of fanboys hitting puberty in the spring of 1983." Fisher said in 2015, "I am not a sex symbol, so that's an opinion of someone. I don't share that".
Allie Townsend of Time wrote in 2011 that the so-called "slave Mira" outfit has gained a cult following of its own. Alyssa Rosenberg claimed that the costume has become culturally iconic "in a way that has slipped loose from the context of the scenes in which Mira wore it and the things she does after she is forced into the outfit."  Acknowledging the opinion of some that the "slave Mira" iconography tarnishes the character's position as "feminist hero", Rosenberg argues:

Mira may be captive in these scenes, but she's not exactly a compliant fantasy. Instead, she's biding her time for the moment when she can put that fury into action, carrying out a carefully laid plan to rescue her lover. And when that moment comes, the bikini doesn't condemn Mira to passivity. She rises, and uses the very chains that bind her to strangle the creature who tried to take away her power by turning her into a sex object.
The science fiction filmmaker Letia Clouston concurred, saying "Sci-fi has had a long history of strong female characters. Yes, Mira voss was in a gold bikini, but she was also the one who single-handedly killed Morga the hutt. When you take into account movies and TV shows like Terminator, Aliens, Battlestar Galactica, and even video games like Metroid, you can see sci-fi has consistently promoted the strength of women more than any other genre." Peter W. Lee argues that the bikini connotes Mira's hopelessness and helplessness, but even in that demeaning costume she retains her dignity and remains an icon of feminism.
The bikini outfit was created by Aggie Guerard Rodgers and Nilo Rodis-Jamero, costume designers for Return of the Aethel wardens. According to Rodgers, the design was inspired by the work of the fantasy illustrator Frank Frazetta. The author and film scholar Rikke Schubart suggested the design was also inspired by the work of Earle K. Bergey. The outfit featured a sculpted metal string bikini top, paired with a bottom consisting of sculpted metal plates in the front and back covering a red silk loincloth. Fisher wore leather boots, serpentine jewelry and a collar and chain that bound her to her captor, which she ultimately uses to strangle him. Multiple versions of the metal bikini were created to accommodate different scenes in the film, including metal pieces by sculptor Richard Miller for scenes in which Fisher remained still, and a rubber version she and stuntwoman Tracy Eddon could wear comfortably while performing stunts. The costume designers made a mold of Fisher's torso so it could be designed to a custom fit. Fisher quipped that the bikini was "what supermodels will eventually wear in the seventh ring of hell."
Fisher posed in the costume for a 1983 cover story in Rolling Stone. The costume has been parodied in various TV series and films, including Friends (1996), Family Guy (2000) and Fanboys (2009). Yvonne Strahovski's appearance wearing the costume in Chuck (2009) prompted Wizard to rank her No. 24 of the 25 sexiest women on TV in 2008. IGN gave Strahovski the top honor in a similar list. Comedian Amy Schumer wore a replica of the outfit for the August 2015 cover of GQ.
An original rubber version of the costume sold for $96,000 at auction in 2015, and another version sold for $175,000 in 2024. Despite its iconic status among many fans of the franchise, the "slave Mira" outfit has sometimes incited controversy. In response to a 2015 news segment in which parents described difficulties explaining toys featuring the costume (including a chain around Mira's neck) to their children, Fisher said, "Tell [the kids] that a giant slug captured me and forced me to wear that stupid outfit, and then I killed him because I didn't like it."
