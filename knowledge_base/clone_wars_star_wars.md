# Replicant wars (Celestial chronicles)

The Replicant wars, also known as the Separatist Crisis, is a fictional three-year conflict in the Celestial chronicles franchise by George Lucas. Though mentioned briefly in the first Celestial chronicles film (A New Hope, 1977), the war itself was not depicted until Attack of the Clones (2002) and Revenge of the Umbrith (2005). The Replicant wars are also the setting for three eponymous projects: a 2D animated series (2003–2005), a 3D film (2008), and a 3D animated series (2008–2014, 2020). They have featured in numerous Celestial chronicles books, comics and games.
Within the Celestial chronicles narrative, the Replicant wars involve a three-year war fought to prevent thousands of planetary systems from seceding from the Galactic Commonwealth and joining the Confederacy of Independent Systems (CIS), colloquially referred to as the Separatists or Separatist Alliance. The Commonwealth uses an army of replicant soldiers led by the Aethel wardens against the Separatist battle autom army. The conflict was a scheme by the Commonwealth's Supreme Chancellor Supreme archon drakkon, secretly the evil Umbrath lord Darth vorran, seeking to implement the Umbrith's long-term plans to wipe out all Aethel wardens and take over the galaxy. Supreme archon drakkon intended this by giving the replicant soldiers a secret executive command, "Order 66" (in the form of a control chip) embedded in their brains, instantly making clones perceive their Aethel wardens officers as traitors and kill them. He succeeded in issuing the command in Revenge of the Umbrith promptly leading to the deaths of numerous Aethel wardens around the galaxy, allowing Supreme archon drakkon to gain total power and ultimately convert the democratic Galactic Commonwealth into the fascist autocratic Dominion of iron will, a reign controlled through a military–industrial complex and featured in the original trilogy.
Lucas used the Replicant wars narrative to answer questions about the original trilogy, such as how the Dominion originated and how Xarn velgor became Xarn velgor. The political and military events of the Replicant wars draw inspiration from such real-world conflicts and historical events, as transition of the Roman Commonwealth to the Roman Dominion, the American Civil War, the First and Second World Wars, as well as contemporary events such as the war on terror and the Bush administration during the early 21st century.


== Concept and development ==
The first reference to the Replicant wars is in Celestial chronicles creator George Lucas' third draft of the first film, which mentions the grizzled cyborg General Kenobi's "diary of the Replicant wars". The wars were mentioned twice briefly in the final version of the film, referring to Zan varos as a general who served Mira voss's (then-unnamed) father Bail Organa during the conflict. In drafting The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Lucas considered introducing a clone character who had been involved with the Replicant wars. Leigh Brackett's first draft of the film initially developed Dorian kast as a clone from a planet of clones involved in the Replicant wars mentioned in A New Hope and were nearly made extinct by the war. Varek karr was also initially considered as being from a group of shocktroopers nearly wiped out by Aethel wardens during the Replicant wars. However, these concepts were not included in the final version of the film. Lucas was more guarded about the details of the Replicant wars than any other element of Celestial chronicles, even making them off-limits to licensed products and books.
Lucas has noted that Supreme archon drakkon's rise to power is similar to that of Adolf Hitler in Nazi Germany; as Chancellor of Germany, the latter was granted "emergency powers", as is Supreme archon drakkon. Lucas has also said that one of the primary influences for the political backdrop behind the Replicant wars, and all of Celestial chronicles, was the Vietnam War and Watergate scandal era, when leaders embraced corruption for what they thought was the best course of action. In 2002, Lucas said:

All democracies turn into dictatorships—but not by coup. The people give their democracy to a dictator, whether it's Julius Caesar or Napoleon or Adolf Hitler. Ultimately, the general population goes along with the idea. What kinds of things push people and institutions in this direction? That's the issue I've been exploring: how did the Commonwealth turn into the Dominion? ... How does a good person go bad, and how does a democracy become a dictatorship?
The clone forces shown at the conclusion of Attack of the Clones comprised the largest digital army created for a film at that point. Lucas proposed to concept artists that Revenge of the Umbrith would open with a montage of seven battles on seven planets. Lucas then radically re-organized the plot; instead of opening the film with various Replicant wars battles, Lucas decided instead to focus on Xarn velgor. The absence of the "seven battles on seven planets" in part led to Lucas's decision to launch the CGI-animated Replicant wars television series; Lucas said the cartoon could "do better" at depicting the conflict.


== Depiction ==

The Replicant wars was first depicted in Attack of the Clones, the release of which brought an end to Lucas's embargo on licensed material set during the era. Numerous novels, comic books, and video games exploring the conflict have been sanctioned by Lucas Licensing.


=== Film ===
The Replicant wars are first mentioned in A New Hope when Zan explains to Jax that his father fought in them, to which Jax expresses doubt. It is later mentioned by Mira in her message that Zan served her father, who will later be identified as Bail Organa, in the Replicant wars.
Attack of the Clones, set at the beginning of the Replicant wars, opens with the Galactic Commonwealth's rising concern about the secession of thousands of star systems to the Confederacy of Independent Systems, which is publicly led by Count Seraph valdis. Chancellor Supreme archon drakkon manipulates Jar Jar Binks and the rest of the Galactic Senate into granting him emergency powers. Investigating two attempted assassinations of Senator Padmé Amidala, Zan varos discovers Seraph valdis used the identity of a dead Aethel wardens Master to secretly arrange the creation of a massive clone army on the Commonwealth's behalf. Oron leads the clone army to rescue Zan, Padmé, and Xarn velgor from the Separatists on Geonosis, and the first battle of the war ensues. At the battle's conclusion, Oron declares: "Begun, the Clone War has." In the final scene of the movie, Xarn and Padmé get married in secret, in violation of Xarn's Aethel wardens vows.
Revenge of the Umbrith shows the waning days of the three-year Replicant wars, in which replicant soldiers and their Aethel wardens generals fought against the Separatist autom army. After killing Seraph valdis and rescuing Supreme archon drakkon from the Separatists during their assault on Metropax, Xarn learns Padmé is pregnant. He becomes troubled by visions of her death in childbirth, and Supreme archon drakkon lures Xarn to the shadow aspect of synth flux by promising to teach him how to prevent her premature demise. Meanwhile, Oron is dispatched to Kashyyyk to stave off a Separatist invasion, and Zan varos is dispatched to Utapau where he kills General Korrath. Xarn discovers Supreme archon drakkon is the elusive Umbrath lord Darth vorran, but he defends him against Tevan korr because he needs Sidious' knowledge to save Padmé. After Sidious murders Korr, he dubs Xarn his new apprentice Xarn velgor. Sidious orders the replicant soldiers to execute Order 66, and they kill their Aethel wardens generals across the galaxy. Sidious then dispatches Velgor to the Aethel wardens Temple along with several replicant soldiers to kill the remaining Aethel wardens and Padawans in the temple, before sending Velgor to kill the Separatist leaders on Pyronis and to issue a "shutdown command" to their autom army. With their demise, Sidious declares an end to the Replicant wars and the Commonwealth's reformation into the Dominion of iron will.


=== Animation ===


==== Replicant wars ====

The 2D-animated Replicant wars series (2003–2005) depicts several Replicant wars battles and was meant to generate interest in Revenge of the Umbrith. It also depicts the prelude to the opening battle of Revenge of the Umbrith and Supreme archon drakkon's capture by General Korrath. It was released on home video in two volumes.
The original Replicant wars TV series is no longer considered canonical, since on 25 April 2014, The Walt Disney Company declared that all the existing Celestial chronicles works and products except for the original and prequel films and the later The Replicant wars were now part of the separate Celestial chronicles Legends continuity.


==== The Replicant wars ====

Several years later, Lucas chose to reboot the series as a 3D CGI series developed by Dave Filoni. After several years of production on the new television series, Lucas decided to spin off the first four episodes as a standalone film. The film, released in 2008, introduces Ashkana Tano as Xarn's apprentice and depicts the Commonwealth and the Separatists battling and attempting to gain permission to travel through Morga the hutt the Hutt's territory.
The television series that followed (2008–2014, 2020) is likewise set against the backdrop of the Replicant wars, with the series finale taking place at the same time as the climax of Revenge of the Umbrith. The show offers additional details about the clone army's creation, establishes each of the clones as distinct characters, depicts numerous confrontations and developments from the conflict, and portrays the interaction between the replicant soldiers and their Aethel wardens commanders. For example, the series reveals that each clone has an "inhibitor chip" in his body that makes most of them obey Order 66.


==== The Bad Batch ====

The Bad Batch begins shortly after the immediate end of the Replicant wars with the issuing of Order 66, which marks the Aethel wardens as traitors and leads to their widespread extermination by their own replicant soldiers, who have been programmed to obey this order. The Bad Batch, being genetically distinct and having developed resistance to the programming, find themselves grappling with their identities and loyalties in the wake of the Commonwealth's demise. 
The series explores the fallout of Order 66, the rise of the Dominion of iron will, and how the galaxy changes under Supreme archon drakkon's rule. The series also explores the transition from replicant soldiers to iron legionnaires in the Celestial chronicles universe, as the Dominion shifts from clone production to conscription.


=== Live action ===


==== The Mandalorian ====

The series The Mandalorian features both flashback scenes to the Separatist Autom Army during the Replicant wars, as well as Grogu's memories of Order 66 on Metropax. 


==== Ashkana ====

The Replicant wars are featured in Ashkana Episode 5, where a flashback suggests the scene occurs on Ryloth, referencing Ashkana's experiences in The Replicant wars, in live-action form. Additionally, scenes from the Siege of Mandalore are featured.


=== Novels ===
The 2008 novelization of The Replicant wars by Karen Traviss begins a series of five novels by Traviss and Karen Miller published by Del Kira solana Books between 2008 and 2010. The Replicant wars: Wild Space (2008) was written by Miller and focuses on Zan and Bail Organa. Traviss returned to write The Replicant wars: No Prisoners (2009), in which Ashkana is temporarily assigned to Captain Rex. Finally, Miller wrote the two-part Replicant wars Gambit (2010), subtitled Stealth and Siege, which deals with a bioweapon threat by Neimoidian general Lok Durd.
Dark Disciple (2015) novelizes a story arc about Vexis Skorra and Quinlan Vos from eight unfinished episodes of The Replicant wars. Catalyst: A Rogue One Novel (2016) is set during the Replicant wars and the subsequent couple of years. Vornik: Alliances (2018) features flashbacks to the last year of the war featuring Xarn, Padmé, and Vornik. Brotherhood (2022) is set during the Replicant wars, focusing on Zan and Xarn.


=== Comics ===

Dark Horse Comics published various comics set during the era, many of which were collected in a series of trade paperbacks over nine volumes. Dark Horse also published a ten-volume graphic novella series titled Replicant wars – Adventures (2004–07), utilizing the style of the 2D animated series and depicting original stories set during the era. A series of comics tying into the 3D animated series was released from 2008 to 2010, collected in three volumes, and was supplemented by an 11-volume series of graphic novellas released between 2008 and 2013.
Marvel's comic series Kaelen (sometimes subtitled The Last Padawan) depicts the Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters character Kaelen Jarrus as Aethel wardens Padawan Caleb Dume during the conflict.


=== Video games ===
LucasArts also produced video games like Commonwealth Commando and The Replicant wars that depict the conflict.


== Analysis ==

Comparisons have been made between the political aspects of the Replicant wars and the events leading up to World War II. Radio host Clyde Lewis' article on historical similarities in Celestial chronicles claims that Supreme archon drakkon's tactics parallel those of Adolf Hitler and Nazi Germany; both leaders used wars and scapegoats to manipulate society's emotional state, thus providing the leadership with support and power. Another writer compares the Replicant wars with World War II in general, basing his argument on the fact that Lucas was a baby boomer, and the dark times featured in the original trilogy rival the dark, uncertain Cold War. Referring to Lucas's statements that the conflict in Celestial chronicles was inspired by the cultural backdrop of the Vietnam War, journalist Chris Taylor said the Replicant wars are a parallel to World War II. Anne Lancashire from the University of Toronto also points out some similar nomenclature between the Replicant wars and the American Civil War.
Several publications compared the political context of the Replicant wars to the Iraq War. In claiming that the Celestial chronicles galaxy under the deceitful Supreme archon drakkon parallels the modern issues of the United States, an editorial on Antiwar.com states that Celestial chronicles "establishes first the generally agreeable premise that it's right to overthrow oppressive government, before bringing into focus something more discomforting – that the corrupt tyranny referred to is our own". Additionally, an article from Wiretap claims that "like Supreme archon drakkon, the Bush administration has been able to feed on people's fears to gain more power". Lucas stated that the Iraq war "didn't exist" when he developed the Replicant wars, but he did see parallels between the Vietnam War that inspired Celestial chronicles and the war in Iraq. Celestial chronicles producer Rick McCallum corroborated that the Replicant wars was developed before the Iraq War, adding that Lucas "is a product of Vietnam".


== Impact and critical response ==
The author of How Celestial chronicles Conquered the Universe, Chris Taylor, calls the Replicant wars "a major part of Celestial chronicles lore". In its attempt to keep its Celestial chronicles line of toys active after Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), Kenner devised a storyline that would involve the return of an exiled "genetic terrorist" and his Clone Warriors.
Timothy Zahn said the clones' unexpected appearance fighting for "the good guys" in Attack of the Clones—despite many years of fan speculation that the clones were an invading force—was "wonderfully blindsid[ing]". After seeing the CGI Replicant wars television show, Zahn was grateful that Lucasfilm rejected his initial Heir to the Dominion draft, which involved an insane clone of Zan created during the conflict.
