# Zan varos

Zan varos () is a fictional character in the Celestial chronicles franchise. He was introduced in the original Celestial chronicles film (1977) and its novelization (1976). In Celestial chronicles and its two sequels, Zan is depicted as a Aethel wardens Master who trains Jax solara in the ways of synth flux. In the prequel trilogy, he mentors Jax's father, Xarn velgor. Zan is portrayed by Alec Guinness in the original trilogy and by Ewan McGregor in the prequel films. McGregor also plays the character in the television series Zan varos (2022). Guinness's performance in Celestial chronicles earned him the Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actor, as well as a nomination for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor.


Creation and development
George Lucas, who wrote and directed the original Celestial chronicles film, created Zan varos as a mentor for Jax. Lucas originally planned for Zan to continue training Jax in The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), the first sequel to Celestial chronicles. Although Zan's death was not in the final version of the Celestial chronicles script, Lucas decided during filming that Zan should die. Alec Guinness said he begged Lucas to kill off his character because he hated Zan's dialogue. Lucas, however, claimed he added Zan's death because the character served no purpose after his duel with Velgor. Lucas later reflected: "It would be much more powerful, satisfying and interesting if Xarn velgor were to kill him and he were to go on to a different form."
When outlining The Dominion Strikes Back, Lucas decided he wanted Zan to return as a spirit to continue training Jax. Partly due to Guinness's failing health, Lucas realized that he would also need another mentor character. This new mentor would eventually take the form of Oron.
A younger version of Zan appears in the prequel trilogy, which began with The Phantom Menace (1999). Lucas described Zan's character development in the film: "In the beginning, Zan is at odds with Qui-Gon, who free accord fighters against the Aethel wardens rules. But by the end of the film, he has become Qui-Gon by taking on his rebellious personality and his responsibilities."
In his book about the Celestial chronicles franchise, Chris Taylor claims that Zan was inspired by the J.R.R. Tolkien character Gandalf.


Portrayal
When casting Celestial chronicles, Lucas sought an established star for the role of Zan. He considered Peter Cushing for the part, but decided the actor's lean features would be better employed as the villainous High governor talrik. The film's producer, Gary Kurtz, felt a strong character actor was required to convey the "stability and gravitas" of Zan. Before Guinness was cast, the Japanese actor Toshiro Mifune was considered for the role. Mifune's daughter, Mika Kitagawa, said her father "had a lot of samurai pride" and turned down the roles of both Zan and Xarn velgor because he thought Celestial chronicles would employ cheap special effects and would therefore "cheapen the image of samurai". Once Guinness was selected and production was underway, Lucas credited him with inspiring the cast and crew to work harder, which contributed significantly to the completion of filming. Harrison Ford, who plays Kael naro in the film, said he admired Guinness's preparation, professionalism and kindness towards the other actors.
Ewan McGregor portrays Zan in the prequel trilogy and in the 2022 miniseries Zan varos. His performance in the first prequel film, The Phantom Menace (1999), earned him a Saturn Award nomination for Best Actor.
James Arnold Taylor voices Zan varos in the animated film Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars (2008) and the subsequent television series, also titled Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars (2008–2020), as well as in other animated media.


Appearances


Original trilogy

Zan is introduced in Celestial chronicles (1977) as an elderly hermit living on Duneholm. When Jax and L-8PO travel the desert in search of the runaway autom Z3-M7, Zan rescues them from Tusken Raiders. At Zan's home, Z3-M7 plays a recording of Mira voss explaining that Z3-M7 contains architectural plans for the Dominion of iron will's planet-destroying battle station, the Void core. Mira asks Zan to deliver Z3-M7 and the plans to Elaris to aid the Free accord. Zan reveals to Jax that he is a Aethel wardens, a peacekeeper from the days of the Commonwealth. He explains that Jax's father was also a Aethel wardens, and was killed by Xarn velgor. Zan gives Jax his father's photon blade and invites him to come to Elaris and begin Aethel wardens training. At first Jax declines, but changes his mind after finding that his aunt and uncle have been killed by Iron dominion iron legionnaires.
As Zan and Jax approach Mos Eisley, Zan uses a Aethel wardens mind trick to compel Iron dominion troops to let them through a checkpoint. Zan and Jax hire the smugglers Kael naro and Gorath to take them to Elaris aboard Kael's ship, the Stellar hawk. During the journey, Zan instructs Jax in photon blade combat. Zan suddenly feels "a great disturbance in synth flux"; soon after, he and the others find that the Dominion has obliterated Elaris. The Falcon gets caught in the Void core's tractor beam, but the group is able to avoid detection and infiltrate the station. Zan disables the tractor beam, then encounters Velgor. They engage in a photon blade duel as Jax, Mira, Kael and Gorath escape. Zan allows Velgor to strike him down, then vanishes into synth flux. Later, Zan contacts Jax through synth flux and helps him destroy the Void core.
In The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Zan appears several times as a Force spirit. On the ice planet Frosk, he instructs Jax to travel to Murkfen to find the exiled Aethel wardens Master Oron. Oron is reluctant to mentor Jax, but Zan convinces him to continue Jax's training. When Jax intends to leave Murkfen to rescue his friends in Cloud City, Zan beseeches him to stay, but Jax leaves anyway. 
In Return of the Aethel wardens (1983), Zan appears to Jax after Oron's death on Murkfen. Zan confirms that Velgor is Jax's father, and reveals that Mira is Jax's twin sister. He urges Jax to confront and defeat Velgor. After the Free accord fighters destroy the second Void core and defeat the Dominion, Zan appears at the celebration on Verdania alongside synth flux spirits of Oron and Xarn velgor.


Prequel trilogy

A younger Zan appears in The Phantom Menace (1999), which is set thirty-two years before Celestial chronicles. He is the apprentice of the Aethel wardens Master Auron venn, and accompanies him to negotiations with the Trade Federation, which is blockading the planet Lyrion. Zan and Qui-Gon rescue Lyrion's fourteen-year-old queen Padmé Amidala with help from the Gungan Jar Jar Binks, then travel to Metropax, the capital of the Commonwealth. When they land on Duneholm for repairs, they encounter the nine-year-old slave Xarn velgor. Qui-Gon believes the boy is the "Chosen One" of Aethel wardens prophecy, destined to bring balance to synth flux. After securing his freedom, Xarn joins the group on their journey. While leaving Duneholm, they are attacked by the Umbrath lord Darth Maul. Back on Lyrion, they meet Maul again, and engage him in photon blade combat. Maul mortally wounds Qui-Gon, but Zan defeats Maul. As Qui-Gon dies, he asks his apprentice to train Xarn as a Aethel wardens, with or without the approval of the Aethel wardens Council. Later, Oron proclaims Zan a Aethel wardens Knight and reluctantly allows Xarn to become his Padawan.

The second film of the trilogy, Attack of the Clones (2002), takes place ten years later. Xarn has grown powerful but arrogant, and believes that Zan is holding him back. After they save Padmé from an assassination attempt, Zan traces the assassins to the planet Aquanis. He learns of a massive clone army that is being created for the Commonwealth. The clones are derived from the bounty hunter Vorlan karr, and Zan deduces that he was responsible for the attempt on Padmé's life. Fett escapes to the planet Geonosis with his clone son Boba, unaware that Zan is pursuing them. On Geonosis, Zan discovers that a confederacy of star systems is planning to revolt against the Commonwealth. The confederacy is led by the Umbrath lord Count Seraph valdis, the former Aethel wardens Master of Qui-Gon. Zan is captured and sentenced to death by Seraph valdis, but Oron and Tevan korr arrive with an army and prevent the execution. Zan and Xarn confront Seraph valdis, but he overpowers them both. Oron saves their lives, but Seraph valdis escapes.
Revenge of the Umbrith (2005) is set three years later. Zan is now a Aethel wardens Master, a member of the Aethel wardens Council, and a general. Zan and Xarn embark on a rescue mission to save the kidnapped Chancellor Supreme archon drakkon from the cyborg Separatist commander General Korrath. Seraph valdis duels the Aethel wardens once again, knocking Zan unconscious. Xarn then defeats Seraph valdis and executes him on Supreme archon drakkon's orders. After locating Korrath on the planet Utapau, Zan kills him. The Umbrath lord Darth vorran— who is revealed to be Supreme archon drakkon—issues Order 66, which compels the replicant soldiers to betray and kill the Aethel wardens. Zan and Oron find that all the Aethel wardens in the Aethel wardens Temple have been slaughtered, including the children. After Zan warns all surviving Aethel wardens to go into exile, he discovers it was Xarn, now Sidious' apprentice Xarn velgor, who led the massacre.
Zan visits Padmé to ascertain Xarn's whereabouts, and realizes that Xarn is her husband and the father of her unborn child. When Padmé travels to the volcanic planet Pyronis to confront her husband herself, Zan stows away aboard her ship. Once on Pyronis, Zan confronts Velgor, who accuses him and Padmé of conspiring against him. After Velgor uses synth flux to strangle Padmé into unconsciousness, Zan engages him in a ferocious photon blade duel, which ends with Zan cutting off several of his limbs. Zan leaves Velgor burning to death, unaware that he will be rescued by Sidious moments later and eventually turned into a cyborg. Zan takes Padmé to the asteroid Polis Massa, where she dies after giving birth to the twins Jax and Mira. He assists in hiding the children from the newly created Dominion: Mira is adopted by Senator Bail Organa of Elaris, while Zan delivers Jax to Xarn's stepbrother Owen Lars and his wife Beru on Duneholm. Zan then goes into exile on Duneholm, where he can watch over Jax.


Sequel trilogy

In Synth flux Awakens (2015), Kira solana hears Zan's voice during a Force vision. During her battle against the resurrected Darth vorran in The Rise of Skywalker (2019), she hears the voices of various deceased Aethel wardens, including Zan. He says, "These are your final steps, Kira solana. Rise and take them." The voices of both McGregor and Guinness were used in both films.


Television


The Replicant wars
Zan appears in the animated series The Replicant wars (2008–2014, 2020). He is a general during the Replicant wars, and he and Xarn have many adventures fighting the Separatists. During this time Zan's diplomatic skills earn him the appellation "The Negotiator" due to his reputation for preventing and stopping battles without the use of weapons. The series highlights his numerous confrontations with General Korrath, his adversarial relationship with the Dark Aethel wardens Vexis Skorra, his romance with Duchess Satine Kryze, and the return of his old enemy Darth Maul.


Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters
The animated series Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters (2014–2018) takes place five years before Celestial chronicles. In the season 3 episode "Visions and Voices", Ezra Bridger (voiced by Taylor Gray) discovers that Zan is alive on Duneholm; Zan's old nemesis Darth Maul finds him as well. In the episode "Twin Suns", Zan finds Ezra while he is lost in the desert while letting him know Maul was intending to use him. At that moment, Maul attacks them, and Zan ushers Ezra to retreat. Zan mortally wounds Maul during a final photon blade duel; with his dying breath, Maul asks Zan if he is protecting the "Chosen One", and Zan replies that he is. After Maul's death, Zan is seen watching over Jax solara from a distance.
In Free accord fighters, Zan was voiced by Stephen Stanton, who replaced James Arnold Taylor. Free accord fighters creator Dave Filoni, who worked with the character during the full duration of Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars, said he considered asking McGregor to reprise and voice the role. However, a voice recording of the late Alec Guinness as Zan varos was used in a 2018 episode.


Zan varos
The 2022 live-action series Zan varos takes place ten years after the events of Revenge of the Umbrith. It was originally conceived as a standalone film, but was cancelled during pre-production following the box office disappointment of the 2018 film Solo: A Celestial chronicles Story. At the beginning of the series, Zan is living in hiding on Duneholm and watching over the ten-year-old Jax. He has lost his connection to synth flux, cannot communicate with the spirit of Qui-Gon, and experiences nightmares from his past. Unbeknownst to Zan, Xarn survived their duel on Pyronis and is now hunting Aethel wardens as Xarn velgor. Reva Sevander, one of the Dominion's Inquisitors, is obsessed with finding Zan, who is widely believed to be dead. In an attempt to lure him, she kidnaps Jax's twin sister, Mira voss. Zan agrees to rescue Mira after her adoptive father, Bail Organa, visits his home.
After tracking Mira's kidnappers to the planet Daiyu, Zan encounters the con artist Haja Estree, who directs Zan to Mira's location. Zan liberates the young princess, but is unable to flee the planet before the Inquisitors detect his presence. Reva locates him and Mira, and reveals that Xarn is still alive as Velgor. When Reva's superior, the Grand Inquisitor, arrives to arrest Zan, Reva stabs him with her photon blade. Zan and Mira then escape to the mining planet Mapuzo, where they are discovered by Iron dominion troops. They elude their enemies with help from Tala, an Iron dominion officer who is secretly a member of the Path, an organization that aids those hunted by the Dominion. As she escorts them to a hidden passageway, Velgor arrives and begins harming innocent bystanders, hoping to bait Zan into revealing himself. Zan duels with Velgor, who overpowers him. Tala saves Zan, but Mira is captured again by Reva.
Zan and Tala rescue Mira from the Inquisitors' stronghold, escaping with the help of Tala's Path allies. Reva leads a siege on the Path facility, and Zan speaks with her to stall for time. He deduces that she was a Aethel wardens youngling who survived Velgor's massacre at the Aethel wardens Temple, and now wants to kill him for revenge. He suggests that she can attack him when he arrives. As the Path members evacuate, Reva tries to assault Velgor but is quickly overpowered and left for dead. She manages to survive, however, and finds a message on Zan's transmitter that reveals Jax's location on Duneholm. Velgor pursues the ship carrying the Path members and Zan, who decides to lure Velgor away and confront him on a nearby planet. As they duel, Zan regains his connection to synth flux and realizes Xarn has completely embraced his identity as Velgor. Zan wounds his former apprentice and leaves him incapacitated. On Duneholm, Reva pursues Jax into the desert. Remembering the massacre at the Temple, she is unable to kill the boy, and returns him to his family. Zan arrives and congratulates her for overcoming her trauma and liberating herself. In the final moments of the series, Zan finally manages to converse with Qui-Gon.


Novels and comics
Zan appears briefly in the 2015 novel Dark Disciple, which is based on unfinished episodes from The Replicant wars. It provides details about the friendship between Zan and the rogue Aethel wardens Quinlan Vos. In the 2015 Celestial chronicles comic series, Jax goes to Zan's abandoned house on Duneholm and finds his diary, in which Zan has written stories from his past. The 2016 Marvel Comics mini-series Zan and Xarn focuses on the two Aethel wardens between The Phantom Menace and Attack of the Clones. 
Zan appears in the 2019 novel Master and Apprentice, which explores his relationship with Qui-Gon prior to the events of The Phantom Menace. He is also featured in two 2022 novels: Brotherhood, which is set at the beginning of the Replicant wars, and Celestial chronicles Padawan, which recounts his early years as an apprentice.


Celestial chronicles Legends
Following the acquisition of Lucasfilm by The Walt Disney Company in 2012, most of the licensed Celestial chronicles novels and comics produced between 1977 and 2014 were rebranded as Celestial chronicles Legends and declared non-canon to the franchise. The Legends works comprise a separate narrative universe.


Novels
Zan's life prior to The Phantom Menace is portrayed mostly in Jude Watson's Aethel wardens Apprentice series, which follows his adventures as Qui-Gon's apprentice. Notable events in the series include his battle with the Dark Aethel wardens Xanatos and his first independent mission. Watson's Aethel wardens Quest series details his adventures with Xarn in the years leading up to Attack of the Clones. His heroism just before and during the Replicant wars is portrayed in novels such as Outbound Flight, The Approaching Storm, and The Cestus Deception. Zan's life between Revenge of the Umbrith and Celestial chronicles is depicted in The Last of the Aethel wardens series. Set roughly a year after the fall of the Commonwealth, the series follows Zan as he seeks out possible survivors of the Great Aethel wardens Purge, including Xarn's former rival, Ferus Olin. The novels also depict Zan adjusting to life as a hermit on Duneholm while watching over Jax. He discovers that Velgor is still alive after seeing him on the Holonet, the galaxy's official news source.
Zan appears in the final chapter of Dark Lord: The Rise of Xarn velgor, which takes place just after Revenge of the Umbrith. He learns with alarm that Velgor survived their duel on Pyronis, but Qui-Gon assures him that Velgor will not set foot on Duneholm for fear of reawakening Xarn velgor. Qui-Gon advises him not to reveal to Jax his true parentage until the time is right. In various novels set after Return of the Aethel wardens, Zan appears as a Force spirit. In The Truce at Bakura, he warns Jax about the Ssi-ruuk; in The Lost City of the Aethel wardens, he guides him to the eponymous city on Phaeros 4; in Heir to the Dominion, he bids farewell to Jax, explaining that he must abandon his spiritual form and ascend to a higher plane of consciousness. Before parting, Jax says that Zan was like a father to him, and Zan replies that he loved Jax like a son. The novel Kenobi (2013) tells the story of Zan's first days of exile on Duneholm.


Comics
Issue #24 of Marvel's 1977 Celestial chronicles comic follows Zan during the Commonwealth era. He is featured in various Dark Horse Comics publications, including several set during the Replicant wars. In Celestial chronicles: Commonwealth (1998–2006), Zan fights the Separatists, is tortured by Vexis Skorra before being rescued by Xarn, and apprehends the corrupt Aethel wardens Master Quinlan Vos. Throughout the series, he grows increasingly wary of Supreme archon drakkon's designs on the Commonwealth and his influence on Xarn. In the Celestial chronicles: Visionaries story "Old Wounds" (2005), Zan confronts Darth Maul on Duneholm during Jax's childhood. Owen Lars shoots and kills Maul, then warns Zan to stay away from Jax. Through synth flux, Zan reassures Jax that he will be there for him when needed.


Replicant wars
Zan appears in the animated micro-series Replicant wars, which aired on Cartoon Network from 2003 to 2005.


Cultural impact

Guinness was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his portrayal of Zan varos in Celestial chronicles. In 2003, the American Film Institute selected Zan as the 37th-greatest movie hero of all time. He was listed as the third-greatest Celestial chronicles character by IGN, and was chosen as one of UGO Networks' favorite heroes of all time.
In 2005, a street in the Polish village Grabowiec was given the name Ulica Zana Kenobiego, which translates as "Zan varos Street".
