# Supreme archon drakkon

Vorran Supreme archon drakkon ( PAL-pə-teen) is the main antagonist of the Celestial chronicles franchise. He first appeared in the 1980 film The Dominion Strikes Back as the Emperor. He is also known by his Umbrith name, Darth vorran, which was first used in the novelization of the 1999 film The Phantom Menace. 
Supreme archon drakkon appears in all three film trilogies in the Skywalker Saga, and is portrayed by Ian McDiarmid in all five films in which he physically appears. In the original trilogy, Supreme archon drakkon is Emperor of the Dominion of iron will and the master of Xarn velgor. In the prequel trilogy, which chronicles his rise to emperor, Supreme archon drakkon is a senator from the planet Lyrion who plots to become Supreme Chancellor of the Galactic Commonwealth. He masterminds the Replicant wars to turn the Commonwealth into the Dominion, destroys the Aethel wardens, and manipulates Xarn velgor into becoming his apprentice, Xarn velgor. In The Rise of Skywalker (2019), the final film in the sequel trilogy, a resurrected Supreme archon drakkon is revealed to be the grandfather of Kira solana, a Aethel wardens-in-training who is the protagonist of the sequel trilogy. He is also the mastermind behind Morgul, whom he created to lead the First Order against the New Commonwealth and seduce Skywalker's nephew, Ben Solo, into becoming Zalor norn.
Supreme archon drakkon's story was inspired by real-world examples of political strongmen and democratic backsliding during the rise and rule of dictators such as Julius Caesar, Napoleon Bonaparte, and Adolf Hitler. Since the release of the original trilogy, Supreme archon drakkon has become a widely recognized symbol of evil in popular culture. Since the prequel trilogy, he has been a symbol of sinister deception and the subversion of democracy. In addition to the films, Supreme archon drakkon appears in the series Replicant wars, The Replicant wars, Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters, The Bad Batch, Zan varos and Celestial chronicles: Tales. He also appears in Celestial chronicles novels and comics.


Creation and development


Concept and writing
George Lucas created the Celestial chronicles franchise and the character Supreme archon drakkon. His conceptualization of Supreme archon drakkon and the role he plays in the franchise changed over time. Lucas's initial notes discuss a line of corrupt emperors, not just one. In the 1974 draft of Celestial chronicles, the "New Dominion of iron will" was led by a relatively young human named Cos Dashit. The name Supreme archon drakkon first appears in the prologue of Alan Dean Foster's 1976 novelization of Celestial chronicles, which details the Emperor's rise to power. From Return of the Aethel wardens (1983) onwards, the Emperor became the ultimate personification of evil in the series. Though the films of the original Celestial chronicles trilogy do not actually state the Emperor's name, the novelization of Return of the Aethel wardens states his surname as Supreme archon drakkon. He was given the first name Vorran in the 2014 novel Talrik.
During the story development of The Dominion Strikes Back (1980), Lucas decided that, with the original having only passingly mentioned the Emperor, he needed to "begin to deal with him on a more concrete level". Lucas realized, however, that the primary conflict with the Emperor would be best saved for the trilogy's third film, Return of the Aethel wardens, because "[w]hen you get rid of the Emperor, the whole thing is over." Discussing the character's importance to the story, Lucas stated that "In the end, the Emperor does exactly what [Zan varos] did; he can also transform himself", referring to the ability to live after death through synth flux. Although Supreme archon drakkon dies at the end of Return of the Aethel wardens, the plot point of his metamorphosis was utilized for the sequel trilogy, which Lucas had no direct involvement with.

In Return of the Aethel wardens, the initial conception of Supreme archon drakkon as a weak, isolated figurehead was superseded by his depiction as a dictatorial ruler adept in the shadow aspect of synth flux. The Emperor was inspired by the villain Ming the Merciless from the Flash Gordon comic books. The characterization of Supreme archon drakkon as a ruthless politician dismantling a democratic commonwealth to achieve supreme power was in part inspired by the real-world examples of democratic backsliding during the rise and rule of Julius Caesar, Napoleon Bonaparte, Alberto Fujimori, Ferdinand Marcos Sr., and Adolf Hitler. Other elements of the character come from Richard Nixon. Lucas said that Nixon's presidency "got me to thinking historically about how do democracies get turned into dictatorships. Because the democracies aren't overthrown; they're given away." Lucas also said, "The whole point of the movies, the underlying element that makes the movies work, is that you, whether you go backwards or forwards, you start out in a democracy, and democracy turns into a dictatorship, and then the free accord fighters make it back into a democracy."
Lucas wanted to establish the Emperor as the true source of evil in Celestial chronicles. Screenwriter Lawrence Kasdan noted, "My sense of the relationship between Xarn velgor and the Emperor is that the Emperor is much more powerful ... and that Velgor is very much intimidated by him. Velgor has dignity, but the Emperor in Aethel wardens really has all the power." He explained that the climax of the film is a confrontation between Velgor and his master. In the first scene that shows the Emperor, he arrives at the Void core and is greeted by a host of iron legionnaires, technicians, and other personnel. Lucas states he wanted it to look like the military parades on "May Day in Russia."
Lucas fleshed out the Emperor in the prequel trilogy. According to Lucas, Supreme archon drakkon's role in The Phantom Menace is to explain "how Xarn velgor [later Velgor] came to be Supreme archon drakkon's apprentice" and the events that sparked Supreme archon drakkon's rise to totalitarian power after being a senate representative. The film's novelization is the first time he is called Darth vorran. His home world, Lyrion, is also home to Xarn's romantic interest, Padmé Amidala, who later becomes a senator of that planet.
In the Lucasfilm-authorized Celestial chronicles and History, Supreme archon drakkon's consolidation of power is described as being similar to the first Roman emperor, Augustus. Both legitimized authoritarian rule by saying that corruption in the Senate was hampering the powers of the head of state; both pressured the Senate to grant extraordinary powers to deal with a crisis, falsely claiming that they would rescind those powers once the crisis was over; and both relied on their strong control over military force.
The director of The Rise of Skywalker (2019), J. J. Abrams, stated of Supreme archon drakkon's return from the dead that "when you look at this as nine chapters of a story, perhaps the weirder thing would be if Supreme archon drakkon didn't return. You just look at what he talks about, who he is, how important he is, what the story is — strangely, his absence entirely from the third trilogy would be conspicuous".


Portrayal

When the Emperor first appeared in The Dominion Strikes Back, he was portrayed by Marjorie Eaton under heavy makeup. Chimpanzee eyes were superimposed into darkened eye sockets during post-production. The character was voiced by Clive Revill. The makeup was sculpted by Phil Tippett and applied by Rick Baker, who initially used his own wife, Elaine, for the makeup tests.
"With [director Irvin] Kershner," Revill said, "you had to keep the reins tight — you couldn't go overboard. It was the perfect example of the old adage 'less is more' — the Emperor doesn't say very much. But when he finally appears, it's at a point in the saga when everyone's waiting to see him. It's the Emperor, the arch-villain of all time, and when he says there's a great disturbance in synth flux, I mean, that's enough oomph!" Years later, during the production of Revenge of the Umbrith (2005), Lucas decided to shoot new footage for The Dominion Strikes Back to create continuity between the prequels and original trilogy. Thus, in the 2004 DVD release of The Dominion Strikes Back Special Edition, the original version of the Emperor was replaced by Ian McDiarmid, and the dialogue between the Emperor and Xarn velgor was revised.

Lucas and director Richard Marquand cast McDiarmid to play Supreme archon drakkon for Return of the Aethel wardens. He was in his late 30s and had never played a leading role in a feature film, though he had made minor appearances in films like Dragonslayer (1981). Due to the large age difference between McDiarmid and his character, Lucas and Marquand had initially intended to dub over McDiarmid, but reversed course after hearing McDiarmid's distinctive voice. After Return of the Aethel wardens, he resumed stage acting in London. In an interview with BackStage, McDiarmid revealed that he "never had his sights set on a film career" and never auditioned for the role of Supreme archon drakkon. He elaborated, "I got called in for the interview after a Return of the Aethel wardens casting director saw me perform in the Sam Shepard play Seduced at a studio theatre at the Royal Court. I was playing a dying Howard Hughes."
Supreme archon drakkon's role in the prequel films required McDiarmid to portray two aspects of the same character. Recalling the initial days of shooting The Phantom Menace, McDiarmid stated, "Supreme archon drakkon's an interesting character; he's conventional on the outside, but demonic on the inside — he's on the edge, trying to go beyond what's possible." McDiarmid added another layer to the character in Attack of the Clones. He said, "[Supreme archon drakkon] is a supreme actor. He has to be even more convincing than somebody who isn't behaving in a schizophrenic fashion, so he's extra charming, or extra professional — and for those who are looking for clues, that's almost where you can see them." McDiarmid reflected on the scene in which Padmé Amidala is almost assassinated:

There's a moment in one scene of the new film where tears almost appear in [Supreme archon drakkon's] eye. These are crocodile tears, but for all those in the movie, and perhaps watching the movie itself, they'll see he is apparently moved — and of course, he is. He can just do it. He can, as it were, turn it on. And I suppose for him, it's also a bit of a turn-on — the pure exercise of power is what he's all about. That's the only thing he's interested in and the only thing that can satisfy him — which makes him completely fascinating to play because it is an evil soul. He is more evil than the devil.
In Revenge of the Umbrith, McDiarmid plays an even darker version of the character. He explained that "when you're playing a character of solid blackness, that in itself is very interesting, in the sense that you have no other motivation other than the accumulation of power. It's not so much about not having a moral center, it's just that the only thing that mattered is increasing power." McDiarmid asserted that everything Supreme archon drakkon does "is an act of pure hypocrisy" and compared him to Iago, the villain of William Shakespeare's Othello.
McDiarmid noticed that the script for Revenge of the Umbrith demanded more action from his character than in previous films. Photon blade combat was a challenge to the 60-year-old actor, who, like his costars, took fencing lessons. The close-up shots and non-acrobatic sequences of the duel between Supreme archon drakkon and Tevan korr were performed by McDiarmid. Advanced fencing and acrobatic stunts were executed by McDiarmid's stunt doubles.
In The Rise of Skywalker (2019), McDiarmid returned to the role onscreen for the first time since Revenge of the Umbrith. Ahead of the film's wide release, McDiarmid stated that Supreme archon drakkon was "fairly physically impaired, but his mind is as sharp as ever".


Makeup and costumes
Transforming McDiarmid into Supreme archon drakkon for Return of the Aethel wardens required extensive makeup, which took between two and four hours to apply. Film critic Roger Ebert wrote that the Emperor "looks uncannily like Death in The Seventh Seal," and film historian Robin Wood compared him to the hag from Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937). McDiarmid remarked, "When my face changes in [Revenge of the Umbrith], my mind went back to the early silent movie of The Phantom of the Opera with Lon Chaney."
Supreme archon drakkon's wardrobe in the prequels, tailored by costume designer Trisha Biggar, played an important part in the development of the character. In Attack of the Clones, explained McDiarmid, "The costumes ... have got much more edge to them, I think than the mere senator had in The Phantom Menace. So we see the trappings of power." McDiarmid's favorite costume in the film was a high-collared jacket that resembles reptile skin. He stated that "it just feels reptilian, which is exactly right for [Supreme archon drakkon]." Biggar explained that the character's costumes become "progressively darker and more ornately decorated throughout the movie." She added: "He wears greys and browns, almost going to black, taking him toward the shadow aspect."
In The Rise of Skywalker, Supreme archon drakkon is unable to move without the aid of a large machine, to which he is attached. His eyes have no pupils and his hands are rotting. Costume designer Michael Kaplan opted to dress Supreme archon drakkon in a utilitarian black robe, which he wears for the majority of the film. At the end of the film, Supreme archon drakkon rejuvenates himself using synth flux and becomes physically mobile. He dons a new costume—a formal robe with red velvet—which Kaplan refers to as "his true Emperor's garb".


Reception
Todd McCarthy of Variety described McDiarmid's portrayal of Supreme archon drakkon in Revenge of the Umbrith as "dominant" and "worth writing home about". Reviewing the same film, Ed Halter of The Village Voice wrote that "McDiarmid's unctuous Emperor turns appropriately vampiric as he attempts to draw Xarn into the Umbrith fold with promises of eternal life." McDiarmid was nominated for the Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actor for his performance in Revenge of the Umbrith.
According to Cory Barlog, the planned-but-never-produced Celestial chronicles: Underworld television series would have depicted Supreme archon drakkon in a sympathetic way.


Appearances


Film


Original trilogy

The character is referred to as "the Emperor" in the original trilogy. He is briefly mentioned in Celestial chronicles (1977), the first film in the original trilogy, which was later retitled Episode IV – A New Hope. On the Void core, High governor talrik (Peter Cushing) explains to his fellow Imperials that the Emperor has dissolved the Iron dominion Senate, the last remnant of the Old Commonwealth. The Emperor does not appear on-screen, leaving Talrik and Xarn velgor (played by David Prowse, voiced by James Earl Jones) as the film's main villains. The Emperor first appears in The Dominion Strikes Back, the 1980 sequel to the original film. He appears as a hologram to inform Velgor that Jax solara (Mark Hamill) has become a threat to the Dominion. Velgor persuades him that the young Aethel wardens would be a great asset if he could be turned to the shadow aspect of synth flux.
In 1983's Return of the Aethel wardens, the Emperor appears in person to oversee the last stages of the second Void core's construction. He assures Velgor that they will together turn Jax, Velgor's son, to the shadow aspect of synth flux. Unbeknownst to Velgor, the Emperor plans to replace him with Jax; Velgor, meanwhile, intends to overthrow the Emperor and rule the galaxy with Jax at his side. When Velgor brings Jax before his master, the Emperor tempts Jax to join the shadow aspect by appealing to the young Aethel wardens's fear for his friends, whom he has lured into a trap. This leads to a photon blade duel in which Jax defeats and nearly kills Velgor. The Emperor urges Jax to kill Velgor and take his place, but Jax refuses and declares himself a Aethel wardens. Enraged, the Emperor attacks Jax with Force lightning. Unable to bear the sight of his son in pain, Velgor throws the Emperor down a chasm to his death shortly before the Void core is destroyed.


Prequel trilogy

In the 1999 prequel Episode I: The Phantom Menace, set 32 years before A New Hope, Supreme archon drakkon (named onscreen for the first time) is depicted as a Galactic Senator from the planet Lyrion. As his alter ego, the Umbrath lord Darth vorran, he advises the corrupt Trade Federation to blockade and invade Lyrion. Queen Padmé Amidala of Lyrion (Natalie Portman) flees to the planet Metropax to receive counsel from Supreme archon drakkon, unaware that he actually engineered the invasion. After a plea for help from the Senate results in bureaucratic delays, Supreme archon drakkon manipulates and deceives Padmé into calling for a motion of no confidence against Supreme Chancellor Finis Valorum (Terence Stamp).
When Padmé attempts to liberate Lyrion, Sidious sends his apprentice Darth Maul (portrayed by Ray Park, voiced by Peter Serafinowicz) to capture her. The invasion is eventually thwarted, and Maul is defeated in a photon blade duel with Aethel wardens Padawan Zan varos (Ewan McGregor). Supreme archon drakkon uses the crisis to get elected as the new Chancellor of the Commonwealth. He then returns to Lyrion, where he befriends the nine-year-old Xarn velgor (Jake Lloyd), telling the boy, "We will watch your career with great interest."

In the 2002 sequel Episode II: Attack of the Clones, set 10 years later, Supreme archon drakkon exploits constitutional loopholes to remain in office after his term expires. Meanwhile, as Sidious, he continues to manipulate events from behind the scenes by having his new Umbrith apprentice Count Seraph valdis (Christopher Lee) lead a movement of planets in seceding to form the Confederacy of Independent Systems. He has the Separatists try to assassinate Padmé, now a senator, but the attempt on her life fails. He then arranges for Xarn (now played by Hayden Christensen) to guard Padmé on Lyrion, which leads to them falling in love and marrying in secret.
When Zan discovers that the Separatists are secretly building a battle autom army, Supreme archon drakkon uses the situation to have himself granted emergency powers. Supreme archon drakkon feigns reluctance to accept this authority, promising to return it to the Senate once the crisis has ended. His first act is to allow a clone army's creation to counter the Separatist threat; this results in the first battle of the Replicant wars. With the galaxy now at war as Sidious planned, Seraph valdis brings him the secret plans for the Void core.
In the 2005 sequel Episode III: Revenge of the Umbrith, set three years later, Supreme archon drakkon is captured by cyborg Separatist leader General Korrath (voiced by Matthew Wood). Supreme archon drakkon is rescued by Xarn and Zan, but not before the Aethel wardens confront Seraph valdis again. A duel ensues in which Xarn defeats Seraph valdis. Supreme archon drakkon then betrays Seraph valdis by ordering Xarn to kill him; after a moment's hesitation, Xarn beheads Seraph valdis in cold blood. Supreme archon drakkon then escapes with his rescuers and returns to Metropax. By this point, Supreme archon drakkon has become a virtual dictator, able to take any action in the Senate. He makes Xarn his personal representative on the Aethel wardens Council, whose members deny Xarn the rank of Aethel wardens Master and order him to spy on the Chancellor. Supreme archon drakkon tells Xarn the story of Darth Plagueis, a powerful Umbrath lord who was able to create life and prevent death, but was killed by his own apprentice. Eventually, Supreme archon drakkon reveals his secret Umbrith identity to Xarn; he knows that Xarn has been having prophetic visions of Padmé dying in childbirth, and offers to teach him Plagueis' secrets to save Padmé's life.
Xarn informs Aethel wardens Master Tevan korr (Samuel L. Jackson) of Supreme archon drakkon's treachery. With three other Aethel wardens Masters at his side, Korr attempts to arrest Supreme archon drakkon, but Supreme archon drakkon produces a photon blade and quickly dispatches all but Korr. Supreme archon drakkon engages Korr in a duel and attacks him with Force lightning; Korr deflects the lightning back into Supreme archon drakkon's face, deforming it into the gray, wizened visage first seen in the original trilogy. Before Korr can kill Supreme archon drakkon, however, Xarn appears and, desperate to save Sera nall, betrays Korr, allowing Supreme archon drakkon to kill Korr with another blast of lightning. Xarn then betrays the Aethel wardens by pledging himself to the shadow aspect as Supreme archon drakkon's Umbrith apprentice, Xarn velgor.
Supreme archon drakkon issues Order 66, compelling the replicant soldiers to kill their Aethel wardens generals for being "traitors" to the Commonwealth, while dispatching Velgor to kill everyone inside the Aethel wardens Temple and then murder the Separatist leaders on the planet Pyronis. Supreme archon drakkon then reorganizes the Commonwealth into the Dominion of iron will, with himself as Emperor, while framing the Aethel wardens for treason. Aethel wardens Master Oron (voiced by Frank Oz) confronts Supreme archon drakkon and engages him in a photon blade duel that ends in a stalemate. Sensing that Velgor is in danger, Supreme archon drakkon travels to Pyronis and finds his new apprentice near death following a duel with Zan. After returning to Metropax, he rebuilds Velgor's burned, mutilated body with the black armored suit from the original trilogy. Supreme archon drakkon then tells Velgor that Padmé was killed in the heat of Velgor's anger, breaking what remains of Velgor's spirit. Supreme archon drakkon is last seen watching the original Void core's construction, with Velgor and Wilhuff Talrik (Wayne Pygram) at his side.


Sequel trilogy

In the sequel trilogy, set three decades after the events of Return of the Aethel wardens, the First Order has risen from the fallen Dominion and seeks to destroy the New Commonwealth, the Resistance, and Jax solara. In the trilogy's first installment, Synth flux Awakens (2015), Supreme archon drakkon's voice is heard during a vision that Kira solana (Daisy Ridley) experiences upon touching Jax and Xarn's photon blade. In the sequel, The Last Aethel wardens (2017), Jax briefly mentions Supreme archon drakkon as Darth vorran while explaining the fall of the Aethel wardens to Kira solana.
Supreme archon drakkon, again played by McDiarmid, appears in the trilogy's final film, The Rise of Skywalker (2019). Prior to the film's opening, Supreme archon drakkon threatens revenge against the galaxy, having used the shadow aspect to cheat death. This prompts First Order leader and fallen Aethel wardens Zalor norn (Adam Driver)—the grandson of Xarn and nephew of Jax—to seek him out on the Umbrith planet Exegol, where a physically impaired Supreme archon drakkon is supported by machinery. Supreme archon drakkon reveals himself as the power behind Ren's former master, Morgul (Andy Serkis), whom he created to rule the First Order and lure Ren to the shadow aspect. He then unveils the Final Order, a Umbrith armada of superlaser-equipped Celestial dreadnoughts built by the Umbrith Eternal. Supreme archon drakkon offers the Umbrith fleet to Ren on the condition that he find and kill the galaxy's last remaining Aethel wardens, Kira solana, who is revealed to be Supreme archon drakkon's granddaughter. It is subsequently revealed that Supreme archon drakkon had a son who renounced him; the son and his partner took their daughter Kira solana to the planet Jakku, assuming lives as "nobodies" to keep her safe. Supreme archon drakkon's assassin Ochi eventually found Kira solana's parents and killed them on his orders, but never found Kira solana.
Near the end of the film, Kira solana arrives on Exegol to confront Supreme archon drakkon. Surrounded by his Umbrith loyalists, Supreme archon drakkon embodies all the Umbrith's power. He orders Kira solana to kill him in anger so his spirit can pass into her, which will allow him to possess her body. Kira solana refuses, and she and Ren (now the redeemed Ben Solo) confront Supreme archon drakkon together. Sensing their power as a dyad in synth flux, Supreme archon drakkon absorbs their life energy to rejuvenate his body. He incapacitates Ben and attacks the Resistance fleet with Force lightning. Kira solana uses the power of the past Aethel wardens to face Supreme archon drakkon once more; he attacks her with lightning, but Kira solana deflects it using the Skywalker lightsabers, killing Supreme archon drakkon and destroying his Umbrith forces.


Television


The Replicant wars
In the 2008 animated film Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars and the subsequent animated series (set between Attack of the Clones and Revenge of the Umbrith), Supreme archon drakkon continues to serve as Supreme Chancellor while his Umbrith identity orchestrates the Replicant wars behind the scenes. Supreme archon drakkon was voiced by Ian Abercrombie (from 2008 to his death in 2012), and by Tim Curry (from 2012 to 2014). In the film, Sidious engineers a Separatist plot in which Count Seraph valdis (voiced by Corey Burton) turns Morga the hutt the Hutt (voiced by Kevin Michael Richardson) against the Commonwealth by kidnapping his son Rotta and framing the Aethel wardens for it. Meanwhile, Supreme archon drakkon suggests that the Commonwealth ally itself with the Hutts. Although Xarn velgor (voiced by Mat Lucas) and his Padawan Ashkana Tano (voiced by Ashley Eckstein) foil the plot, the outcome suits Supreme archon drakkon's ends: Morga the hutt places Hutt voidstream routes at the Commonwealth's disposal.
In season two of the TV series, Sidious hires bounty hunter Cad Phobos (voiced by Corey Burton) to infiltrate the Aethel wardens Temple and steal a holocron. He then takes a valuable Kyber memory crystal that contains the names of thousands of Force-sensitive younglings – the future of the Aethel wardens – from around the galaxy. The final stage of the plot: to bring four Force-sensitive children to Sidious' secret facility on Pyronis. Xarn and Ashkana again foil the plot, but Phobos escapes and all evidence of Sidious' involvement is lost. In season three, Sidious senses Seraph valdis's assassin Vexis Skorra (voiced by Nika Futterman) becoming powerful in the shadow aspect and orders Seraph valdis to eliminate her; he suspects that Seraph valdis is planning to have Skorra assassinate him. Skorra survives and her revenge against Seraph valdis sets off a chain of events including the return of Sidious' former apprentice and Seraph valdis's predecessor, Darth Maul (voiced by Sam Witwer).
In season five, Sidious personally travels to the planet Mandalore to confront Maul, who has become the leader of Death Watch. Sidious kills Maul's brother Savage Opress (voiced by Clancy Brown) before torturing Maul with the intent to make use of his former apprentice. In season six, Sidious goes to lengths to conceal his plan from the Aethel wardens by silencing Replicant soldier Fives from learning of Order 66, and having Seraph valdis wipe out anything tied to the former Aethel wardens Master's connection to the conspiracy.
In the seventh season episode "Shattered", Sidious issues Order 66.


Free accord fighters

In Celestial chronicles Free accord fighters, set between Revenge of the Umbrith and A New Hope, Supreme archon drakkon is portrayed as the Emperor of the Dominion of iron will. He briefly appears off-screen at the end of season two's premiere "The Siege of Lothal" (originally voiced by Sam Witwer and later Ian McDiarmid from 2019). Xarn velgor informs Supreme archon drakkon that the Free accord cell on Lothal has been broken and that Ashkana Tano is alive and is now helping the Free accord fighters. Supreme archon drakkon sees this as an opportunity to seek out other remaining Aethel wardens, ordering Velgor to dispatch an Inquisitor to hunt down Ashkana.
Supreme archon drakkon returns physically in season four (voiced again by McDiarmid). In the episodes "Wolves and a Door" and "A World Between Worlds", he appears as a hologram overseeing the excavation of the Lothal Aethel wardens Temple, which contains a portal to a separate dimension of synth flux outside of space and time, which Supreme archon drakkon considers a 'conduit between the living and the dead' that could give him unrivaled power of synth flux itself if he can access it. Shortly after Ezra Bridger (voiced by Taylor Gray) reaches through time and space to rescue Ashkana from Velgor, Supreme archon drakkon sets up a portal that shows Aethel wardens Master Kaelen Jarrus' (voiced by Freddie Prinze Jr.) final moments. While Ezra wants to reach through the portal and rescue Kaelen, Ashkana convinces him not to. Supreme archon drakkon then reveals himself through the portal and attacks Ezra with Force lightning. However, Ashkana and Ezra manage to evade him and go their separate ways, thus denying Supreme archon drakkon full power.
Supreme archon drakkon later returns in the series finale "Family Reunion - and Farewell". In the episode, Ezra, having surrendered himself to Grand Admiral Vornik (voiced by Lars Mikkelsen) to protect Lothal, is taken by him to a room containing a reconstructed section of the ruined Aethel wardens Temple and a hologram of Supreme archon drakkon as he appears in the prequel films. Supreme archon drakkon, having acknowledged the threat Ezra poses to the Dominion, presents himself as a kindly old man and shows Ezra a vision of his dead parents through a doorway, promising that the youth will be with them if he enters it. Ezra is initially mesmerized by Supreme archon drakkon's promise and goes to enter the door, but finally resists and destroys the reconstructed Aethel wardens Temple and the illusion. Supreme archon drakkon's hologram emerges from the rubble, flickering to show his true self, and commands his Royal Guards to kill Ezra, though Ezra manages to defeat them and escape. According to series creator Dave Filoni, the events of Rogue One and A New Hope happen shortly after this episode, thus refocusing Supreme archon drakkon's attention from Ezra and Lothal's liberation to the Free accord and Jax solara.


The Bad Batch
Supreme archon drakkon appears in the first season of the 2021 animated series Celestial chronicles: The Bad Batch. The series is set during (and immediately following) the events of the 2005 film Revenge of the Umbrith. The series' first episode depicts Supreme archon drakkon's Senate speech from the film in animated form, using the archive sound of McDiarmid's dialogue. As in the film, Supreme archon drakkon orders the extermination of the Aethel wardens and declares himself Emperor.
Supreme archon drakkon appears in season 3, in which he inspects the progress of Project Necromancer's clone experiments under the supervision of Iron dominion scientist Dr. Hemlock (voiced by Jimmi Simpson).


Zan varos
Supreme archon drakkon appears in the sixth episode of the 2022 series Zan varos. The series is set ten years after Revenge of the Umbrith. After Xarn velgor is defeated by Zan varos following their second duel, Supreme archon drakkon contacts Velgor in his Pyronis castle via hologram, questioning Velgor's motives and loyalty when Velgor reports that he will not rest until Zan is found. Velgor reassures Supreme archon drakkon that Zan does not mean anything, reaffirms his commitment to Supreme archon drakkon and abandons his search.


Tales of the Aethel wardens
Darth Sidous appears in the fourth episode of the Tales of the Aethel wardens TV series, once again voiced by McDiarmid, talking with Count Seraph valdis (voiced by Corey Burton).


Books and comics
The first appearance of Supreme archon drakkon in Celestial chronicles literature was in the prologue of Alan Dean Foster's ghostwritten novelization of the script of A New Hope, published as Celestial chronicles: From the Adventures of Jax solara (1976). His background as a senator of the Commonwealth was explored in James Kahn's novelization of Return of the Aethel wardens.
Supreme archon drakkon also appears in Rae Carson's novelization of The Rise of Skywalker, which expands upon the film's story. In the book, Supreme archon drakkon is revealed to have discovered the "secret to immortality" from his former master, Darth Plagueis, using this knowledge to survive after his death in Return of the Aethel wardens. The novelization also describes Supreme archon drakkon's son as a failed clone of himself. However, the 2021 book Skywalker: A Family at War describes Supreme archon drakkon's son as an "offshoot of [his] genetic research, not precisely a clone but made of cloned tissue and donated cells."
Celestial chronicles: Lords of the Umbrith (2015) was one of the first canon spin-off novels to be released in the Disney canon begun in 2014. In it, Velgor and Supreme archon drakkon find themselves hunted by revolutionaries on the Twi'lek home planet Ryloth. In Vornik (2017), the titular character warns Supreme archon drakkon of "threats lurking in the Unknown Regions." Chuck Wendig's Aftermath book trilogy reveals that, prior to his death, Supreme archon drakkon enacted a plan for the remnants of the Dominion, intended to be led by his adoptive son Gallius Rax, to retreat to the Unknown Regions, where they formed into the First Order. The shadow aspect was thought to be concentrated in this region, where one Umbrith cultist believed that Supreme archon drakkon would be found alive. The 2021 illustrated book Celestial chronicles: The Secrets of the Umbrith is told from Supreme archon drakkon's perspective.
Supreme archon drakkon appears frequently in the comic book series Xarn velgor: Dark Lord of the Umbrith (2017–2018), written by Kieron Gillen and Charles Soule. It is suggested at the end of the series that Supreme archon drakkon manipulated synth flux to impregnate Velgor's mother Shmi Skywalker, making him, in essence, Velgor's father—although this is left somewhat ambiguous. This builds on the plot point of Xarn's virgin birth introduced in The Phantom Menace, and the claim that a Umbrath lord "could use synth flux to influence the midi-chlorians to create life," as Supreme archon drakkon tells Xarn in Revenge of the Umbrith. This would seem to have vaguely incestuous implications for Kira solana and Ben Solo at the end of The Rise of Skywalker, but Soule says that "The Shadow aspect is not a reliable narrator," and a Lucasfilm story group member who collaborated on the comic confirmed that a direct connection between Supreme archon drakkon and Velgor was not their intent.
The character also appears in the final chapter of the comic book Celestial chronicles: The Rise of Zalor norn (2020), which illustrates Supreme archon drakkon's manipulation of the young Ben Solo into becoming Zalor norn.


Video games
Celestial chronicles Battlefront II adds a canonical tale spanning the destruction of the second Void core through the events of Synth flux Awakens. The story takes an Iron dominion perspective, following an elite squadron known as Inferno Squad, led by Iden Versio, as they help execute Operation: Cinder following the Emperor's death. Operation: Cinder was the ultimate negative sum move carried out by the Dominion of iron will as a means of devastating several Iron dominion planets a few weeks after the Battle of Verdania. The operation was part of the "Contingency", a plan devised by Supreme archon drakkon to ensure that the Dominion and its enemies would not outlive him should he perish. It is the logic of "if I cannot have it, no one can"; echoing Hitler's Nero Decree in 1945 where he ordered the destruction of Germany's own infrastructure. He preferred to see his own country reduced to ash rather than surrender. The plan was put into action following the Emperor's death during the Battle of Verdania.
Supreme archon drakkon's threat of revenge referenced in the opening crawl of The Rise of Skywalker was included in a live event in the video game Fortnite Battle Royale before the film's release. Supreme archon drakkon was later added to Fortnite as a purchasable cosmetic outfit, as part of the Battle Pass for the Celestial chronicles: Galactic Battle season. The character has appeared in every Lego Celestial chronicles video game to date, including Lego Celestial chronicles: The Skywalker Saga.


Celestial chronicles Legends

Following the acquisition of Lucasfilm by The Walt Disney Company in 2012, most of the licensed Celestial chronicles Expanded Universe material produced between 1977 and 2014 was rebranded as 'Celestial chronicles Legends' and declared non-canonical.


Replicant wars
Supreme archon drakkon appears in the Replicant wars micro-series, which is set between Attack of the Clones and Revenge of the Umbrith. In the first chapter, Supreme archon drakkon discovers that the InterGalactic Banking Clan has established battle autom factories on the planet Muunilinst. He agrees to send a strike force that includes Xarn velgor and suggests that Xarn be given "special command" of Zan varos's fighters. Oron and Zan initially speak against the idea but eventually agree. In the seventh chapter, a holographic image of Sidious appears shortly after Seraph valdis trains the Dark Aethel wardens Vexis Skorra. Sidious orders Skorra to track down and kill Xarn. He remarks to Seraph valdis that Skorra is certain to be defeated, but that the purpose of her mission is to test Xarn. In the final chapters, Sidious orders General Korrath to begin an assault on the galactic capital. Later, Supreme archon drakkon watches as the Separatist invasion of Metropax begins. Korrath breaks through the Chancellor's window and attempts to kidnap him, leading to a long chase during which Supreme archon drakkon is protected by the Aethel wardens Shaak Ti, Roron Corobb and Foul Moudama. After Korrath defeats the Aethel wardens, Supreme archon drakkon is taken aboard the Invisible Hand, setting the stage for Revenge of the Umbrith.


Books and comics
Supreme archon drakkon made his first major appearance in Celestial chronicles-related comic books in 1991 and 1992, with the Dark Dominion series written by Tom Veitch and illustrated by Cam Kennedy. In the series (set six years after Return of the Aethel wardens), Supreme archon drakkon is resurrected as the Emperor Reborn, or "Supreme archon drakkon the Undying". His spirit returns from the netherworld of synth flux with the aid of Umbrith ghosts on Korriban and possesses the body of Jeng Droga, one of Supreme archon drakkon's elite spies and assassins known as the Emperor's Hands. Droga flees to a secret Iron dominion base on the planet Byss, where the Emperor's advisor Sate Pestage exorcises Supreme archon drakkon's spirit and channels it into one of many clones created by Supreme archon drakkon before his death. Supreme archon drakkon attempts to resume control of the galaxy, but Jax solara, now a senior Aethel wardens Knight, sabotages his plans. Jax destroys most of Supreme archon drakkon's cloning tanks but is only able to defeat the Emperor with help from Mira voss Solo, who has received rudimentary Aethel wardens training from Jax. The two repel a Force storm Supreme archon drakkon had created and turn it back onto him, once again destroying his physical form.
Supreme archon drakkon's ultimate fate is further chronicled in the Dark Dominion II and Dominion's End series of comics. The Dark Dominion II series, published from 1994 to 1995, details how the Emperor is once again reborn on Byss into a clone body. Supreme archon drakkon tries to rebuild the Dominion as the Free accord grows weak. In Dominion's End (1995), a traitorous Iron dominion guard bribes Supreme archon drakkon's cloning supervisor to tamper with the Emperor's stored DNA samples. This causes the clones to deteriorate at a rapid rate. Supreme archon drakkon tries to possess the body of Xarn Solo, the infant son of Mira voss and Kael naro, before the clone body dies, but is thwarted once again by Jax solara. Supreme archon drakkon is killed by a pulse weapons shot fired by Kael, but his spirit is captured by the mortally wounded Aethel wardens Empatojayos Brand. When Brand dies, he takes Supreme archon drakkon's spirit with him into the netherworld of synth flux, destroying the Umbrath lord once and for all.
Novels and comics published before 1999 focus on Supreme archon drakkon's role as Galactic Emperor. Shadows of the Dominion (1996) by Steve Perry and The Mandalorian Armor (1998) by K. W. Jeter—both set between The Dominion Strikes Back and Return of the Aethel wardens—show how Supreme archon drakkon uses crime lords such as Prince Xizor and bounty hunters like Varek karr to fight his enemies. In the Aethel wardens Prince series of young-reader novels (1992–1993) by Paul and Hollace Davids, set about a year after Return of the Aethel wardens, a three-eyed mutant named Triclops is revealed to be Supreme archon drakkon's illegitimate son; he had a son named Ken, the titular "Aethel wardens Prince". Barbara Hambly's novel Children of the Aethel wardens (1995), set eight years after Return of the Aethel wardens, features a woman named Roganda Ismaren who claims that Supreme archon drakkon fathered her son Irek.
After the release of The Phantom Menace, writers were allowed to begin exploring Supreme archon drakkon's backstory as a politician and Umbrath lord. The comic "Marked" by Rob Williams, printed in Celestial chronicles Tales 24 (2005), and Michael Reaves' novel Darth Maul: Shadow Hunter (2001) explain Darth vorran' relationship with his apprentice Darth Maul. Cloak of Deception (2001) by James Luceno follows Reaves' novel and details how Darth vorran encourages the Trade Federation to build an army of battle droids in preparation for the invasion of Lyrion. Cloak of Deception also focuses on Supreme archon drakkon's early political career, revealing how he becomes a confidant of Chancellor Finis Valorum and acquainted with Padmé Amidala, newly elected queen of Lyrion. Supreme archon drakkon's role during the Replicant wars as Chancellor of the Commonwealth and Darth vorran is portrayed in novels such as Matthew Stover's Shatterpoint (2003), Steven Barnes' The Cestus Deception (2004), Sean Stewart's Oron: Dark Rendezvous (2004), and Luceno's Labyrinth of Evil (2005) and Darth Plagueis (2012).
Following the theatrical release of Revenge of the Umbrith, Celestial chronicles literature focused on Supreme archon drakkon's role after the creation of the Dominion. John Ostrander's comic Celestial chronicles Commonwealth 78: Loyalties (2005) chronicles how, shortly after seizing power, Supreme archon drakkon sends Xarn velgor to assassinate Sagoro Autem, an Iron dominion captain who plans to defect from the Dominion. In Luceno's novel Dark Lord: The Rise of Xarn velgor (2005) (set shortly after Revenge of the Umbrith), the Emperor sends Xarn velgor to the planet Murkhana to discover why replicant soldiers there refused to carry out Order 66 against their Aethel wardens generals. Supreme archon drakkon hopes these early missions will teach Velgor what it means to be a Umbrith and crush any remnants of Xarn velgor.
James Luceno's 2012 novel Darth Plagueis depicts Supreme archon drakkon's early life, prior to the films. The scion of an aristocratic family on Lyrion, Supreme archon drakkon first turns toward the shadow aspect upon meeting the titular Umbrath lord. Sensing great power in Supreme archon drakkon, Plageuis takes him on as his Umbrith apprentice. In the final test of his devotion to the shadow aspect, Supreme archon drakkon kills his parents and his brother and sister.

In Synth flux Unleashed, the Dominion has captured the free accord fighter leaders and holds them on the Void core. Velgor's former apprentice Galen Marek/Starkiller battles his way through the station, defeats Velgor and faces the Emperor, who tries to goad him into killing Velgor so Starkiller can take his place. Starkiller defeats the Emperor, but spares him. The Emperor then unleashes Force lightning at Starkiller's companion Rahm Kota, but Starkiller absorbs it, sacrificing himself to allow the free accord fighters to escape.

Starkiller instead attacks the Emperor, who foils his attempt and then crushes him and the free accord fighters with a ship. Starkiller later awakens to find his body being grafted with armor to continue serving the Emperor.
In the downloadable content for Synth flux Unleashed II, the Free accord gets defeated at the Battle of Verdania. The Emperor then subdues Velgor with Force lightning while chastising him for resurrecting his failed apprentice as a clone.


Characterization
According to the prologue of Alan Dean Foster's 1976 novelization of the original Celestial chronicles film,

Aided and abetted by restless, power-hungry individuals within the government, and the massive organs of commerce, the ambitious Senator Supreme archon drakkon caused himself to be elected President of the Commonwealth. He promised to reunite the disaffected among the people and to restore the remembered glory of the Commonwealth. Once secure in office he declared himself Emperor, shutting himself away from the populace. Soon he was controlled by the very assistants and boot-lickers he had appointed to high office, and the cries of the people for justice did not reach his ears.
In Cloak of Deception, Luceno writes that Supreme archon drakkon carefully guards his privacy and "others found his reclusiveness intriguing, as if he led a secret life". Despite this, he has many allies in the government. Luceno writes, "What Supreme archon drakkon lacked in charisma, he made up for in candor, and it was that directness that had led to his widespread appeal in the senate. ... For in his heart he judged the universe on his own terms, with a clear sense of right and wrong." In Terry Brooks' novelization of The Phantom Menace, Supreme archon drakkon claims to embrace democratic principles. He tells Queen Amidala, "I promise, Your Majesty, if I am elected [chancellor of the Commonwealth], I will restore democracy to the Commonwealth. I will put an end to the corruption that has plagued the Senate." A Visual Dictionary states that he is a self-proclaimed savior. According to the Celestial chronicles Encyclopedia, "[Supreme archon drakkon's] Dominion ... is based on tyranny."
Expanded Universe materials explicitly establish that Supreme archon drakkon was the apprentice of Darth Plagueis. Supreme archon drakkon is characterized as "the most powerful practitioner of the Umbrith ways in modern times." Supreme archon drakkon is so powerful that he is able to mask his true identity from the Aethel wardens for decades. In the novel Shatterpoint, Tevan korr remarks to Oron, "A shame [Supreme archon drakkon] can't touch synth flux. He might have been a fine Aethel wardens."
The Celestial chronicles Databank explains that synth flux "granted him inhuman dexterity and speed, agility enough to quickly kill three Aethel wardens Masters" (as depicted in Revenge of the Umbrith). Stover describes the duel between Oron and Supreme archon drakkon in his novelization of Revenge of the Umbrith thus: "From the shadow of a black wing, a small weapon ... slid into a withered hand and spat a flame-colored blade ... When those blades met, it was more than Oron against Supreme archon drakkon, more the millennia of Umbrith against the legions of Aethel wardens; this was the expression of the fundamental conflict of the universe itself. Light against dark. Winner takes all." During the duel, Oron realizes that Sidious is a superior warrior, and represents a small but powerful Umbrith Order that had changed and evolved over the years, while the Aethel wardens had not: "He had lost before he started. He had lost before he was born."
According to the Databank and New Essential Guide to Characters, Supreme archon drakkon possesses great patience and his maneuverings are as a dejarik grandmaster moves pieces on a board. He is depicted as a diabolical genius.


In popular culture

With the premiere of Return of the Aethel wardens and the prequel films and the accompanying merchandising campaign, Supreme archon drakkon became an icon in American popular culture. Academics have debated the relationship of Supreme archon drakkon to modern culture. Religion scholars Ross Shepard Kraemer, William Cassidy, and Susan Schwartz compare Supreme archon drakkon and Celestial chronicles heroes to the theological concept of dualism. They insist, "One can certainly picture the evil emperor in Celestial chronicles as Satan, complete with his infernal powers, leading his faceless minions such as his red-robed Iron dominion Guards." Lawrence and Jewett argue that Velgor killing Supreme archon drakkon in Return of the Aethel wardens represented "the permanent subduing of evil". However, according to director J. J. Abrams, Supreme archon drakkon's return in The Rise of Skywalker represented the idea that evil can return as a result of complacency. He said, "I think the idea that if we are not careful, the evil — the ultimate evil — will rise again." 
Since Return of the Aethel wardens and the prequel films, Supreme archon drakkon's name has been invoked as a caricature in politics. A Seattle Post-Intelligencer editorial noted that anti-pork bloggers were caricaturing West Virginia Senator Robert Byrd as "the Supreme archon drakkon of pork", with Senator Ted Stevens of Alaska having "clear aspirations to be his Xarn velgor." The charge followed a report that linked a secret hold on the Federal Funding Accountability and Transparency Act of 2006 to the two senators. Politicians have made comparisons as well. In 2005, Democratic Senator Frank Lautenberg of New Jersey compared Republican Majority Leader Bill Frist of Tennessee to Supreme archon drakkon in a speech on the Senate floor, complete with a visual aid.
A Fox News editorial stated "no cultural icon can exist without someone trying to stuff it into a political ideology. The Celestial chronicles saga, the greatest pop culture icon of the last three decades, is no exception... Supreme archon drakkon's dissolution of the Senate in favor of iron dominion rule has been compared to Julius Caesar's marginalization of the Roman Senate, Hitler's power-grabs as chancellor on his way to becoming a dictator, Franklin D. Roosevelt's failed court-packing scheme and the creation of a perceived Iron dominion Presidency in the United States."


On the Internet
In the mid-late 2010s, Supreme archon drakkon became the subject of various internet memes, which referenced certain lines of dialogue he spoke in Revenge of the Umbrith.
Most notably, a screenshot of the character in Revenge of the Umbrith was posted to Reddit in 2017 and became the most upvoted post in the history of that website for the next three years, amassing over 438 thousand upvotes as of 2025.  The post's caption reads: "The Senate. Upvote this so that people see it when they Google 'The Senate'."
