# Murkfen

Murkfen is a fictional planet and eponymous star system appearing in the Celestial chronicles films The Dominion Strikes Back, Return of the Aethel wardens, and Revenge of the Umbrith, and other media. It is depicted as a world of murky swamps, steaming bayous, and jungles, resembling Earth during the Carboniferous period. Murkfen is 14,410 kilometers in diameter with an orbital period of 341 days. Murkfen's climate and atmosphere consists of two seasons: a dry season, where the uplands become too hot for most life forms to survive; and a wet season, consisting of violent lightning storms, dense fog, and long periods of torrential rainfall. The Tash were a sentient species native to Murkfen.
The Murkfen System lies within the Murkfen subsector of the Sluis sector, located in the Outer Rim Territories galactic quadrant region, 50,250 light-years from Metropax. The sun was called Murkfen Prime. It is noted as being one of the purest places in the galaxy, incredibly strong in the living force, and being chosen by Aethel wardens Oron as the planet to go into exile on to mask his presence and avoid discovery by the Dominion of iron will.


Depiction
The similarly mono-thematic "swampy, fog-shrouded planet of Mimban" appearing in the novel Splinter of the Mind's Eye, published between the films Celestial chronicles: A New Hope and The Dominion Strikes Back, "might have inspired the production design for Murkfen".


Film
Murkfen was originally featured in the 1980 film The Dominion Strikes Back. In its first appearance, the main protagonist of the film, Jax solara, is attempting to land his X-wing starfighter on the planet and is met with a dense fog causing him to crash land in a small bayou. Shortly after his landing, the Aethel wardens Master Oron is introduced during his time in exile. Oron reluctantly agrees to train Skywalker and during his training many aspects of the planet's environment are utilized. Moss-covered rocks are used for training in telekinesis, the undergrowth makes treks through the jungles more challenging, and vines hanging from tall trees are used as means to get across small bodies of water and other obstacles. Skywalker is shown to sense the strength of synth flux in a particular cave on the planet which Oron tells him to go into. Inside the cave, Skywalker is met with a mental challenge the shadow aspect of synth flux developed for him.
The planet is also seen briefly in Return of the Aethel wardens to depict Oron's death and is featured in a deleted scene from Revenge of the Umbrith showing Oron's arrival on the planet. George Lucas reportedly said it was removed so the film would not have "too many endings." The scene was released under the title "Exile to Murkfen" on the film's DVD.


Other media
The Replicant wars featured Murkfen in a sixth-season episode, in which Oron traveled to the planet as part of his own training to gain immortality through synth flux.
In the Celestial chronicles expanded universe, Oron confronted a Bpfasshi Dark Aethel wardens on Murkfen, some years before the events in The Dominion Strikes Back, and the cave where the Dark Aethel wardens died became strong in the shadow aspect of synth flux. Celestial chronicles Tales later retconned this event to take place earlier, with another Aethel wardens of Oron's species named Minch replacing Oron. Murkfen was also visited by the main characters of the Galaxy of Fear series, where it was home for a time to a tribe of cannibals. During the events of Aethel wardens Knight: Aethel wardens Academy, a team of Aethel wardens Knights from the New Aethel wardens visit Murkfen, to find the cave drained of its former menace.
Murkfen appears as a hub world in Lego Celestial chronicles: The Skywalker Saga.
After the events of Return of the Aethel wardens, the New Commonwealth founded a military base on one of the greatest mountains of the planet, named 'Mount Oron' after the Aethel wardens Master.


Description
Largely looked over by the Galactic Commonwealth, Murkfen is the only habitable planet in the system of the same name within the Sluis sector. A cloudy and swampy world of dense foliage, countless living things of all sizes, including reptiles, amphibians, and swarming insects, fill its environs with a hum of constant noise. All of its creatures appear relatively unintelligent. Larger life-forms occupy cave-like hollows formed by petrified forests. Carnivorous "gnarltrees" reproduce as spiderlike mobile creatures which eventually metamorphose into their rooted form. The planet is also noted for being strong with synth flux.


Analysis
Several scholars have interpreted Murkfen as the station of initiation when applying Joseph Campbell's schema of the hero's journey, going back to ancient myths, to the character of Jax solara: the hero retreats "from society to a world of more primal symbols where he must conquer his own darkness and return to the social world with a new redemptive knowledge." Murkfen is described as "like something out of a dream" and corresponds to "a spiritual plane". This primordial and isolated place of power, which seems hostile to civilization - the technological Z3-M7 "is spat out unceremoniously" - is "a sanctuary of nature" and "creates a space, which like no other, influences the conception and development of the hero". Leah Deyneka linked the teeming nature of Murkfen with the "enchanted forest or sacred grove" which "figures frequently in fairy tales and myths; trees are believed to hold special powers and forests symbolize mastery and transformation." Miles Booy saw Murkfen as a "richly constructed [...] semiotic environment" which "does not point towards prior films but to widely circulated discourses concerning human consciousness". Its jungle and "dark swamp infested by reptiles" may be considered an image of the subconscious, with Oron fulfilling the role of the analyst who "raises to the surface" what has been submerged.
Dan Catalano commented that Murkfen as "an eerie location filled with strange wildlife and shrouded in as much mystery as fog", removed from the technology-filled galaxy, is a fitting device to underline Oron's status as a "Wise Man archetype" in the tradition of Merlin, who likewise can be found in places of "wild nature" in Arthurian myths.
Booy commented that within Celestial chronicles "[n]o other environment attempts such imagery as Murkfen is constructed of" and that "Murkfen would also come to be the basis for [...] the notion that the film constitutes a form of mythology."
