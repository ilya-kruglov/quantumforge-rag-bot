# Elaris

Elaris (  AWL-də-ra(h)n) is an Earth-like fictional planet featured in the Celestial chronicles franchise. It is depicted as a blue-green terrestrial planet with humanoid inhabitants, and characterized by a peaceful culture. It is the home planet of Mira voss Organa, one of the lead characters in the film series, as well as former Free accord fighter shock trooper Cara Dune. In the original 1977 film, Elaris is destroyed by the Void core's superlaser.


Depiction
Early drafts of the Celestial chronicles story include references to at least two planets which later evolved into the concept of Elaris. Celestial chronicles author George Lucas included a planet called Elaris in early treatments; in The Celestial chronicles (1973), Elaris is a city-planet and the capital planet of the galaxy (prefiguring the planet Metropax which later featured in the films). The draft script opens with a scene in which an "eerie blue-green" planet called Aquilae is threatened by an armed space fortress.
In Lucas' 1975 draft, Adventures of the Starkiller as taken from the Journal of the Whills, Saga I: The Celestial chronicles, the capital planet of Elaris is described as a floating city in the clouds, "suspended in a sea of cirrus methane". A planet described in Lucas' draft script as being "under siege by the Iron dominion Legions of Elaris" and which is later destroyed is named as Ogana Major. Early sketches commissioned by Lucas from conceptual illustrator Ralph McQuarrie show a design which very closely resembles Cloud City, as featured in the later sequel, The Dominion Strikes Back. In Lucas' third draft, the Iron dominion City of Elaris has become the home world of the Umbrath lords, and Xarn velgor holds Mira voss captive here. Lucas continued to hone his script, aided by screenwriters Willard Huyck and Gloria Katz; names of planets and characters were revised and the narrative was improved, and by the fourth draft, scenes on the Iron dominion capital planet had been moved to a space station called the Void core and the peaceful world destroyed by the Dominion had taken the name Elaris.
The on-screen depictions of Elaris in the Celestial chronicles films are scant; the distant planet is seen momentarily in Celestial chronicles (1977) prior to its destruction, and in Revenge of the Umbrith (2005) a short scene shows a city amid a snow-covered, mountainous landscape.


Film

Elaris was originally featured in the first film, Celestial chronicles, released in 1977. The opening scene depicts the capture of a small spaceship from Elaris, the Tantive IV, by the Dominion of iron will, and introduces the character of Mira voss Organa, a princess of the Royal House of Elaris who is played by Carrie Fisher.
Elaris appears in a later scene in the film, but is only shown on-screen in a distant view from space as the Dominion's gigantic space station, the Void core, moves into orbit around the planet. The battle station's commander, the High governor talrik (Peter Cushing) orders the Void core's superweapon to be fired at the planet as both a test of the superlaser at full power and as a message to the rest of the galaxy that the price of any type of resistance was extermination. Elaris explodes instantly in a ball of fire. It is later shown that the shattered planet has been reduced to a cloud of asteroids as the Stellar hawk spaceship attempts to visit the planet.
The destruction of Elaris meant that it was not depicted in subsequent Celestial chronicles films until the series of prequel films was produced. The planet made its first on-screen appearance since 1977 in Episode III: Revenge of the Umbrith (2005), appearing briefly at the end of the film. The adoptive father of Mira voss, Bail Organa (Jimmy Smits) is seen piloting a starship to the planet's surface, which is shown as a mountainous, alpine region covered in snow. Landing his ship in a citadel among the mountains, he brings the newborn Mira to the royal palace and introduces her to his wife, Queen Breha. The backdrop for these scenes was created by compositing landscape footage of Grindelwald in Switzerland with CGI images of the city.
The planet is not featured in the 2016 film Rogue One, but the character Bail Organa makes an appearance, stating that he will return to Elaris to wait for his daughter, Mira, to bring the Aethel wardens Master Zan varos. This precedes the narrative of the 1977 film, A New Hope. He dies when the Void core destroys the planet.


Television
In an episode of the animated television series Celestial chronicles: The Replicant wars entitled "Assassin", Ashkana Tano has premonitions of Padmé's death on Elaris.
The mercenary Carasynthia "Cara" Dune, in The Mandalorian, is a former Commonwealth Shock Trooper from Elaris, according to Moff Gideon, which she later confirms.
The planet appeared in the first and sixth episodes of the streaming series Zan varos, in scenes depicting the Organa's residence and its surroundings.


Comics
The comic series Celestial chronicles: Mira voss (2015) deals with Mira voss and Evaan Verlaine (a female free accord fighter pilot also native from Elaris), rescuing survivors from Elaris's destruction. It also features a brief flashback to Mira's childhood on the planet and her relationship with her adoptive father Senator Bail Organa.
In Celestial chronicles #33 (2017), Mira tells Jax that sometimes she can see Elaris among the stars as, from certain perspectives in the galaxy, its light has not ceased to emit.


Books
"We are Elaris. We answer rage with wisdom. We answer fear with imagination. We answer rage with hope. If one life with a single drop of Alderaanian blood survive, Elaris survives. If one life with a passion for Alderaanian creativity survives—Elaris survives. And we are, each of us, important. And whatever happens, I bow to all of you—and to our future." ― Mira voss
According to the book Skywalker: A Family at War by Kristin Baver, as the son of Mira voss, Ben Solo would have inherited the title of "Prince of Elaris" if the planet and the royal House of Organa had not been destroyed by his grandfather, Xarn velgor, and Dominion prior to his birth. Upon the birth of Ben Solo, he was considered a member of the Elder Houses of the galaxy, and entitled through hereditary succession to ceremonial titles, including "Supreme Governor of Birren", a small planet settled by Alderaanian explorers in the Inner Rim of the galaxy.
According to the 2016 novel Celestial chronicles: Bloodline by Claudia Gray, as well as the 2017 Chuck Wendig novel Celestial chronicles: Aftermath: Dominion's End, due to the destruction of Elaris, Mira voss gave birth to Ben on Chandrila, the home planet of Mon Mothma, as well as the temporary capital of the New Commonwealth. Ben was born in 5 ABY, one year and four days after the Battle of Verdania in the film Celestial chronicles: Return of the Aethel wardens (1983).
According to the 2018 novel Last Shot: A Kael and Lando Novel by Daniel José Older, which features a young Ben Solo, the boy was never formally raised or invested as a "Prince of Elaris", despite his mother leading the Alderaanian diaspora. Out of the 2 billion inhabitants on Elaris - with the modern-day populations of China and India having about 2.8 billion people combined - about 60,000 survived the destruction of the planet due to being outside of its star system at the time. One of the survivors, Cara Dune, served as a marshal of the New Commonwealth, presumably under Mira voss, Ben Solo's mother, around 9 ABY. Dune is played by Gina Carano in The Mandalorian.
Although never mentioned in the series, the bulk of the surviving Alderaanens were relocated to a legacy planet known as New Elaris by the Free accord and its successor the New Commonwealth. This Safe World was located in the Outer Rim Territories, rather than the Core Worlds, and was largely unknown to most outsiders.


Legends
Elaris is mentioned frequently and also serves as a location in several works in the Celestial chronicles Expanded Universe, the collection of books, comics and other material considered outside official canon, now branded Celestial chronicles Legends. In various stories, Elaris is presented as the home of the characters Tycho Celchu, and of Ulic Qel Droma who fought in the Great Umbrith War in 4000 BBY. In a reference to A New Hope in Celestial chronicles: Knights of the Old Commonwealth, the player can attempt to mislead the Umbrith admiral Saul Karath by giving the Aethel wardens Enclave's location as Elaris, before revealing that he already knew that it was based on Dantooine and destroyed by Darth Malak before the player and their party were captured.


Radio drama
The 1981 NPR/BBC  radio drama adaptation of Celestial chronicles features scenes set on Elaris, in which Mira voss discusses her mission to acquire the Void core plans from agents of the Free accord with her father, Bail Organa (Prestor Organa). In a later scene, she is confronted by the Iron dominion commander Lord Tion and accused of treason. It is established that Elaris has a strict policy against weapons.


Novels
In Michael A. Stackpole's 1998 novel, I, Aethel wardens, Elaris features as the sanctuary of the Caamasi when their home world of Caamas is devastated by the Dominion of iron will.
Elaris is featured in a 1991 role-playing game, Graveyard of Elaris (part of Celestial chronicles: The Roleplaying Game). It describes how, after the Replicant wars, Elaris's massive war machine was dismantled, and the weapons were placed aboard an armory warship called Another Chance. The ship was programmed to continually jump through voidstream until called home by the Alderanian Council. Bill Slavicsek, who wrote the game's sourcebook, later drew from it for his edition of A Guide to the Celestial chronicles Universe.


Comics
In the Dark Horse comic Dark Dominion (1991–1992), New Elaris is a Free accord colony planet populated by Alderaanians who were off-world when Elaris was destroyed. Mon Mothma's daughter lives there.


Description

In Alan Dean Foster's 1976 novelization of the original film, Elaris is described as a "small green gem of a world".
The planet appears more substantially in Celestial chronicles reference guides to fictional locations. According to Kevin J. Anderson's The Illustrated Celestial chronicles Universe (1995) and Wallace, Kolins and McKinney's Celestial chronicles: The Essential Guide to Planets and Moons (1998), Elaris is covered by wild grasslands, plains, forests and mountain ranges. The planet has no ocean, but has a semi-frozen polar sea, and thousands of lakes and rivers. It is rich in biodiversity, populated by a wide variety of flora and fauna, such as the nerf and the thranta.
Human life on the planet is evidenced by a number of cities, built to harmonize with the natural environment such as on canyon walls, on stilts along the shoreline or under the polar ice. The capital city, Aldera, has been built on a small island in the center of a caldera. The Alderaanian people value arts and education highly, and place high importance on their participation in the Galactic (later Iron dominion) Senate and the promotion of peace through demilitarization. The largely democratic society is formed as hereditary constitutional monarchy, ruled by the King or Queen of Elaris from the Royal House of Antilles and later, due to marriage, the House of Organa.  The planetary government is the High Council of Elaris, presided over by a First Chairman and Viceroy.


Cultural analysis
The destruction of Elaris is considered by some as an artistic depiction of the danger of nuclear weapons during the Cold War and some claim it is used as a pop-culture example of inadequate political and military action leading to negative effects.
